- Rules, for someone who knows no software: one line adds a rule, "When Dot wants to …", "It should …" (*Allow on its
  own*, *Ask me first*, which it starts at, or *Leave it to me*) and **Add rule**. Dot reads your words and shows the
  rule back as a sentence ("Dot understood: When Dot wants to run “git status” (with plain arguments, after changing
  folder at most), it goes ahead without asking.") and adds it only when you say **Add it**, or **Change the words**
  takes you back to the box; a draft that does the opposite of the choice you made is not offered, and nothing is
  added. Words that make no rule stay in the box with the reason, and **Fill in the details yourself** opens the parts
  of the rule to fill in. The rules are plain sentences that end in their answer, read from the rule's pattern and
  saying neither less nor more than it fits (a pattern that is more than words is shown as it is), with a switch,
  **Delete**, arrows that move a rule up or down (when the pointer is on its row, always on a phone) and **Details** for
  the tool, the pattern, why and how the rule was used; a rule that is never used says so, in words, and what helps.
  The sandbox switch ("Keep Dot's commands in a safe box") and what is always on are in one fold, **Advanced**, shut
  until it is opened. **Make a rule of this** on an approval card fills the box and the choice (item 622).
