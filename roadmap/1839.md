### 1839. Follow a run live inside its chat

**Status: done (2026-11-25).** The owner: following the agent in real time from the phone matters a lot.

What must hold: while a chat's run drives its browser or the Mac's desktop, the chat shows what it sees, live, with what
it is doing now; the picture follows the owner while they scroll the chat; one tap makes it full screen; nothing is read
while it is folded away, the chat is left or the app is away.

What was built:
- Engine (pure): `runSurface` (the newest `browser_*` or `desktop_*` call: the browser's cast or the Mac's screen),
  `liveAction` (the newest such call without a result: click, type, key, scroll, open, look, wait, with its words and
  how many steps the batch holds), `castFrame` (an event of `GET /api/runs/{id}/screencast`), and
  `DaemonAPI.screencast(run:)`.
- App: `CastFeed` decodes only the newest JPEG of what came at once, off the main thread; `LiveFollowModifier` (one line
  in the chat screen) floats a mini player at the top of the chat (176 pt wide on a phone, the picture's aspect, a live
  dot, the action caption on dark glass, a fold button), which a drag moves to the other side, folds into a Live pill
  (which reads nothing), and opens full screen on a tap; the header has Watch live. Full screen: the picture zoomed and
  panned, the caption ("Typing “Ada”", "Opening 127.0.0.1", "Clicking · 3 steps"; a ref such as `e12` is not shown);
  between steps, the chat capsule's own words (Thinking, Writing, the step that runs). An ended run keeps its last
  frame and says "The task has ended".
- Measured: in the app on the simulator, the first frame of the cast 0.43 to 0.78 s after the view opened, then 10 to 11
  frames a second, the play page's own repaint rate. Over a link proxy (no Network Link Conditioner on this Mac):
  1.5 Mbit/s with 40 ms each way, first frame 0.38 s, 7.4 frames a second; 600 kbit/s with 75 ms, 0.58 s, 2.9 frames a
  second. A frame is a 19 KB JPEG (quality 80, 1280 pixels): Chrome sends the next only once one is read, so a slow
  link lowers the rate, never the delay. No phone-sized cast was needed.
- A real model: `claude_code:haiku` on the owner's Claude plan in a throwaway home (`DOTNEXT_E2E_MODEL`) opened the
  play page, typed and pressed three times; the phone followed it in the mini player (`shots-v1/v1-real-model-follow.mp4`).
- The full screen's takeover (Take over, Hand back, the page keyboard; `RunControl.swift`) is in this commit too, as
  the full screen draws it; what it does, its limits and its end-to-end check are item 1840's record (the next commit),
  and `docs/phone.md` › "Watching from the phone" describes it since item 1838.
- Not done: a click point drawn on the picture. A step names its element by a ref, and the phone does not know where
  that is on the picture; `click_at`'s pixels are in the page's CSS pixels, which the cast does not tell. Drawing a guess
  would be wrong.
