### 682. Enter in a field and a click on a control of no known role are judged by what they press

**Status: done (2026-10-26).** The whole-project review of 2026-10-26 found two whole kinds of browser step that the
policy never judged by what they do. A click was judged by its element's name only when the element's role was a
button, a link, a menu item, a tab, an option, a box or a switch: a shop or a composer that draws its Pay or Send as a
`div` (the snapshot's `generic [cursor=pointer]: Pay now`) was a plain click, and the typed judge was not asked either.
And Enter, Meta+Enter or a line break typed in a text field, which a page takes as the press of its form's button or of
the Send beside a chat's box, was judged by the field's own name alone: a run could fill WhatsApp Web's box and press
Enter, or submit a checkout from its promo code, with no question.

**What must hold, and where; what only asks.** The browser's judgment is by the names it can read, and these two rules
widen what it reads, not what it guesses. A click (and a check) is judged by its element's name whatever its role, an
element of no name of its own by the text it shows inline; a drag still judges the dragged element only when it is a
control, as what is dragged is named for what it is. Enter or a line break in a textbox, a searchbox or a combobox is
judged by the field's name and by the buttons it may press (`controls_near`): those under every ancestor up to the form
the field is in, else up to three levels, while an ancestor holds at most eighty lines, where a composer's Send sits and
a form's Pay sits a level above a harmless button beside the field (the security review of this item found the nearest
level's buttons alone could hide it); a number field submits as a text field does, and Enter on a checkbox or a radio
button of a form too; a
step that pays so counts as a payment for `[policy.spend]`. The typed judge, when it is on, is asked about a click on
any element with a name no pattern knows. Not read, and said in `docs/safety.md`: a field whose surroundings show no
button, a line break typed with no field named, and what a page's script does on a key.

Enter that names no field goes where the batch last put the focus (`keys_go_to`: the field a fill or a click
named), and is judged there; where the batch named none, or a click at a point came between, it sends what is not known
and is judged as a send (the code review of this item: `[fill e12 "hello", press Enter]` passed unjudged).

A Tab (with or without a modifier) moves the focus where Dot cannot tell, so the Enter after it names no field and
asks as a send (the fifth code review).

The focus a key goes to is one class of steps, not one step at a time: the steps that put it on what they name (a
fill, a type, a click, a choice, a check) move it there, a hover and a scroll into view leave it, any other step
leaves it where Dot cannot tell (the sixth code review: a hover after a fill took the Enter to the hovered link).

A text field without a name is not named by the text it shows, which is its value (the seventh code review): a step on
it was judged by what it held, and its summary carried that value to the approval and the log.

**Checked.** `tests/unit/test_browser_submits.py` (a `generic` and an `img` that pay are high with `financial`; the
buttons near a composer's box and in a checkout's form; Enter, Meta+Enter and a typed line break in the box raise
`message_send`, text without a break does not, Enter in the promo code is a payment step, Enter in a search box is
nothing, a text field's value never its name), the browser's unit and smoke tests (1,570), the browser tests that drive
Chrome (96), and `dotx eval check`.
