### 1145. A browser step says whether it was sent and what was checked of it

**Status: done (2026-11-13); no model was played against it, so what it does for a run is not measured yet.** The
owner asked for browser and computer use that depend less on the model: a run should not take "done" for "it worked".
A step's report had one word, `done`, and it meant that the input went out. A click on a button that did nothing, a
field that put its old value back and a form whose answer came late all read the same as one that worked. The call's
`expect` is free text for the typed judge, a model's estimate, and off by default.

**What holds now**, in the session and the driver, with no model call:

- **Done is not verified.** `StepReport` (`core/outcomes.py`, shared with the desktop, item 1146) keeps `outcome` and
  `detail` as they were and adds `dispatched` (`sent`, `not_sent`, `unknown`) and `checks`, each `verified`,
  `contradicted` or `uncertain` with what was checked, what was observed and, when it is not verified, why and what to
  do. A step with no check is rendered `[not checked]`, never as verified.
- **What a step wrote is read back.** `fill`, `type`, `insert`, `select`, `check` and `uncheck` read their field once
  the page took a turn (`driver.run_step`). The text as written, or with the same letters and digits (a phone number
  the page formats), is verified. A field, dropdown or box that is as it was fails the step as sent and not taken, as
  a rich editor that dropped a write already did. A field that changed some other way is uncertain.
- **A secret is never read back and is no oracle.** A field the page marks secret, or that Dot filled a login into,
  answers "secret" inside the page: its value never reaches Python, in any mode, and the step is neither verified nor
  contradicted. Evidence holds lengths only, for every field: a password typed into a plain field is no secret field
  to the page.
- **An expected result is waited for, once.** `expect_text` and `expect_url` on a step are looked for before it goes
  (shown already: the step is not sent) and after it, 10 s at most, 20 s over one batch, less once the page stood
  still for 3 s. Seen is verified; not seen is uncertain, never contradicted, and the batch stops. Such a click is
  never tried again with a script (item 781's retry): its wait can end while the first click is still being answered.
- **A step that acts waits for a look.** After a step whose own check is not verified, steps that only write into a
  field go on, and the batch stops before the next step that acts on what they left.

**What it costs.** Every run's first step carries about 280 tokens more: the two fields of a step, the words of a
result and one line of the prompt's Browser, each cut to a line after a first wording took 580
(`tests/smoke/test_context_budget.py` holds the new ceiling and says why). A step that writes into a field makes three
more round trips to its page, a few milliseconds each. A click that names what it expects and does nothing takes
about 3.5 s to say so, where one that names nothing takes half a second.

**What it does not do.** Verified says that the page showed it, not that a server took it. A change the page makes
after its turn shows in the next look. Text that appears for another reason than the step can still satisfy
`expect_text`; the two cheap cases are refused (words the batch itself writes, words the page shows already). A
download that finished is not a condition yet, and neither is a dialog by itself.

**Design review** (two independent readers of the design, before any code; a red team on the semantics and a
practicality reader on compatibility and the retry). Taken: evidence never carries a field's value (the red team's
first objection: it reversed `describe_step`'s rule that typed text shows as a length); a click that names what it
expects is never retried, and the stale "did not change" advice is not said beside it (both readers: a slow success
became a second submit on any form the flags do not name); a value the page formats counts as verified (both: the
hold-back would otherwise fire on every phone or date field and be switched off); "contains" counts occurrences
before and after (text the field held already proved nothing); an address is matched in its host and path; a batch's
waits have one budget, so they cannot carry it past its tool's time and lose the reports of steps that went out; a box
or a dropdown acts, as its change can send a form; the state set is a set of its own, not part of the weekly catalog.
Changed from the reader's proposal: a condition that already holds is refused before the step is sent, rather than
reported uncertain after it (the red team) or passed as a note (the practicality reader): nothing was sent, so
correcting it repeats nothing. Not taken: renaming the fields to `see_text`/`see_url`, or reading a step-level
`expect` as `expect_text`; the name stays beside the call's `expect`, and a step-level `expect` is refused with what
to write, since a phrase in the model's own words is not text the page shows.

**Checked.** `tests/unit/test_outcomes.py` (the rules and the words), `tests/unit/test_browser_retry.py` (a click
that names what it expects is not clicked again), `tests/smoke/test_browser_verified.py` in a real headless Chrome
against a site that keeps its orders: every claim is compared with what the site took (a read-back form, a field
that puts its value back, a value the page cuts, a secret field, a dead button, an answer 1.5 s late that is sent
once, a result the page shows by a timer, a step that expects what shows already, a page that only looks like an
answer). The existing browser smoke tests pass unchanged; three tests that pin a step's field list and two that build
a `StepReport` were updated for the new fields, and the scaffold test's small window grew by 1,000 tokens for the
larger fixed part, as it has for each item that added to it.
