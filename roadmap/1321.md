### 1321. A chat chosen in the sidebar on a phone takes the place of the chat left, so the edge swipe opens the sidebar

**Status: done (2026-11-22).** The owner's word of 2026-11-22: with the left hand, swiping a chat to the right does not
open the sidebar, it goes to another chat (a screenshot of two chats side by side).

The edge swipe of iOS (a Home Screen app, a Safari tab) goes back through the history on its own, before any script
sees the touch, so `shell.js`'s own swipe (item 1079) never ran. Item 1272 turned WebKit's swipe off in the iPhone app,
and said a page cannot turn it off in Safari. What the page can do is leave nothing to go back to: a chat chosen in the
sidebar pushed the history, and the chat before it was what the swipe showed.

On a touch screen at a phone's width (`(hover: none) and (pointer: coarse)` and `PHONE`) a plain click on any link of
the sidebar (a chat, Settings, an agent) now takes the place of the page left (`location.replace`), told after the
link's own handlers have run, so a click one of them cancelled (the hold of a row, which opens its sheet) goes nowhere.
The other ways a chat opens go through `goTo` (`hop.js`), which does the same on such a screen: a tap on a push
notification (the service worker's `dot:open-route`), a click on a browser notification, the toast's Open for a chat
started on its own, and the home after the open chat was deleted. Links outside the sidebar keep their
place in the history (a page opened by a link goes Back to it), and a window with a pointer, however narrow, keeps the
browser's Back as it was (`hop.js`).

Not covered: a page that opens another with `ctx.navigate` (a page's own button, as after a chat is made) still adds a
place; a chat it leads to is a page opened by a link of a page, whose Back leads to it.

Checked: `tests/js/hop.test.js` (the rule, and `goTo` on a phone and on a Mac), `tests/smoke/test_phone_back.py` (in WebKit as an iPhone: the history is no
longer after a chat chosen in the sidebar), and the chat menu and phone layout smoke tests.

Not verified: the swipe itself on the owner's iPhone, which no test here has; the one thing it depends on is that WebKit
goes back through the history alone.
