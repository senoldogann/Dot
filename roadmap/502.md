### 502. The Harness page in plain words

**Status: done (2026-10-21).** The owner opened Settings › Harness and understood nothing of it: its name, its states
(candidate, probed, adopted, retired), its sections (steps of the loop, experiences, scoring set) and its buttons were
the words of the method, not of the owner. **As built.** The tab and its heading are *Self-improvement*, under *How Dot
works*, whose phone row says *built-in* or *changed*. The page opens by saying what it is: Dot learns from its own
runs how to work better on this Mac; besides its built-in instructions every run follows two short texts, habits and
tool guidance; each night Dot can propose a new version and try it on test tasks first; the owner chooses the version
it follows and never has to write one. A version's state reads *new*, *tried*, *in use* or *set aside* (`STATE_WORDS`),
a night's outcome *done*, *no change*, *skipped* or *failed*, and who made it *written by you*, *proposed by Dot* or
*combined by Dot*; the buttons are **Use**, **Set aside**, **Save for later** and **Save and use**; the sections *What
Dot follows now*, *Write your own (optional)*, *Versions*, *Nightly learning*, *Lessons learned* and *How it learns*
(when, the test tasks, the final check, who chooses, the limits). The Memory page's learning list says *The nightly
self-improvement*. The API, the command line and the config keys keep the harness's own words; a setting is still
named by its key in faint type. Tests: `tests/smoke/test_harness_page.py` and `tests/smoke/test_phone_settings.py`
read the new words. Docs: harness.md.
