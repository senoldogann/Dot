### 1960. Fewer turns on the desktop path: a batch's result is the look, and Safari's pages go to the Safari tools

**Status: done (2026-11-26).** The owner's Apple Developer run (run_mwxgssp4_e1839b) made 53 calls in 322 s: 24
`desktop_act`, mostly of one or two steps, and 22 looks (13 screenshots, 9 snapshots), 12 of the 13 screenshots right
after a `desktop_act` whose result already carried the window after its steps.

What changed:
- `desktop_act`'s result says, on its own line above the view, that the view is the window after the steps (what
  changed, the rest as it was) and the look the run needs, with no `desktop_snapshot` or screenshot unless a step is
  uncertain or failed (`AFTER_THE_STEPS`, `desktop/render.py`); the tool's description and the prompt's Desktop section
  say the same.
- A result of `desktop_open_app`, `desktop_snapshot` or `desktop_act` whose application is Safari opens with a line that
  sends the run to `safari_read` and `safari_act` for the page (item 1959), keeping the desktop tools for Safari's own
  windows.
- The tool eval (item 736) can hold a moment in the Mac's applications: a case marked `desktop` is captured with the
  desktop's tools on, and every case is asked with the tools of the groups its earlier steps used, as the loop turns a
  group on once one of its tools was called (`with_used_groups`). Before, a case whose steps used a group's tools was
  asked without them, so the model could only turn the group on.
- Two cases from the run, told with made-up apps and addresses: `safari-page-by-its-elements` (after
  `desktop_open_app Safari`, the page is opened or read with the Safari tools, not the window) and
  `no-look-after-a-change-the-result-shows` (after a `desktop_act` whose result shows the change, as `render_act_outcome`
  renders it, the run answers instead of looking again).

Measured with `uv run dotx eval tools` on the owner's main model (`opencode_go:deepseek-v4.1-flash`), before on `main`
with only these eval changes copied in, after on this branch, 15 attempts each:

| Case | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `safari-page-by-its-elements` | 0/15 (`desktop_act` on the address bar, a screenshot) | 15/15 (`safari_open`, `safari_read`) |
| `no-look-after-a-change-the-result-shows` | 2/15 (`desktop_snapshot full`) | 8/15 (`finish`) |

A replay of the run's own steps, from its recorded costs: the pages' part was 33 calls (43.0 s of tools and 33 × 3.7 s
of the model, about 166 s of the 322 s). Mapped to the Safari tools (a read, then batches that carry what changed and
check `expect_text`): about 11 calls, the sign-in's two pages in 2, the code and Trust in 2, the service's form in 7
(open, Configure, the add button, the form with Next, Register, Done, one read), about 41 s of the model and 11 calls of
0.2 to 1 s of tools plus the page's own settle. That is a projection from measured costs, not a run: a live run waits on
the owner turning Safari's setting on (item 1959). The steps in Safari's own windows (AutoFill, the Mac's password,
Apple's code prompt; 16 calls) stay with the desktop tools; the second case above is what shortens those.

What was not changed: a press that is "not checked" stays so (the window's change says that something happened, not
that the press did what it was for, as for the browser's reaction, item 1744); a look after an uncertain step stays the
run's to take.
