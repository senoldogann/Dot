### 692. A hostile page or a very long line no longer makes the text readers take quadratic time

**Status: done (2026-10-26).** The whole-project review of 2026-10-26 found readers whose time grew with the square of
their input, one line or one page of a hostile site being enough to hold the daemon (the Python ones) or the page of the
owner (the JavaScript ones) for seconds:

- `web/text.py`: `handle_endtag` asked `tag in self._skip_stack` (a list scan) at every end tag, and `_render` joined
  `parts[start + 1:]` again at every link's close (nested links: all the text after each). Measured on 12-30 thousand
  elements: 10-13 s.
- `browser/snapshot.py` `link_lines`: `lines[index + 1:]` copied the rest of the snapshot for every link, and each link
  scanned its subtree for its `/url`: 10.8 s for 30,000 links.
- `markdown.js`: `/!?\[([^\]\n]+)\]\(.../g` (and `LINK`) read to the end of the line from each `[` of a line of `[a`
  (2.4 s for 100,000 characters); `TABLE_SEPARATOR` had two `\s*` side by side with nothing between (0.8 s for a line
  of 40,000 spaces and an `x`); the heading's `/\s+#+\s*$/` tried each space of a run for a `#` (0.8 s).

**The change.**

- `_Reader._skipped`, a `Counter` of the tags in the skipped stack: an end tag asks it, and pops (and counts down) only
  when it names a skipped element. `_render` keeps the index of the last part with more than white space, so a link has
  words when that index is past its own part.
- `_link_targets` finds the `/url` child of every link in one pass with the parents `_parents` already makes.
- `markdown.js`: `linkMatches(text, withPicture)` replaces the two link patterns: the closing bracket and the end of the
  line are looked up once for all the `[` before them, a failed address run is remembered so that no `](` inside it
  reads it again; `TABLE_SEPARATOR` is written with no two stretches of white space side by side; `withoutClosingMarks`
  trims a heading's closing hashes from its end.

**Checked.** The output is the same as before: the new `linkMatches`, the separator and the heading trim against the old
code on 350,000 random lines made of brackets, parentheses, bars, dashes, hashes, spaces and backticks (`parseInline`,
`parseSourcedBlocks`, `sourceLines`: no difference), and `html_to_text` against the old on 20,000 random pages. Measured
after: 100,000 characters of `[a`, 40,000 spaces after a table head or a heading: 1-10 ms (2.4 s, 0.8 s, 0.8 s before);
the three Python cases 0.2-0.45 s (10-13 s before). `tests/unit/test_web_parsers.py` times the worst cases (a bound of 3
s) and keeps what nested links, skipped elements and `link_lines` give; `tests/js/markdown.test.js` times the three
lines (a bound of 500 ms) and keeps what links, separators and closing marks read. `npm run check`.

**Not done.** `EMPHASIS` and the code-span matching (`segments.indexOf(fence, index + 1)`) of `markdown.js` were not
measured for a line of their own worst case.
