### 1848. DotNext streams a run smoothly: the end is followed after layout and only at the end, a word redraws the open chat alone, the live thinking no longer reflows

**Status: done (2026-11-25).** The owner, on his iPhone with the build of items 1756 and 1759, shot a streaming run
(`owner-refs/dotnext-streaming-glitch.png`): "while streaming, everything crackles and freezes like static, everything
runs into each other, very low quality." In the shot the live thinking's lines sit under the floating capsule and the
glass composer, and the glass refracts words that change under it many times a second.

What was found, on a throwaway daemon whose scripted model streams a run like the owner's (`stream:` in
`ios/Next/scripts/e2e_daemon.py`: four steps that each think aloud for about 4 s and read a file, then a long markdown
answer, about 40 words a second), recorded on the simulator (`shots-d2/before-streaming.mp4`):
- The transcript did not follow the words. It scrolled to an invisible end marker on each change of the last item,
  before the new words were laid out, so the marker's old place was shown and the new lines grew under the capsule,
  the composer and the keyboard; in the recording the whole answer streamed behind the keyboard.
- "Near the end" meant within a quarter of the viewport (200 pt), and the distance ignored the composer's inset: a
  short scroll up to read was pulled back down by the next word.
- The live thinking showed its last three lines cut at the head (`truncationMode(.head)`): every new word re-cut the
  text from its start, so every word on screen slid along its line on every delta; under the glass that reads as
  static.
- Every snapshot of the store (one per delta, about every 20 ms, item 1756) was one observed property of the app's
  model: the chat list beneath the open chat, the tab shell and any open Settings page were drawn again on every word,
  and the row that came into sight wrote a `@State`, drawing the whole chat again while scrolling.

What changed (the app only; the engine's rule `isAtEnd` replaces `followsNewWords`):
- The transcript follows new words only while the owner is at its end (24 pt of slack, the composer's inset counted):
  their own drag or fling away stops it, a scroll back to the end starts it again, a growing transcript never stops
  it. It follows when the content's height changes, after layout, with `ScrollPosition.scrollTo(edge: .bottom)`, so
  the newest line stands above the capsule and the composer.
- The live thinking's last three lines are clipped from the top of the text wrapped from its start, so a new word
  never moves the words before it.
- `AppModel` publishes the store's snapshot by part (`list`, `chats`, `outbox`, `accepted`, `transport`, `online`,
  `savedAt`, `workingAt`), each set only when it changed, at most 30 times a second (the store's stream keeps only the
  newest, so words that came meanwhile are drawn together, never dropped): a word redraws the open chat alone.
- The row in sight is kept in a reference the view does not observe.
- The capsule's clock and a helper's clock count their one-second ticks from the run's or the helper's start instead
  of from `.now`, which began a new schedule at every drawing of the screen. `ChatUITests`' clock test, which failed
  once on GitHub's macOS runner and passed on retry (#1105), reads the capsule's label every 100 ms rather than through
  an `XCTNSPredicateExpectation`, which reads it about once a second and so could see a tick a second late, past its
  2.5 s wait; the waits are unchanged. That failure was not reproduced here, so which of the two caused it is not
  known.
- A debug frame meter (`FrameMeter`, `-DotNextFrameMeter 1`): frames, hitches and the main thread's waits of each run
  of the open chat, one line in `Library/Caches/dotnext-frames.txt`; `scripts/e2e_streaming.sh` runs
  `StreamingLiveUITests` on the throwaway daemon and prints it.

Measured with the debug frame meter (`-DotNextFrameMeter 1`) and Time Profiler on the simulator (iPhone 17 Pro, iOS
27.2, 60 Hz), the app launched on the live chat and the `stream:` message queued from the daemon's side, so no UI test
queried the app meanwhile; about 52 s of streaming per run, the Mac shared with other builds (load 7 to 154):

| | before (4 runs) | after (5 runs) |
|---|---|---|
| frames drawn | 986-1575 (19-30 fps) | 1986-2985 (38-58 fps) |
| hitches (a frame over 1.5 intervals), median | 704 | 119 (29 on the quietest Mac) |
| frames over 16.7 ms, median | 739 | 138 (33) |
| hitch time per second, median | 522 ms | 93 ms (30 ms) |
| main thread blocked per second, median | 411 ms | 95 ms (33 ms) |

Time Profiler, one run each at a like load: the main thread ran 41.8 s before and 14.3 s after. The composer's body
alone took 5.8 s before (built again for every word, its menus' strings looked up each time) and 0.29 s after; the chat
list, the split view and the tab shell under the open chat took about 1 s before and nothing after.

How it was checked: `swift test` in `ios/DotEngine` (`isAtEnd`); `StreamingLiveUITests.testAStreamingRunIsMeasured` on
the throwaway daemon (the long answer streams to its end with the keyboard up); the recordings
`ios-rewrite-2026-11-24/shots-d2/before-streaming.mp4` and `after-streaming.mp4` of the owner's workspace. The UI
test's own numbers are not the measure: XCUITest reads the app's accessibility tree while it waits, which takes the
main thread too.

Not done here (helper C2's): the work line, the thinking block's own scrolling, tighter spacing, the tool names, the
soft fade of new words. A real model's run (DeepSeek, Claude) was not streamed for this measure: the scripted run is
faster than the owner's DeepSeek (14 deltas in 0.6 s, item 1756) and the same every time.
