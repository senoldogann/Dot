### 574. Voice: the composer's microphone, and answers read aloud

**Status: done (2026-10-23).** The owner wants to reach Dot by voice as well as by the web UI's keys. This item adds a
microphone to the composer and a voice that reads answers, both by what the browser itself offers: the speech
recognition and the speech synthesis of Safari and Chrome. Dot sends no voice anywhere and builds no model of its own
for it.

**What must hold.** Nothing is sent by voice alone: the microphone only fills the box, and a message goes when the owner
presses send, as any message does. The browser does the recognizing, and Safari and Chrome may send the voice to Apple's
or Google's servers to do it: said in `docs/safety.md` ("What leaves this Mac") and on the card in Settings › General.

**As built.**
- `static/js/voice.js`: the words and the engines, with no page in them. `createDictation` runs one recognition
  (`continuous`, with interim results, in a language tag): the box after what was heard is `dictated(base, final,
  interim)`, `base` the text the box held when the dictation began, so the words go after it and a result that the
  engine revises is rebuilt, not appended; it stops by itself after six seconds of silence (`SILENCE_MS`) and when the
  page goes out of sight, `stop()` lets the engine settle what it has and `abort()` drops it; the engine's errors are
  said in plain words (a microphone or recognition the browser does not allow, none found, no network, a language it
  does not know), and a silence and the owner's own stop say nothing. `spokenText` turns an answer's Markdown into what
  is said (its code left out, a link as its words, an address as "link", its marks gone, a full stop where a line ends
  without one), `sentenceChunks` cuts it into utterances of at most 220 characters, since some browsers stop a long one
  in the middle, and `readAloud` speaks them one reading at a time.
- `static/js/microphone.js` and `composer.js`: the button (`mic-button`, `aria-pressed`, the accent and a ring while it
  listens, none under reduced motion) stands before the send in the bar of the three composers (the new chat's, a run's
  dock and the agent's page), and is **not drawn where the browser has neither `SpeechRecognition` nor
  `webkitSpeechRecognition`** (Firefox, the desktop app). The words come into the box through its own `input` event, so
  the box grows, the draft is kept and the send wakes as for typing; what the owner types takes over (the microphone
  goes off, what the box holds stays), and so does a message sent (`clear()`), a second press and the composer's end.
  `mirror.js` draws the words the engine may still change quieter (`::highlight(md-interim)`, `setInterim([from, to])`),
  the text under the box being the one that shows. On a desk it stands left of the send; on a phone, a finger's size on
  the line of the choices (`run.css`, `has-mic`), so that the box keeps the line it had and the words of an empty box
  still fit the narrowest phone.
- `static/js/readaloud.js`, `messages.js`, `timeline.js`: a speaker under each answer (`read-aloud`) reads it aloud and
  stops it at a second press, one answer at a time; an answer that comes in under the reader's eyes (the timeline's
  `revealing()`, the same test that has it written in as it arrives) and ended done is read as it appears when Settings
  › General says so; an answer shown again by a chat opened later is not. A browser that cannot speak has no speaker.
- Settings › General › **Voice** (`views/system.js`, `appearance/settings.js`, kept in this browser like the composer's
  keys): the language (the page's own, which is `<html lang>` and then the browser's, or English, Türkçe, Suomi,
  Svenska, Deutsch, Français or Español), the switch **Read answers aloud** (off by default; turned on, the voice says
  so, which is also the touch a browser that speaks only after one asks for), and what the browser lacks and who hears
  the voice.
- `types/interface.d.ts` declares the part of the recognition the page uses (TypeScript's DOM library has none of it);
  `docs/daemon.md` (the composer, the answer's foot) and `docs/safety.md` say it.

**Tests.** `tests/js/voice.test.js` (Node): the language, what is heard as final and interim, the box after it, the
errors' words, an answer as it is said (code, marks, addresses, tables, a line without a stop), the pieces.
`tests/smoke/test_voice.py` (`browser`, Chrome, with objects standing in for the speech recognition and the speech
synthesis that the test drives, wide and on a phone): the microphone shows, listens in the page's language with a
session that goes on, fills the box as words come, the interim ones quieter (the highlight's ranges are read), settles
them, and starts no run; a dictated message is sent by the owner's Enter as any; the box keeps what it held, and typing
takes over; a second press and a silence (the real six seconds) stop it; a refused microphone is said, and "no speech"
is not; the language chosen in Settings is the one it listens in; the Voice card says what the browser does and has; a
browser with neither shows no microphone and no speaker and a disabled switch; the speaker reads an answer (code and
link left out), stops it and reads it again; with the switch on an answer that arrives live (the run held at its first
call and then let go) is read in the chosen language and a chat opened again reads nothing; the microphone stands by the
send on a desk and on the line of the choices on a phone, in the dock of a run too, with no overflow. The tests of the
bar and of an answer's foot (`test_composer.py`, `test_message_foot.py`) count the microphone and the speaker, which
Chrome's speech recognition and speech synthesis give them, and `test_phone_fit.py` is why the phone's microphone is
under the box: at its side the words of an empty box did not fit an iPhone SE's line.

**Questions.**
- *Not tried in a real browser's engine.* The tests stand in for the speech recognition and synthesis: what a real
  Safari or Chrome sends to its servers, and how an app on an iPhone's home screen behaves (iOS may keep the recognition
  from such an app; the page then says the browser refused), are the owner's to try. The code reads only the standard
  events (`result`, `error`, `end`).
- *A network voice sends the text it reads* (the review's N1). Chrome lists "Google …" voices beside macOS's own, and
  speaks with one when it is asked for a language and not for a voice. `voice.js` chooses the voice: one of the language
  with `localService` (the region asked for first, then any of the language), asked for as the page loads
  (`primeVoices`: Chrome lists its voices only after the first request); with none, the utterance has the language only
  and the browser chooses, which may be a network voice. `docs/safety.md` and the Voice card in Settings say so. Not
  built: refusing to read when only a network voice is there.
- *The language of an answer is not detected.* The voice speaks in the language set in Settings (the page's own by
  default): an answer in Turkish on an English page wants Türkçe chosen.

Not built: a wake word, a voice that answers questions asked by voice on its own, dictation into the other boxes of the
page (the queue's editor, the notes' box), the choice of a voice by name.
