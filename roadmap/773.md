### 773. web_extract reads four sources at once

**Status: done (2026-10-28).** **Measured** on the owner's run `run_mvrbj1il_f72dc4`: one `web_extract` of six
Duunitori job lists took 200 s, the sources one after the other, each answer of the fast model (`opencode_go:beam-free`)
16 to 46 s long for 4,500 to 13,000 tokens. The sources do not depend on one another, and each is fetched over plain
HTTP, never in the browser.

- The sources are read and extracted four at a time (`_EXTRACT_AT_ONCE`), and the result tells them in the order they
  were given. The provider's own `max_concurrent` bounds the fast model's calls too. The call's own time (item 554) is
  the same for all: a source begun with less than 5 s left, or not begun when the time runs out, is listed as not
  extracted, and so is one the time cut. A fast model paused after a rate limit (item 240) is not asked by a source that
  begins after the pause, nor by one whose text was being read when it came (checked again before the source is sent,
  after the pre-push review); the ones already sent may have been.
- The six lists of the measure would have taken about two rounds of the longest answers, some 90 s instead of 200 s.

**The change.** `tools/web.py` (`_all_at_once`, `_EXTRACT_AT_ONCE`), docs/runs.md.

**Checked.** `tests/smoke/test_crawl.py`: eight sources four at a time in two rounds, told in their order although the
later ones answered first; a call that runs out of time keeps the one done, lists the four slow ones it was in and the
one it did not reach; a rate limit pauses the fast model and the sources after it say so; the fixture site's
extraction answers each page by its product, whichever is asked first.
