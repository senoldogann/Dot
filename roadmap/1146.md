### 1146. A desktop step says whether it was sent and what was checked of it

**Status: done (2026-11-13); tried on the AppKit fixture, not yet on the owner's applications.** The desktop half of
item 1145, on the same contract (`core/outcomes.py`). `set_value` read its field back; `type` and `paste` returned
once the keys were posted, and every step that went through said `done`.

**Built.**

- `type` and `paste` read back the field the step names, or the one that has the keyboard, for up to a second
  (`actions._written_checks`): keys reach an application after the step returns. The text in the field one more time
  than before is verified; anything else is uncertain and never contradicted, so a field that is slow to take its
  keys is not typed into twice. A secure field is never read; an element whose value is no text (its label is not
  what it holds), a text area of more than 4,000 characters and a text that presses Return or Tab are not checked.
- `set_value` keeps its readback and now reports it: verified, or failed as sent and not kept (`ValueNotKept`).
- `expect_text` on a step: exact words an element of the front window must show in its name or value. The window is
  read by a walk of its own (`session._shows_job`), so the thread's refs and views stay as they are, under the rules
  a look holds (a password manager's window and Dot's own while the vault shows a secret are never read). Shown
  already: the step is not sent. After the step it is looked for every quarter second, 5 s at most and 10 s a batch.
- A failed step says whether it went out: reading what a step touches, its review, and the re-check before it runs
  send nothing (`DesktopStepNotSent`), and so does an accessibility call that says nothing was done.
- The same stop rules as the browser: a step whose expected words were not seen stops the batch; a step that acts
  waits when a check of the steps before it is not verified.

**Left as it was.** The failsafe, the pointer drift check, the re-inspection before a step runs, the refusal at a
secure field and the stop when another application comes forward (item 749). Another window of the same application
taking the keyboard does not stop a batch: there is no measure yet of how often a batch opens a window on purpose
(Cmd-N, then type), and a stop there would cost those batches a round trip.

**Checked.** The fixture window (`tests/fixtures/dot_fixture_app.py`) got four controls: a field that takes digits
alone and puts anything else back, a button with no action, a button whose label changes 1.2 s after the press, and
one that brings Finder to the front. `tests/smoke/test_desktop.py`, with the real pointer and keyboard, compares each
step's report with the events the window logged: typed text verified; refused text uncertain and the press after it
not sent; the dead button uncertain and the batch stopped; the late result verified with one press logged; words
shown already not sent; Finder coming forward and the text that was to follow not typed. All 23 desktop smoke tests
passed on this Mac on 2026-11-13.
