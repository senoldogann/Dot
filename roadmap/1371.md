### 1371. Dot for iPhone works on the worst network: it draws what it holds first, reads together and tries again, keeps what the owner did with no link and sends it once, and says when it is offline

**Status: done as a simulator-tested slice (2026-11-23); what is left, and what is not verified, is listed at the end.**
The owner asked that the app be as good as it can be: a chat must load after the app was sent to the background, or
closed and opened again, even on the worst connection. Read `ios/README.md` ("On a bad link") for how each part works;
what follows is what was decided, what was found and what was measured. No real iPhone and no real cellular network were
used.

**Measured first** (`DotTests/PerfTests`, a `FakeDaemon` behind a `URLProtocol`; the iPhone 17 Pro simulator; the worst
network is 2 to 8 s of latency per request and three requests in ten lost, decided by the request's path and how often
it was asked, so a run is the same twice; `TEST_RUNNER_DOT_PERF_SCALE=1`). A chat of twelve runs and about a thousand
events, eleven others, the disk warm:

| Moment | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| List on the screen, launch with a warm disk (any network) | 0.6 to 0.7 ms | 0.9 to 1.5 ms |
| Chat's rows on the screen, launch or relaunch, any network, or none | 10 to 17 ms | 13 to 20 ms |
| Chat is the Mac's, relaunch after a kill, good network | 24 ms | 19 to 29 ms |
| Chat is the Mac's, foreground after the streams died, good network | 16 ms | 12 to 18 ms |
| Chat is the Mac's, relaunch after a kill, **worst network** | **not in 60 s** | **8.0 s** (one round trip) |
| Chat is the Mac's, foreground after the streams died, **worst network** | **not in 60 s** | **8.0 s** |
| First version of the new read (four round trips in a row), worst network | | 28 s |
| A message typed with no link | "Not sent" until the owner presses Retry | kept, sent by itself, once |

The disk already drew the first frame before this item (the cache of item 1353); what was missing was everything after
it: on a bad link the read that brings the chat up to date failed at the first lost request and was not tried again, and
nothing the owner did with no link survived. The first frame is not slower in any way that matters (a millisecond more
for the larger list file).

**What was decided.**

- **(a) The cache holds what a chat screen needs.** `StoredChat` keeps its newest runs, helpers and last 400 events and
  now the chat's queue, schedules and reactions and the list time it was read up to; `extras.json` keeps the models,
  every provider's models and the schedules; `list.json` the list and the agents. A write goes through a temporary file
  renamed over the old (atomic), is not repeated when the bytes did not change (sorted keys), and makes its folder again
  if it is gone. `schema` is 2 and a build reads layout 1 too (a field it lacks reads as none); a newer layout, a file cut
  short and a foreign file are dropped and read again. The folder is held within 24 MB by letting go the chats read or
  written longest ago (a read touches the file's time); the list and the extras are never let go. The first eight
  chats are decoded off the main actor, several at once, while the screen is made. The session does not hand the screen
  a state it already has.
- **(b) Resume after the background or a kill.** The stream goes on after the last event id (`after` and
  `Last-Event-ID`, item 1352). The catch-up asks for everything it can at once (the chat's runs, its helpers, the
  questions that wait, its newest run's page, and the events after the cursors of the runs it holds, which need no list)
  and draws the events as they arrive; a cursor moves only when its events are in the state, which also closes a hole of
  the old read, where a second page that failed after the first moved the cursor. After a gap of 500 events or more it
  asks for the newest 400 (`?tail=`, item 1370) and a line says that steps are not shown; a daemon that does not know
  `tail` is read by pages as before. A read that fails says "reconnecting", keeps the rows and tries again by itself.
- **(c) The outbox** (`Outbox`, `OutboxModel`, `outbox.json`): a message, an answer to a card, a stop, a removal from the
  queue and a new order of it are written to disk before anything is asked of the network, shown at once, and sent in
  order by one loop when the link allows. A message goes with its key, which the daemon dedupes (`server/sending.py`:
  `send_once`, a key kept a day, a repeat answered with what the first made, 409 while the first is under way, which the
  loop waits out); so an answer lost after the daemon took it, a kill during the send and a link that came back twice
  send it once (the tests drop the answer after the daemon accepted, and kill the outbox between). A message that waited
  more than a day after a first try is the owner's to send again (the daemon forgot its key). An answer to a card that
  closed meanwhile is told: *That question had expired when your answer reached Dot, so it was not used.* (or that it
  was answered already). A message the daemon refuses stays as "Not sent" with its words and does not hold the next; a
  failure that may pass (no link, the daemon down) holds the order. Photos are kept beside the file, uploaded once, and
  let go when the message is sent. Edit takes a message back with its photos.
- **(d) Request policy.** Reads time out in 10 s, writes in 30, a session waits for connectivity up to 45 s, photos get 10
  minutes; a read is tried three times (0.4 s doubling to 3 s, half fixed and half random) inside a 20 s budget, and
  while the link is down it waits for the link instead of spending tries (`LinkMonitor`, on `NWPathMonitor`); an answer
  that will not change (4xx but 408 and 429, an answer that cannot be read, a cancelled task) is never repeated; one list
  read is in flight however often it is asked; leaving a chat cancels its reads; the feeds wait for the link rather than
  trying against nothing. **Not done: HTTP caching.** The daemon sends no `ETag` or `Cache-Control` on its JSON routes
  and adding them means a middleware over every route (and the streams, which must not be buffered), which this item did
  not take on; a read the daemon cannot answer "unchanged" to is the same bytes, and the cache is what makes it cheap
  to the owner.
- **(e) The line.** `ConnectionBanner` (pure: what to say for the link, the daemon and the outbox) and
  `ConnectionBannerView`, a stack over the list and the chat in `RootView`: *Offline — showing saved chats · last
  updated 3 min ago*, *Offline — 2 waiting to send*, *Reconnecting…* (not for a blip: it shows after 1.5 s), or the key
  refused. It has no height while nothing is said and no clock runs. An overlay or a safe-area inset drew it behind the
  navigation bar (found in the simulator); the stack does not. The list's own "Reconnecting to your Mac…" line is gone.
- **(f) Read ahead.** After a list read, the chats that moved on past what is held (a newer run, another status, or a
  newer `updated_at` than the one the copy was read up to) are read, six at most, three when the link is expensive,
  none with Low Data Mode (`NWPath.isConstrained`), one after another, never the chat that is open. A background
  refresh (`BGAppRefreshTask`, `dev.dotagent.phone.refresh`, Info.plist `fetch` and the permitted identifier, no
  entitlement) and `didReceiveRemoteNotification(fetchCompletionHandler:)` read the list and up to three chats within
  20 s. **Neither could be run here**: the simulator does not run background refreshes and the daemon's alerts do not carry
  `content-available`, so the push path is ready and unused.
- **(g) Memory and battery.** Out of sight for 20 s, the lifecycle stream and the open chats' streams are closed, the
  chats write what they hold, the outbox stops, and nothing runs (a test counts the requests over 700 ms: none). Back in
  front the stream is open again and the list is the Mac's within a second (a test asserts it; it is not timed more
  finely). The banner's clock exists only while it is shown. Feeds and reads wait on the link and not on a timer.

**What was found by running it.**

- **The streams of `NativeStore` and `ChatSession` used a session of their own** that no test could reach and no proxy
  configuration applied to; they are made like the requests' session now (`makeStreamSession(like:)`).
- **A banner as a safe-area inset sat behind the navigation bar** in the simulator (its words under the compose button).
- **A working chat keeps XCUITest from becoming idle** (the spinner of the status line): a tap on Send timed out after 65 s;
  the resilience UI test uses a chat whose run ended.
- **A failed read used to be final**: the first lost request of a catch-up ended it with a notice and nothing asked
  again until the next foreground or a lifecycle message, which is why the worst network never got there.
- **`catchUp` ended every read with "ended"**, which replaced the "reconnecting" a failure had just said.
- **The simulator cannot be taken off the Mac's network**: the debug build reads the phone's link from
  `-DotLinkFile <file>` (`up` or `down`) so that a test can flip it; the release build has no such code.
- **The review of the first push found the read for a daemon without `tail` stopping after its first 500 events**: the
  first page asks for 500 and the loop still ended at a page short of 2000. It ends at a page short of what it asked
  for, and the test that passed over it (its run was at work, so the stream filled in what the pages left) now uses a
  run that ended and counts the rows of the gap. The upload session's 60 s of silence did not apply (the request's own
  20 s won), and a background refresh that asked nothing on a dead link told the system it had new data.
- The simulator has no usable speech recognizer, so `testTheMicrophoneWithNoPermissionIsALineWithTheWayToSettings` of
  the slice-2 UI tests fails here with "Dictation is not available now" (it did before; nothing of dictation changed).

**Checked.** `xcodebuild test -scheme Dot` (208 tests, 0 failures: the 127 of slices 1 and 2, and 81 new: `RetryPolicy`
and `Retrier` (growth, jitter bounds, what is repeated, the budget, the link, cancellation), `Coalescer`, `LinkMonitor`,
the banner's words, the read-ahead rules, the cache (a reader never sees half a file while it is written 60 times, a
cut file, an empty and a foreign file, layout 1 and a later layout, the queue kept beside a chat, the size cap with the
least recently read going first, an unchanged chat not written again, a list of a hundred chats read in well under a
frame), the outbox (three messages made with no link, kept, a restart, sent in order once; an answer lost after the daemon
took it; a kill between; a 409 waited out; a daemon that cannot be reached holding the order; a refused message not
holding the next; a message older than a day; photos kept, uploaded once, let go; Edit; an unreadable file kept aside;
an answer given with no link sent when back, one that expired and one answered on the Mac told; a stop and a removal
that find nothing done; an order the daemon refuses), the session (a gap of 1,300 events read as the newest 400 with its
line and no page of 2000; a daemon that does not know `tail`; a small gap; an offline start from the disk that asks
nothing and reads by itself when the link returns; rows on the screen with every answer three seconds away; half the
requests lost; "reconnecting" with the rows kept; a stream that drops going on from the last id; a feed that waits for
the link; leaving cancels the request; an unchanged chat not handed over again), the store (an offline start with the
banner, the banner gone when back, six asks for the list one request, read ahead within the budget and not again,
Low Data Mode and a costly link, streams closed after the grace and back inside a second with no request while away, a
short trip keeping them, a chat open when the app goes away, a message typed offline, an answer given offline, one that
expired) and the seven scenarios of `PerfTests`; `xcodebuild test -scheme DotUI` (eleven of twelve pass; the new
`DotUIResilienceTests`: a chat read before comes back with no link with the line and a message typed there goes once
when the link is back, the daemon's list and queue checked; a chat on a link that takes seconds to answer is there at once)
with `ios/scripts/flaky_proxy.py` between the simulator and a scratch daemon; the screenshots are in the workspace's
`ios-native-shots/resilience/`.

**Not done, in the order they should come:** (1) HTTP caching (`ETag`) on the daemon's list routes; (2) a new chat
started with no link (the sheet sends it once with its key and says so when it fails; it is not kept in the outbox,
because a chat that does not exist has nowhere to be shown); (3) a card answered with no link shows no sign of it on the
card itself, only the line under the screen and the notice; (4) the lock screen's Approve and Deny
(`AppDelegate.answer`) still try five times and tell the owner when they fail, they do not use the outbox; (5) the
daemon's push does not set `content-available`, so the silent refresh does nothing yet; (6) a real iPhone's check of the
background refresh, of Low Data Mode, of `waitsForConnectivity` on a real cell network and of the memory the preload takes;
(7) the 400-event tail and the 24 MB cap are numbers chosen, not measured on a phone.
