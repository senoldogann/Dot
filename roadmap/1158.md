### 1158. A step says what its page sent to its site and what the server answered

**Status: done (2026-11-13).** **Measured** on the owner's Dot in the first two hours of item 1145: 52 `browser_act`
calls of five runs held 79 steps, and none named what it expected (`expect_text`, `expect_url`). Their 56 clicks were
all `not checked`; only what a step wrote into a field was verified (11) or uncertain (2). A check that waits for the
model to name it is, in practice, not made, and a click, the step that sends a form, was the one told least. In the
traces of the state set (item 1147) a model whose click had sent the form went on to read the page's requests, its
console and a script in it to learn whether anything went out.

**Built.** What a step did to the page's own server is observed by Dot, with no word from the model
(`browser/session.py`):

- From before a step until Dot's own look at what it did is over, the requests of its page that are not reads (not
  `GET`, `HEAD` or `OPTIONS`), that a form or a script sent (`document`, `fetch`, `xhr`; a beacon's answer is never
  read by the page) and that go to the page's own site (`same_site`) are heard (`_Sends`), twenty at most.
- After the step Dot waits for the server's answer to each, 5 s at most for a step and 10 s over the steps of one
  batch, and then for the new page an answer may be to be there (`_let_answer_commit`), within the same wait. The
  step's line says `its page sent POST <address> → 200`, the first three, each without its query; `no answer yet`;
  or why the request failed.
- A request that has no answer yet stops the batch: the steps after it would run on a page whose server has not
  answered, and the reason says not to send the step again.
- A press on a form's own button (`_submits_form` in `browser/driver.py`: a `submit` or `image` control of a form
  that is no dialog's) after which no request at all left the page within half a second, on the same address, says
  `its form sent nothing to the site within 0.5 s`. That is what was seen, not a failure: a page that keeps its work
  in itself sends nothing either.
- None of this is a check. An answer of 200 says that the server took a request, not that what the step was for
  happened, so the step stays `not checked` unless something of it was read back or awaited.

`_run_steps` was one method of 150 lines that each rule had been added to; the step is now `_run_one`, which returns
what it came to (`_StepMade`), and the batch's loop holds only what goes from one step to the next.

**What it does not do.** A request to another site (an API under another name) is not named, and neither is a read: a
form sent with `GET` shows in the address. Two names under a suffix such as `co.uk` count as one site, since no list
of public suffixes is read. A request the page sends later than the step's own wait is not the step's.

**Reviewed** before the first push by an independent reader with one question: where is this wrong, slow or unsafe on
real sites. It found the approach sound (an answer is read when its headers come, so a stream answers at once) and
four ways it could still end in a step sent twice, each closed here:

- A step that went out and then failed (a page that left under the click) said only that it failed. It now names
  what its page sent meanwhile, and the batch's stop says to look before sending the step again. A step that was
  not sent says nothing of requests: what a page sends while a step waits for an element is not the step's.
- `its form sent nothing` was said at the page's first change, before a form whose script checks it first (a token it
  asks for) had sent. The form is given the half second a page that did not change is watched for, the line says how
  long Dot looked, a page that holds a socket open is not said to have sent nothing, and a dialog's form is none.
- A request that left just after the page had been watched as unchanged gave a line that said both `no request` and
  `sent POST`, which the scripted second click (item 781) would have clicked again. The unchanged finding is dropped
  once something went out.
- A request the page makes by itself while a step waits (a connection kept open with POSTs) was told as the step's
  and stopped the batch. The page is heard only until the step's own look at it is over, and the line says `its page
  sent`, which is true of both.

A wait of nothing, once a batch had used its 10 s, called an answer that was there not answered: a step waits a
quarter second at least. The pre-push review added three: a press Playwright tried and never made (its element was not
shown in time) is `not sent`, read from Playwright's own log, so that what the page sent by itself in those seconds
is not told as the step's; a press that opened a tab still on its way is not said to have sent nothing; and the
question whether a button sends its form is taken off the press's own time. **Dissent kept:** the reader would rather not make the negative claim at all than make it
after a short look; it is made with how long Dot looked. **Left as they are:** the path of a request may hold a
token of the page's own, as the address of a navigation the batch reports already does; `→ 200` may be read as
success where an API answers 200 with an error in its body, against which the line's `not checked` stands; a page's
own request that leaves within a step's half second is still named on that step's line.
