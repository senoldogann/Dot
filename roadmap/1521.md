### 1521. A phone's reads give up after 10 seconds, try three times, wait for the link instead of spending their tries, and share what is asked twice

**Status: done (2026-11-24); what was checked in a browser, and what was not, is listed at the end.** The web app's reads
had one try with no time limit of its own: a link that hangs left the page waiting for the browser's own timeout, a link
that is gone made every read fail at once and the page say so, and the same list asked by three parts of the page was three
requests. The iPhone app's rules for this are item 1371 (d); this is the same for the page's reads on a phone.

What changed:

- `js/retryrules.js` (pure, `tests/js/retryrules.test.js`): 10 s a try, three tries, a wait of 0.4 s doubling to
  3 s with half of it chance, 20 s in all, which failures are asked again (no answer, 408, 429, 5xx) and which never are
  (any other 4xx, an answer that cannot be read, a cancelled read), what to do after a failed try (`nextStep`: wait, wait
  for the link, or give up), when a gap of events is long (500) and how many the tail reads (400).
- `js/api.js`: on a touch screen with no pointer that hovers, once Dot has answered the page, a read is made by
  `readWithTries` (a try ends at 10 s as a request with no answer does; no try is spent while `navigator.onLine` is false or
  the page is out of view: it waits for `online` and for the view), shared with the same read on its way (callers each
  have a copy of the answer; the read is cancelled when the last caller has left), and cancelled by the caller's
  `AbortSignal`. A list the phone holds a copy of (item 1520) is answered from it at once with no link, and after 3 s
  when Dot is slow, Dot's answer replacing it (`dot:fresh`, which `state.js` answers by reading again what the pages
  show). The proof the daemon gives before a request has the time of a read too, the first one included, and so has a
  read made before Dot has answered the page once: that read is tried once (the three tries begin when Dot has answered),
  so that a link that died as Dot opened leaves the page with its copies, where it first waited for the browser's own
  timeout. A computer's page keeps its one try and its own timeouts.
- `views/run.js`, `history.js`, `queue.js`: the chat's reads end when the chat is left (its run, its record read again, the
  conversation before it, the queue). A chat drawn from a copy that was at work when it was kept asks for the events after
  the last it holds, 500 at most (`afterTheGap`): when they are 500 it reads the newest 400 instead (`readEventsTail`,
  item 1370), says *Some steps are not shown* (English and Turkish), and its stream goes on after the last of them, so
  that no history is read again; a daemon that does not say `X-Dot-Events-Tail` is ignored and the stream replays what was
  missed, as before.

What was found. The brief took the page's live stream for an `EventSource`; `api.js` read it with `fetch` and a stream
reader already, so the stream's rules are item 1522's smaller task. The probe of the gap asks for 500 events of the run
(compressed on the wire) to count them: there is no cheaper count in the daemon, and it is made only for a chat that was at
work when it was kept. The events of the run's helpers inside such a gap are not read (the tail is the run's own, and the
stream then goes on after it): that is what the note says.

Checked: `tests/js/retryrules.test.js`; `tests/smoke/test_phone_reads.py` (a read lost twice is asked three times and the
chat comes; a refusal is asked once; with no link nothing is asked for three seconds, and once the link is back the read
goes once; the same read asked twice is one request and each caller has its own copy; a read whose caller leaves is
dropped; a run that stored 1,400 events while the phone was away is read as its newest 400 (one `tail=400`), says that some
steps are not shown, and its stream goes on after the last of them; a link that carries nothing as Dot opens, a request
the browser holds for ever, gives the screen of a Dot out of reach in a little more than 10 s, where the page waited for the
browser's own timeout: the test fails with the proof and the first read left as they were); `npm run check`.
Not verified: a real link that hangs (the browser tests abort or hold a request; a link that answers in 9 s was not
tried); `navigator.onLine` on a real iPhone, which is known to be optimistic (it says online on a network that carries
nothing, so the tries then run as usual); the 10 s, three tries and 20 s are the iPhone app's numbers, not measured here.
