### 1149. A field the page adds to holds what was written

**Status: done (2026-11-13).** **Found** in the owner's Dot seventeen minutes after item 1145 went live, in the 32
`browser_act` calls its runs had made by then. A run wrote 21 characters into an address field and the field held 29:
the page had put `https://` before them. The fill was called uncertain, the click after it was held back, and the
run did not read the field: it wrote it again with other text, was held back again, and only then sent the click
alone. Two model round trips, and a text changed for no reason. Item 1145's reader had warned that a check which
fires on ordinary forms gets worked around; the phone number a page formats was covered, a prefix was not.

**Built.** `judge_replaced` (`core/outcomes.py`) takes a field for holding the text when its letters and digits begin
or end with those of the text (`_added_to`): verified, with how many characters the field holds and how many were
written. A text of fewer than three letters and digits has to be the field's own ("a" ends "banana"), and a text in
the middle of other text is still uncertain, as is one the page cut or replaced. A field that is as it was fails its
step only when what it holds is not the text: written again into the field that holds it with its scheme, the same
text is held (the pre-push review's nit: the rule first ran after the check for a field that is as it was, so the
retry the owner's run made would have failed its step). The price, kept on purpose and named by a test: a page that
puts back an old value which ends with the new text reads as one that added to it; the check's two lengths are what
tell. The line that says why a batch stopped before a step now says what to do: where
the field's value shows, and that the steps from the held one on go again without the write, in the browser and on
the desktop.

**Checked.** `tests/unit/test_outcomes.py`: a scheme before, a unit after, a country code, one letter, the middle of
a text, a field that is as it was. `tests/smoke/test_browser_verified.py`, in a real headless Chrome: a field that
puts `https://` before what is written is verified, the click after it goes, and the site took the order with the
address as the page made it; the test fails on the code before.
