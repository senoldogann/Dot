### 1204. A repeating schedule the Mac slept through runs its newest missed firing late, within 12 hours

**Status: done (2026-11-16).** A firing more than `[scheduler] misfire_grace_s` (an hour) late was skipped to the
schedule's next time, so a morning brief at 08:00 on a Mac that woke at 09:30 did not run that day; only one-time
schedules and the nightly reflection caught up. OpenMausBot (studied 2026-11-16, Apache-2.0; read, not run) runs the
newest occurrence a routine missed when it is at most 12 hours late, closes the older ones on one counted "missed" row,
and starts the routines it found late 15 seconds apart.

Dot now does the same for a repeating schedule. Of the firings it missed (from its stored next time up to now,
`newest_missed` in `scheduler/timing.py`, a pure function), the newest runs:

- within `misfire_grace_s` of its time, as at its time, as before;
- later, up to the new `[scheduler] catch_up_s` (43,200 s, 12 hours; 0 never), late: its run reads when it was due,
  when it starts and how many firings before it were missed and do not run (`caught_up_goal`), and the title of a
  chat made for it (not one it goes on in, with the same chat), which the schedule's History lists, ends `· late`
  and `, N missed before`;
- later than `catch_up_s`, it is skipped as before, its status counting the firings missed before it.

The older firings it missed are told in one `schedule_missed` notice (the phone, the desktop, the notifications of the
web UI), only when the oldest is past `misfire_grace_s`: within the grace they collapse unsaid as they did, so a
five-minute schedule after a short sleep tells nothing.

`misfire_grace_s` keeps its meaning of "on time" rather than being folded into the window: a firing within it is not
late (no note, no stagger, nothing told), and setting `catch_up_s` to 0 (or below the grace) restores the old skip.
Folding it in would have made every firing a few seconds late after a busy tick carry a late note.

Late starts are `CATCH_UP_GAP_S` (15 s) apart. The wait is the scheduler's memory only: a firing waiting its turn is
still due in the store, so a daemon restarted meanwhile starts it at its first tick. Nothing runs twice, because the
claim that writes the schedule's next time (`claim_schedule_fire`, only while it is still due at the time read) is
committed before the run starts, as for every firing; a crash between them loses that firing rather than repeating it.
A one-time schedule keeps its own rule (overdue, it fires late however late) and its late start takes the same turn.
The emergency stop, a paused agent and a run still going skip a repeating schedule's missed firings as before.

Checked with smoke tests against the real store and scheduler with a scripted manager that starts no model
(`test_a_daily_firing_the_mac_slept_through_runs_late_once`,
`test_the_newest_missed_firing_runs_and_the_older_are_told_as_missed`,
`test_firings_missed_within_the_grace_collapse_into_the_newest_unsaid`,
`test_late_firings_start_apart_and_one_left_waiting_survives_a_restart`), a unit test of `newest_missed` (several
firings, none, and more than one read of 256), and the two tests of a skip moved past the window.

Not done here, from the same study's item 2 (unattended runs that recover by themselves: a schedule's run a restart cut
off resumed once, one that failed on a passing provider error retried once, one that hit the Claude plan's usage limit
started again after its reset): it changes who a resumed run is taken to be (docs/safety.md: a schedule's run resumed
after a restart is taken as the owner's, there to answer) and which runs may run again unasked, which is the owner's
decision.
