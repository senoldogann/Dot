### 1688. DotNext's engine: a store per daemon, one connection supervisor, the outbox moved, the cache, pairing

**Status: done (2026-11-24).** Wave N1-client of the iPhone rewrite (plan §9-§13, R-f §4 #3, R-e §3).

What was built, as the Swift package `ios/DotEngine` (Foundation, Network and Security only, so it builds for iOS and for
the macOS app the owner wants later; `swift test` runs it on the Mac in a tenth of a second):
- The contract of item 1686 as types (`Contract/`), checked against `docs/schemas/thread-sync.schema.json` by decoding
  the smallest and the fullest instance the schema allows of every shape the engine reads.
- Pure reducers (`Sync/`): a chat keeps each run's events once per id in id order (a late lower id is taken, never a
  `seq <= cursor` drop), its per-run cursors, the words being written only on the open step, and its freshness
  `empty / cached / syncing / live / deleted`, `live` only once a view read on the stream's present connection and the
  stream's `synchronized` agree. A break, a `resync`, a revision other than the view's, an unreadable frame: one view
  read (one at a time, a second owed), read beside the stream's opening, not after. Unknown kinds move the cursor and
  draw nothing; a deleted chat closes its stream and is said to be deleted, not unreachable; a rewound run leaves.
  The list keeps its rows by `thread_changed` and does not bring back a chat told deleted.
- One `Supervisor` per daemon owns every stream and the outbox's pace: jittered waits from 0.5 s to 15 s, the count
  started again only after 30 s up; a stream silent 12 s is replaced; back in front after 10 s or more every stream is
  opened again from its cursors, sooner only a stream whose last byte is older than 6 s; the link's loss lets the streams
  go (the picture stays, as not live), its return cuts every wait, a change of interface opens every stream again; a
  refused key and a daemon from before item 1686 stop trying and say so. Transport health and data freshness are told
  apart.
- The outbox MOVED from the old app with its rules (24 h key, 409 "being sent" waited out versus `RunConflict`
  refused by body, photos uploaded once, the say-the-same-twice doings, "already decided" as an outcome, a refusal kept
  with its reason for the composer), plus the kind fixed when an item is made: a steer refused because its run ended
  is never sent again as a queued message under its key; `sendAsMessage` makes a new item with a new key. A new chat
  goes only while the list is live. A secret a card asks for is sent at once and kept nowhere on the phone.
- The delivery contract of §13 as a pure function: offline, sending, queued N (starts after this run), held, sent to
  the running task, "Dot reads it after this step" while a tool of the run is open (the `steer` event is the
  harness's word that it was handed over), rejected; optimistic rows are an overlay keyed by the client id, dropped
  once the chat shows the message in its own place.
- The cache under `v2/` of DotNext's own app group: projection, cursors, daemon version, schema and savedAt together;
  shown under 12 h, its cursors resumed only from the same daemon version; a run at work is saved as "was working at".
- Pairing with the typed 8-character code into DotNext's own Keychain service; a daemon that answers the list view 404
  is "Update Dot on your Mac".
- The transcript as a pure filter: the four tool-call modes and the "Response streaming" switch (§20).

How it was checked: 55 tests (`swift test` in `ios/DotEngine`, and in the DotNext scheme on the simulator): the
reducers, the schema, the moved outbox and SSE parser tests, and a FakeDaemon v2 that speaks the new contract with a
test for each R-e gap (#1 delivered-not-read, #3 a break with the queue changed meanwhile, #4 resync, #5 back from the
background, #10 rename and delete) and for the supervisor's numbers, on a clock the tests move by hand (no sleeps).
Each new test was made to fail once: 47 mutations of the engine, each one red.
