### 2054. DotNext: the AI services and their keys on the phone, write-only

**Status: built, waits for the owner's look (touches `routes_keys.py`, under REVIEW.md "Always needs a human").** Backlog
item K1: the owner asked on 2026-11-26 08:55 to manage the AI providers and their API keys from the phone: see each
provider's state (key set, missing, failing, last check), add or replace a key, remove one, run its check.

What must hold (written before the code):

- **A key goes to the daemon and never comes back.** `GET /api/keys` answers the same view to the phone as to the web:
  the provider, the variable's name, where the key in use comes from, whether Dot's `.env` holds one, and its last four
  characters, which the web already shows. No route answers a key, whole or in part, beyond that tail. The app holds a
  typed key only in the field's memory until it is sent, then empties the field; it is never put in the app's cache, its
  outbox (a key is sent at once or not at all, never queued), its logs or its performance reports.
- **Only the owner's devices.** The routes answer the installation's token or a listed phone's key, as every API route
  does (`server/guard.py`, unchanged). A key is not a way into Dot, so these routes stay open to a phone: the owner asked
  for it. Removing a phone stays the Mac's alone and ends its access at once.
- **No key in a log, an event or a notification.** The daemon logs `key_saved` / `key_removed` with the provider, the
  variable's name and, for a phone, its id; a refused body is answered without the value (`invalid_request` in
  `server/app.py` drops `input`). The tell below names the provider and the phone, nothing of the key.
- **Limits on a phone, none new on the Mac.** A phone may save or remove keys at most 5 times in 10 minutes, and ask a
  provider's check at most once in 10 seconds for each key (Ollama's check is one hosted search, TypeSafe's one small
  judgment); past them it is answered 429 with the seconds to wait. The web, on the installation's token, keeps every
  limit it had: none.
- **A stolen phone, and its limit.** A taken, paired phone could put a key of the thief's own account in place of the
  owner's, so that what Dot sends that provider (prompts, files read into them) goes through the thief's account, whose
  console may keep a log of it; or remove a key, which stops work on that provider. Every save and removal a phone makes
  is told to the owner at once (`tell_owner`, kind `system`: the desktop notification, the push, the Messages channel),
  naming the provider and the phone, and opening Settings › AI services; the owner removes the phone on the Mac and puts
  their own key back. The phone cannot change where a provider's requests go (no base URL is set from these routes).
  This adds little to what a paired phone already holds: it starts tasks with the owner's rights, in yolo if the owner
  chose it. Custom providers (#1179, item 1949) are not on main yet; when they land, a phone must not set or change a
  provider's address, only its key.
- **The last check.** The daemon keeps the last check of each key in memory (ok, the provider's words, when), forgets it
  when the key changes or the daemon restarts, and answers it in the key's view (`check`), so the phone's list shows a
  failing key without asking every provider. The words are the provider's, as the web shows them today (OpenAI's
  refusal names a key by its own mask, first three and last four characters).
