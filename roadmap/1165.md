### 1165. A long chat's earlier turns far from what is seen are not laid out again

**Status: done (2026-11-13).** **Measured** on the owner's Dot, read-only, a chat of 40 turns on an iPhone-sized page
at four times slower CPU (Chromium): opening it forced 190 layouts of the whole page, 1.4 to 1.6 s of the 3.5 s it took,
and one layout of the open chat cost 15 to 40 ms in WebKit on the Mac. Every change of the page lays it out again: each
event of a live run, each scroll to the foot, each line of the box.

**Built.** `.history > .turn` keeps the size it was last laid out at (`contain-intrinsic-size: auto 600px`), and once
the history was laid out once and a frame passed (`skipWhenSized` in `history.js`, a `ResizeObserver` and a frame),
`data-sized` lets the browser skip the turns far from what is seen (`content-visibility: auto`, `app.css`). Skipping
from the first layout on would have given each turn a guessed height: the conversation's height, the place of each
message and the marks at the left would have been off until each turn was seen.

Two things a skipped turn no longer gave, each found by the test before the change was done: Chromium gives no
`innerText` of it, so the marks at the left (`outline.js`) showed an empty preview for a message far up; and WebKit does
not scroll to an element in it, so a mark's click did not go there. `laidOut(node, act)` (`history.js`) lays out only
that turn while the words are read or the scroll is made. The focus goes into a skipped turn and is scrolled to by both
browsers, Tab too in Chromium (WebKit tabs to no button by default); a safety net for it was written, measured as not
needed, and taken out.

**Measured, before and after,** on the test's chat (30 turns, tall answers, no events, so nothing in it is drawn as
an error), 390 x 844, Chromium at four times slower CPU, the median of five, three rounds taken in turn on a loaded Mac
(load 7 on 10 cores): one layout of the open chat 10.0 to 10.7 ms before, 1.0 to 1.9 ms after; twenty lines added at
the foot, each scrolled to and given a frame, as a live run's events are, 662 to 896 ms before and 322 to 333 ms after,
against a floor of 20 frames (about 330 ms). Opening the chat: 0.49 to 0.61 s before, 0.42 to 0.76 s after, no
difference beyond the noise. On the owner's chat, three openings each with the first version of this item: 0.87 to
1.23 s before and 0.89 to 1.00 s after, the layout forced while opening 500 to 770 ms before and 560 to 650 ms after;
that is no gain either. The opening's layout is the first one, which this keeps whole on purpose; skipping from the
first layout on would save 0.1 to 0.25 s of it (measured the same way) and give the guessed heights above. Most of
that layout is not the earlier turns: the next item is to find what it is.

**A turn filled while it was skipped, the pre-push review's first finding.** A turn's work is read and drawn 800 px
before it is seen (`whenNear`, item 708), nearer than the browser lays a skipped turn out again, so it grew while
skipped: its height stayed the last one kept, and it grew later, above the reader. Measured with a chat whose turns
fill in as the reader scrolls up, in WebKit (26.5, the iPhone's and Dot.app's engine), the message read moved down 4
to 20 px a step; on `main` it stayed, in both browsers. Two changes close it: a turn is laid out
(`content-visibility: visible` on it) while its work is read and drawn, and may be skipped again two frames after
(`skippableAgain`), once a frame kept its new height; and `keepPlace` measures how far what follows the growth moved in
every browser and scrolls by that much: WebKit says it anchors scrolling and, with earlier turns skipped, did not keep
the place; a browser that did moved nothing, and nothing is scrolled. `laidOut` leaves a turn being filled as it is.
The second pre-push review found the first version of this kept every filled turn laid out for good, so a reader who
had scrolled up and come back lost what this item gains; the test now checks that, back at the foot, no turn is held
laid out and each stands where it did. Measured on that test's chat (24 turns, each with its work), read to its top
and back, the same way as above: one layout 1.7 to 1.8 ms before, 0.9 to 1.0 ms after.

An earlier version of this entry said WebKit moved the message 24 to 284 px on `main` too. That was wrong: the test's
events lacked fields the page reads, every turn drew "What this task did could not be shown", and the test measured
that line. The test now checks that the turns draw their work (what the run said, `Ran 1 command`) and that no turn
says it could not be shown, before it measures anything; a fixture with the old events fails there.

**What it does not do.** The page does not open faster; what is seen does not change. A turn far up whose size changed
while it was skipped by anything but its own work (a window turned from upright to wide) keeps its last height until
the browser lays it out again, nearer.

**Checked.** `tests/smoke/test_history_skips_offscreen.py`, in Chromium and WebKit, on a phone and on a wide window: the
far turns are skipped; with every turn laid out again the conversation is exactly as tall and each turn where it was;
it opens at its foot; a link of the oldest turn takes the focus from the foot and is in the window, as with every turn
laid out; on a wide window the mark of the oldest message shows its words and goes to it as with every turn laid out;
and, in both browsers, scrolling up a chat whose turns fill in, the message read stays within 2 px of where it was
each step. It failed on `main` (no `data-sized`); the preview failed without `laidOut` in Chromium, the jump in WebKit,
and the scroll in WebKit (4 to 20 px) before a turn was laid out before it is filled and `keepPlace` measured in every
browser. `tests/smoke/test_outline.py`, `tests/smoke/test_thread_turns.py` (item 708's place kept),
`tests/smoke/test_chat_follows.py` and `tests/smoke/test_phone_return.py` pass.
