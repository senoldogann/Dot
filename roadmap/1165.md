### 1165. A long chat's earlier turns far from what is seen are not laid out again

**Status: done (2026-11-13).** **Measured** on the owner's Dot, read-only, a chat of 40 turns on an iPhone-sized page
at four times slower CPU (Chromium): opening it forced 190 layouts of the whole page, 1.4 to 1.6 s of the 3.5 s it took,
and one layout of the open chat cost 15 to 40 ms in WebKit on the Mac. Every change of the page lays it out again: each
event of a live run, each scroll to the foot, each line of the box.

**Built.** `.history > .turn` keeps the size it was last laid out at (`contain-intrinsic-size: auto 600px`), and once
the history was laid out once and a frame passed (`skipWhenSized` in `history.js`, a `ResizeObserver` and a frame),
`data-sized` lets the browser skip the turns far from what is seen (`content-visibility: auto`, `app.css`). Skipping
from the first layout on would have given each turn a guessed height: the conversation's height, the place of each
message and the marks at the left would have been off until each turn was seen.

Two things a skipped turn no longer gave, each found by the test before the change was done: Chromium gives no
`innerText` of it, so the marks at the left (`outline.js`) showed an empty preview for a message far up; and WebKit does
not scroll to an element in it, so a mark's click did not go there. `laidOut(node, act)` (`history.js`) lays out only
that turn while the words are read or the scroll is made. The focus goes into a skipped turn and is scrolled to by both
browsers, Tab too in Chromium (WebKit tabs to no button by default); a safety net for it was written, measured as not
needed, and taken out.

**Measured, before and after,** on the test's chat (30 turns, tall answers), 390 x 844, Chromium at four times slower
CPU, the median of five: one layout of the open chat 7.9 ms before, 0.9 ms after; twenty lines added at the foot, each
scrolled to and given a frame, as a live run's events are, 645 ms before and 326 ms after, against a floor of 20 frames
(about 330 ms). Opening the chat: 0.39 s before and after. On the owner's chat, three openings each: 0.87 to 1.23 s
before and 0.89 to 1.00 s after, the layout forced while opening 500 to 770 ms before and 560 to 650 ms after; that is
no gain. The opening's layout is the first one, which this keeps whole on purpose; skipping from the first layout on
would save 0.1 to 0.25 s of it (measured the same way) and give the guessed heights above. Most of that layout is not
the earlier turns: the next item is to find what it is.

**What it does not do.** The page does not open faster; what is seen does not change. A turn far up that changes while
it is skipped (its work filled in 800 px before it is seen, item 708; a window turned from upright to wide) keeps its
last height until the browser lays it out, nearer, and then grows above the reader: scroll anchoring is left to keep
their place, as `keepPlace` (item 708) leaves it wherever `CSS.supports("overflow-anchor", "auto")`, and a browser
without it skips no turn, since `keepPlace` would see no growth to make up for (the pre-push review).

**Found on the way, not fixed here.** In WebKit (26.5, the iPhone's and Dot.app's engine), scrolling up a chat whose
turns fill in as they come near moves the message read down by 24 to 284 px a step: WebKit says it anchors scrolling
and does not keep the place here. It is so on `main`, by the same pixels as with this item (the test below, run on
both), so it is item 708's and the next item's, not this one's.

**Checked.** `tests/smoke/test_history_skips_offscreen.py`, in Chromium and WebKit, on a phone and on a wide window: the
far turns are skipped; with every turn laid out again the conversation is exactly as tall and each turn where it was;
it opens at its foot; a link of the oldest turn takes the focus from the foot and is in the window, as with every turn
laid out; on a wide window the mark of the oldest message shows its words and goes to it as with every turn laid out;
and in Chromium, scrolling up a chat whose turns fill in, the message read stays where it is. It failed on `main` (no
`data-sized`); the preview failed without `laidOut` in Chromium and the jump in WebKit.
`tests/smoke/test_outline.py` and `tests/smoke/test_thread_turns.py` pass.
