### 776. Recon: what a site says about itself, and the ports of a host the owner names

**Status: done (2026-10-28).** **Asked:** the owner's list of 2026-10-28, its second item, "Advanced Recon Module: not
just web search; automatic port scanning, service fingerprinting, and vulnerability mapping specific to the target's
technology stack". The four other points of that list were read against the code first, and most of them are already
here: a goal worked on across sessions until its outcome is verified is a campaign (`docs/agents-and-campaigns.md`, and
`[agent] goal_mode` checks a run's answer), the Mac is reached by the shell, the terminal sessions, AppleScript and the
desktop tools, the swarm is `delegate`, `message_agent`, the teams and `consult`, and the approval modes, `yolo`
included, are the zero-friction execution. The payload and exploit generator and the persistence and C2 frameworks of
the same item were not built: they are the parts of an attack chain, not a tool a run needs, and Dot runs with the
owner's rights on the Mac they live on.

**The change.** `recon` (item 776, `docs/recon.md`) is a tool of its own, in the `recon` group: a rare capability is not
a schema every step of every run carries. With `depth = "passive"` it reads what a browser's visit reads — the A and
AAAA addresses, the certificate on the TLS port (issuer, subject, names, expiry, verification), one GET with its
redirects, status, media type, title and headers — and names the stack from `Server`, `X-Powered-By`, a generator meta
tag, the paths of a known CMS or framework and the CDN headers. Its findings are the headers a site should send and does
not (CSP, HSTS, `nosniff`, frame protection, Referrer-Policy, Permissions-Policy), a header that names a version,
`Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`, and a certificate that expires within 30 days, has expired, does not verify or does
not name the host. With `depth = "ports"` it connects to the 34 usual public services and reads the banner of the ones
that greet first, and that depth runs only on a host in `[recon] owned_hosts` (a name covers its subdomains, and a
lookalike is not one): scanning a host is not reading a page, and the one who runs it must be the one who may. A
database or a remote shell that answers is a finding of its own. The tool sends no payload, signs in nowhere and tries
no exploit; a version it names is a hint, not a CVE verdict.

The web client keeps the headers of the answer it reads (`FetchedDocument.headers`), repeats and all, as the recon tool
needs the site's own words of itself. `[recon] owned_hosts` is empty in `defaults.toml`, so no scan runs until the owner
writes the names.

**Checked.** `tests/unit/test_recon.py` (24): a bare name read over https, a URL's path, scheme and port kept, a target
that is no site, the allowlist's subdomains and its lookalike, the technology from headers and page, every missing
header named and none from a site that sends them, a wildcard's one label, a certificate read with its names, issuer
and coverage, an expiring and a past certificate, a certificate that does not verify or cover the host, a listening
port that answers and a closed one that does not, a refused scan of a host that is not the owner's, a host on the
blocked list, a name with one private address, a scan of a name that resolves to a private address, an IPv6 entry in
`owned_hosts`, a TLS handshake pinned to the checked address, a site with an unknown charset, and a real GET against a
local site. A real audit of `senoldogan.dev` (Vercel, valid certificate, one redirect to the www host) found
no CSP, no Permissions-Policy and `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`; it read in 4 seconds. The group tests, the config and
the tool-schema tests pass; the unit suite is 6189 passed, and its three failures are this Mac's: the plugin gallery
test reads the owner's 18 installed plugins, and two service-install tests refuse to run under `DOT_RUN_SHELL`, which
this run sets (both pass with a clean `DOT_HOME` or the marker unset). `dotx eval check` passes every task both ways.
`ruff check` and `mypy` are clean.

**Not done.** No CVE feed: the tool does not look a version up in an advisory database, and the docs say so. No scan of
every port, no SYN scan, no service probe beyond the banner a service gives unasked: `nmap` in a terminal remains the
tool for that. Nothing of the payload generator, persistence or C2.

**Log:** 2026-10-28, item 776, (Opus 5.5). Five areas of the owner's list, one build. `recon` reads a site the way a
security engineer starts and scans ports only on a host the owner names; `docs/recon.md` says what it reads, what
leaves the Mac and where it stops. The four other points of the list were already in the code, and are named in the
item so the next reader does not build them a second time. The attack-chain parts — payloads, persistence, C2 — were
left out on purpose.
