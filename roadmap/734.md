### 734. A chat at work opened again shows its helpers as the store has them

**Status: done (2026-10-27).** The owner, with two screenshots of the same chat on the iPhone four minutes apart: before
the app was closed its three helpers stood at steps 304, 595 and 333; opened again, at 136, 169 and 103, all three at
work, though the first had ended. The store had them at 309 (done at 12:34), 666 and 396.

**Why.** A run that is over was read whole and its record put over the events (items 285, 454, 646). A run at work was
drawn from its stream as it came: the stored events of the run and of the runs it delegated to, 7,235 events and 4.7 MB
for that chat (the run's own were 26), then the live ones, and its record was put over the rows only when a helper
started or ended afterwards. On the phone the replay took minutes, and the rows showed wherever it had got to: steps
behind, an ended helper at work, the head "3 working".

**The change.** `GET /runs/{id}/stream?stored=true` ends after the events stored when it was asked for
(`stream.follow_run`'s `stored_only`). The page reads them whole for a run at work as for one that is over, draws them,
puts the run's record over them (`drawRecord`, which settles the helpers' rows) and follows the stream on from the last
event read. A helper's row whose record says it ended keeps how it ended: an event of it paints neither its line nor
its state (`timeline.atWork`).

**Checked.** `test_helper_group.py`: a chat at work whose helper's record counts 40 steps where its events say 1, opened
in WebKit at a phone's width, shows "step 40" and "1 working · 3 done" (the code before showed "step 1 Command"), and
follows the stream on to "3 done · 1 stopped". `test_server.py`: the stream of a run at work asked for what is stored
ends after it, and without `stored` it waits. The helpers' and chats' browser tests (104).

**Not done.** That chat opened in WebKit at a phone's size in 1.1 to 1.6 s on the Mac and held 75,340 elements: the
steps of every helper are drawn into its closed row when the chat opens. Drawing them when the row is first opened is
the next step. The pieces of a message being written that come between the read and the stream that follows on are not
shown (they have no id); the step's message, stored, takes the bubble's place when it comes, as it did for a chat
opened in the middle of one.
