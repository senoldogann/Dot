### 1391. Dot for iPhone: a socket that is dead and does not say so is dropped and replaced

**Status: done (2026-11-24).** From the research of 2026-11-23 on how T3 Code's phone reconnects (`supervisor.ts`: a quick
probe after a short stay in the background, a longer one after a long stay, a stream that is silent on a route that answers
a fresh request is dead and replaced) and the owner's word that the phone must work on the worst network. Dot's feed
reconnected at once when the app came to the front and when the network changed, but a connection the system still
believed in (a path that died while the phone slept, a Wi-Fi to cellular hand-over) waited for the system's own 45 s.

- `EventFeed` takes `connectTimeout` (8 s) and `idleTimeout` (35 s). A request whose headers have not come in 8 s is
  dropped; an open stream that has had no line for 35 s (the daemon's keepalive is every 15 s, so two missed and a little
  more) is dropped; either way the feed goes on from the last event it read, on a new connection, after the usual first
  wait (half a second). A kick still reconnects at once.
- No probe: T3 needs one because its stream may say nothing; Dot's stream writes `: open` at once and a keepalive every
  15 s, so a stream's silence is itself the probe.

Checked: `SilentSocketTests` (a stream that goes silent after an event is replaced and goes on after that event; a
connection that never answers is replaced; keepalives every 0.25 s keep a stream for two seconds under an idle timeout of
0.6 s; a stream silent from the start is replaced; a kick does not wait for any timeout).

**Not verified:** on a real phone on a real cellular network; the numbers (8 s, 35 s) are chosen from the daemon's
15 s keepalive and T3's 2.5 s to 15 s, not measured on a bad link.
