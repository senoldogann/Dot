### 818. The trend engine: a rule tried over years trades instead of a model, and a run supervises it

**Status: done (2026-10-29).** **Asked** by the owner ("Trading'de gerçekten para kazandıracak bir otonomi ve bot
istiyorum; bunun için ne gerekiyorsa yap; internette araştır, makaleleri oku; X'te, YouTube'da, Reddit'te araştır"),
who chose, of three ways put to them with what was measured, "a rule engine and a supervising model".

What was found before any code:

- **What the model shift cost and did.** The old store's 37 runs of the hourly paper shift (2026-10-28) read 510,000
  tokens each on average (`deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017`, 0.30 USD a million at OpenRouter): about 0.16 USD a run, some
  115 USD a month, for a paper account of 1,000. Its three trades (ZEC, UNI, BCH, 25 USDC each, chasing hourly
  breakouts) were all stopped out the same day.
- **What the evidence says.** Live contests of language models trading lose more often than not (Nof1's Alpha Arena,
  season 1, 2025: four of six models lost, GPT-5 about 60%; the organisers call it no ranking), and the studies find
  buy-and-hold hard to beat (StockBench; LiveTradeBench: general reasoning does not carry over; Agent Market Arena:
  short windows, unstable ranks). What holds up best in crypto once costs are counted is time-series momentum traded
  seldom (Zarattini, Pagani and Barbon 2025; Man Group "In crypto we trend"; Monash's study; and the warnings: costs
  erase the short lookbacks, cross-sectional momentum is weak). Reddit's r/algotrading says the same in its words:
  overfitting and unrealistic execution kill most strategies; freeze the rules, walk forward, model the costs, paper
  trade, kill switches. The bots that claim wins there are martingales that "cannot realize a loss", or a week's
  results. YouTube's subtitles and X's search could not be read (a rate limit, a sign-in): their titles only.
- **Coinbase's fees.** Advanced Trade's entry tier was 0.50% maker and 0.90% taker after the September 2026 change, by
  the guides (the logged-in fee page says the owner's own); a round trip of market orders costs about 1.2-1.8%.
- **The rule, tried on Coinbase's own closes before it was built** (BTC, ETH and SOL in USD, July 2019 to October 2026,
  written down before it was run, no parameter chosen for its result): at 0.7% a side, a band of 5 points, 22.1% a year
  (Sharpe 0.80, deepest fall -45%) against holding Bitcoin's 32.0% (0.77, -77%); a band of 10 points 26.5% (0.90, -38%)
  at a turnover of 5.4 a year. It made less than holding in Bitcoin's best years and lost far less in its worst (2022:
  -23% against -64%). The engine takes the band of 10 points, for the fees, and says the others in the docs.

Built:

- `dotagent/trading/trend.py`: the rule (strength over 20/50/100/200 days, 60-day volatility sized to
  `engine_target_vol`, an equal share for each coin), the steps toward its shares (sells first, the band, no order over
  `max_order`; a sell by what it brings, as the pre-push review found that one by its size, at a bid a little higher
  when placed, went over the cap and was refused at every look), and the backtest of the same code with a cost per side against holding the first product, by year.
- `dotagent/trading/engine.py`: a part of the scheduler's tick, once an hour, on paper only, with no model; its orders go
  through `Trading.place` under the run id `trend-engine`, so the limits, the emergency stop and the mode judge them
  as a run's; a coin Coinbase gave too few days of is left as it is at that look rather than sold, and an order whose
  price could not be read leaves those placed before it in the record (the review); what held one back is kept with
  its last look (`engine_state.py`, the store's key/value table, with
  `Store.kv_delete`), and a pause holds it.
- `[trading] engine` (`off`, `trend`), `engine_coins`, `engine_target_vol`, `engine_band`; switching the engine on
  widens what Dot does with money, so a phone cannot (the page's PATCH), and can switch it off.
- `trading_engine`: status, pause and resume, each with its reason; the prompt's trading lines tell a run never to trade
  the engine's coins itself nor change its rule, and to pause it only for what its closes cannot show.
- `CoinbaseMarket.daily_history` (300 days a request) and `dotx trading backtest`.
- Settings › Trading: Trend engine, its switch, the shares beside those held, what held an order back, the pause.
- docs/trading.md.

Not done, each an item of its own: live orders by the engine (after item 770's live exits, on the owner's word);
maker (post-only limit) orders, which would about halve the fee; a funding-rate or basis trade.

**Checked.** `tests/unit/test_trend.py`: the strength and the volatility, shares over the products judged, the steps
(sells first, the band, the cap, a sell by its worth), the backtest's falls and fees, only ended days as closes; the engine buying what
rises within the limits and saying what held the rest, selling what stops rising at the cap, keeping a coin it was given
too few days of, keeping the orders placed before one whose price could not be read, placing nothing while paused or
out of paper; the supervisor's tool; the history reading Coinbase's days until now and stopping (a fraction of a second at
the end once asked for nothing forever). `tests/smoke/test_trading_settings.py`: the engine switched on from the Mac
only, off from a phone, the page's view of a pause. `tests/smoke/test_trading_settings_page.py`: the card on a wide
page and a phone. `dotx trading backtest` on the real closes.
