### 705. Dot's own pointer shows where its steps on the desktop go

**Status: done (2026-10-26).** The owner: an agent's pointer, glowing, that moves by itself to what it does, as in
another coding agent's computer use; the HID event tap that does the steps is to stay as it is, and nothing that works
is to break.

**The change.** `desktop/pointer.py`: a helper process (`python -m dotagent.desktop.pointer`, built as the flight of
SnapShots is, item 414) draws a violet arrow with a white edge and a glow of its colour in a borderless, click-through
window at the screen saver's level, on every Space, kept out of screen sharing where macOS honours it. It reads one
JSON line per command: `move` glides the arrow's tip to a point (0.16 to 0.45 s, by the way it covers) and, for a
click, ripples there; `hide` takes it off the screen and answers `hidden <n>`; `reveal` puts it back; `rest` fades it.
It fades by itself 2.5 s after its last command. `DesktopSession` starts it with each act (it takes about a second to
start), moves it after each step that left the pointer somewhere (the step's own `Effect.pointer`, where the HID
events went; a click, a double click and a right click ripple), hides it around `desktop_screenshot` and its zoom, and
rests it when the last run that moved it ends. The steps do not wait for it: a glide lands a moment after the click it
shows. `[desktop] show_pointer` (on) turns it off; only the runtime's session draws (a session made elsewhere, as in
tests, has none).

**Never at a step's cost.** A helper that cannot start, or dies, is logged and started again at the next move; a hide
that gets no answer within two seconds (2 ms once the helper runs) ends the helper and draws no pointer until the
daemon starts again, so that no screenshot waits twice. A late answer of an earlier hide is not taken for the next one's
(each hide is numbered).

**The review of the first push:** a hide and a reveal around a screenshot called off the fade the step's move had set
off, so the arrow stayed until the run ended; the fade now counts moves only (`Fades`), and a reveal sets it off again
for the same move. And a SnapShot of the owner's (⌘⌘), whose picture goes to the model, captured the arrow when it was
over the window: `SnapshotService.take` now hides it around its capture, as `desktop_screenshot` does.

**Checked.** `test_desktop_pointer.py`: a click moves the pointer to where the step went and ripples, a scroll moves it
only, a key does not; the setting off moves nothing; a capture is taken with it hidden and it comes back after, also
when the capture fails; the tip lands on the point; a hide waits for its own answer; a helper that does not answer is
ended and nothing waits for it again; one that cannot start costs nothing; the fade of the last move still comes after a hide and a reveal.
`test_snapshots.py`: a SnapShot is captured with the pointer hidden and it comes back after, also when the capture
fails. `test_desktop_pointer_helper.py` (marked
`desktop`, it draws): the real helper moves, answers a hide within 0.1 s once it runs, rests and exits 0.

**Not done.** Dot's browser shows no pointer: its steps go to Chrome directly and move none. Showing one there (over
Dot's Chrome, or in Dot's computer) is a change of its own.
