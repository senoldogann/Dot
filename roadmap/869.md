### 869. Dot's pointer shows the steps done through accessibility, with a label of who does what

**Status: done (2026-10-31).** **Asked** by the owner after item 862: "agent desktop'tan ayrıca ajana nasıl imleç
gösteriyorlar onu da alsaydık keşke". vercel-labs/agent-desktop draws an agent's cursor with its name beside it.

Item 862 made a click on a button's ref into a press through accessibility, which leaves the Mac's pointer where the
owner has it. Dot's own pointer (item 706) was drawn only where the Mac's pointer went. So every such click, and every
press, focus and set_value, showed nothing on the screen.

**What holds.**
- A step done through accessibility says where it went, separately from where it left the Mac's pointer (`Effect.aim`
  and `Effect.pointer`). The batch's check that nobody else moved the mouse reads only `pointer`, as before.
- The aim is the element's press point, read before the step while the element shows on a display. Reading it never
  fails the step: an element that cannot be read is drawn nowhere.
- Dot's pointer glides to the aim and ripples for a press, as for a click.
- The label says who and what: the name of the owner's agent the run runs as, or Dot, then the step's verb, then its
  target as its review named it. It is one line, cut at 56 characters. Near the right edge of the screen the tip is on,
  it stands on the arrow's left.
- The label is drawn in the pointer's own window, which macOS leaves out of other captures and which Dot hides around
  its own. The model never reads it.

**Where.**
- `desktop/actions.py`: `Effect.aim` and `Effect.about`, and `_aim_at`; press, click-as-press, focus, set_value, type
  and paste aim.
- `desktop/session.py`: `act(..., who)`; the batch draws `pointer` or `aim`, with `label_of`.
- `desktop/pointer.py`: `PointerCommand.label`, `label_of`, `label_flips`, and `window_origin(..., flipped)`. In the
  helper, the arrow is a layer of its own, and the label is a pill with a text layer in a window `LABEL_ROOM_PX` wider.
- `tools/desktop.py`: `_who(ctx)`, the agent's name.
- `docs/browser-and-computer-use.md` says so.

**Checked.**
- `tests/unit/test_desktop_pointer.py`:
  - a press and a focus done through accessibility move Dot's pointer to the aim, rippling for the press, with the
    label "Trader presses button 'Archive'", and the batch still expects the Mac's pointer where it was;
  - a key draws nothing;
  - the label's words, cut and flipped, and the flipped window keeps the tip on the point.
- `tests/unit/test_desktop_press_click.py`: a click done as a press and a press aim at the element's press point with
  the Mac's pointer untouched; an element whose frame cannot be read is drawn nowhere, and it is pressed all the same.
- On the screen: the helper's own stage drew the arrow and the label at a point, and near the right edge, where the
  label stood on the left. A probe captured both with the window's sharing allowed for the probe alone.
