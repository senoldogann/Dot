### 1956. `search_files` and `find_files` read ripgrep's output up to a cap, as it comes

**Status: done (2026-11-26).**

The run review of 2026-11-26 (problem 18) found `tools/files.py: run_rg` reading all of ripgrep's output with
`communicate()`, decoding it and splitting it into lines to show 300 of them.

Measured before (a tree of 60 files of 30 000 lines each, 117 MB, searched for a word on every line, in a process of its
own): **397 MB of output, 1.8 M lines, 1.8 GB peak memory, 1.0 s**. After: **2.1 MB read, 9 537 lines, 62 MB peak (the
interpreter's own), 0.07 s**; ripgrep is ended once the cap is read.

`run_rg` now returns `RgOutput` (exit code, stdout, stderr, `capped`), reads stdout in 64 KiB chunks up to 2 MiB and
stderr up to 256 KiB, ends ripgrep when stdout passes the cap (its output would not be read, and it would block writing),
keeps whole lines only and reports the exit code as 0. A capped answer ends with
`[the search was ended at 2 MiB of output: more matches are not shown, narrow it]` (`files` for `find_files`), since how many
more there are is no longer known (it said "N more matches not shown" before). A search under the cap is unchanged, and so
is a cancelled one (`tests/unit/test_search_cancel.py`).

The cap is about 30 000 matches at a typical line, a hundred times the 300 shown. Not changed: the 300 lines shown.

Checks: `tests/unit/test_search_cap.py` (a search past the cap is ended with whole lines; one under it is whole and its
exit code ripgrep's), `tests/unit/test_search_cancel.py`.
