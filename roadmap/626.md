### 626. The iPhone's microphone hears every time: the phone records, the Mac writes it down

**Status: done (2026-10-25).** The owner, from their iPhone (Dot added to the home screen): "after I give the
permission, the first dictation works; when I try again it does not." They got the toast item 616 added ("Safari heard
nothing this time … reload the page"), and they want the microphone to work every time.

**Why.** Dot's microphone used the browser's speech recognition (`webkitSpeechRecognition`, `voice.js`
`createDictation`). On iOS, Safari's recognition goes deaf after the first dictation of a page: `start` and `audiostart`
fire, then nothing (no `speechstart`, no result, no error).
- WebKit bug 321436 (open) has reports on iOS 26.6, 27.0 and 27.2, with no media playing at all.
- Bug 317741 (an audio-session fix in WebKit's recognition source, June 2026) did not end it.
- Per that thread nothing tried restores hearing: `abort()` in place of `stop()`, priming with `getUserMedia`, a single
  instance kept, `navigator.audioSession`. In a home-screen app only killing and relaunching the app does.

**Ruled out.** More workarounds inside Safari's recognition. Item 616 (it detects the deafness and offers a reload) and
item 603 (the first dictation's permissions) stay as they are: 616's toast is what an iPhone shows where the Mac cannot
write down, and it now says what the Mac lacks.

**The structural fix.** On an iPhone or an iPad the page does not use the browser's recognition at all. It records the
microphone itself (`getUserMedia` and Web Audio, which iOS handles every time), sends the recording to the daemon, and
the Mac writes it down on the device with macOS 26's own dictation model (`DictationTranscriber`) through a small Swift
program the daemon builds with `swiftc` on first use. The words go into the box, as before. The voice never leaves the
owner's devices, where Safari's recognition may send it to Apple.

**What was measured on this Mac** (macOS 26.6.2, Xcode installed), before and while building:
- *No permission.* A probe compiled with `swiftc -parse-as-library -O` and run as a plain command-line program asked
  for no Speech Recognition permission and needed none (`SFSpeechRecognizer.authorizationStatus()` stayed 0).
- *Accuracy and speed.* A Turkish sentence spoken by `say -v Yelda` came back word for word, English the same: a
  recording of four or five seconds takes 0.3 to 0.8 s through the daemon's helper, once the language's model is there.
- *The preset.* `.shortDictation` and the `.shortForm` hint give volatile results only, so they are left out; the
  final results, joined by a space, are the words. English is punctuated, Turkish is not (macOS's Turkish dictation adds
  none).
- *Two classes.* `SpeechTranscriber` has no Turkish (de, en, es, fr, it, ja, ko, pt, yue, zh);
  `DictationTranscriber` has 54 locales, Turkish among them (`tr-TR` and `en-US` installed here). The program uses
  `DictationTranscriber`.
- *The format.* `AVAudioFile` reads a WAV, an AIFF and an AAC `.m4a`; `analyzeSequence(from:)` converts by itself.
- *The last word.* A recording that ends as its last word does loses the word (`…the weather in Oulu is cold` came
  back `…is.`); 200 ms of silence after it brings it (0, 200, 400, 600 and 1000 ms measured). The daemon adds 0.8 s.
- *The compiler.* The toolchain's `swiftc`, started by the path `xcrun --find swiftc` gives, in a small environment,
  cannot find its standard library (`unable to load standard library for target 'arm64-apple-macosx26.0'`): the stub at
  `/usr/bin/swiftc` sets `SDKROOT`. The daemon passes `-sdk` (`xcrun --sdk macosx --show-sdk-path`) and builds in 1.4 to
  7 s.
- *The page's own capture.* Two `say` recordings pushed through the page's capture (a Web Audio stream in the place of
  the microphone, 48 kHz, the worklet, the downsampling, the WAV) were written down as the originals were.
- *Loudness.* The same recordings' blocks (2048 samples, 43 ms) of speech are mostly 0.1 to 0.2 (the root of the mean
  square, 0 to 1), their pauses 0, the soft ends of words between 0.003 and 0.03: the level of a sound is 0.01.
- *Chrome's fake microphone.* `--use-fake-device-for-media-stream` still makes macOS ask for the microphone for the app
  that runs the tests: the first attempt raised one permission request (`kTCCServiceMicrophone`, for the app that ran
  this agent), `getUserMedia` waited for an answer, and the runs were ended. No test uses those flags (see Tests).
- *Probing languages.* The probes also wrote down English as `en-GB` and Finnish as `fi-FI`, so macOS may have
  downloaded those two models from Apple onto this Mac.

**What must hold, and what only asks** (item 540: it changes what a run's commands may change).

| What must hold | Held by | What only asks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A run's commands cannot replace the program the daemon builds and runs, nor plant one in the folder it builds in | The kernel: `voice_dir()` is among `_dot_paths`, so the sandbox profile lets a command read the folder and not write, link or move in it, whether it is there or not yet (a test on the real macOS sandbox) | In `yolo` nothing is sandboxed, as for Dot's own code |
| A file changed under the daemon is never run | The SHA-256 of what was built is kept in memory and checked before each run; a mismatch builds anew | |
| The recording and its words stay on the owner's devices and are kept nowhere | A file of mode 0600 in a folder of mode 0700, deleted in a `finally` after success and failure; swept at the daemon's start; never logged (the log has the length and the time); `DictationTranscriber` is on the device | What leaves the Mac is the language's model, downloaded from Apple once |
| Apple's "install the developer tools" dialog never opens on the owner's screen | `xcode-select -p` first; `xcrun` only once it succeeded; the compiler by the path `xcrun` gave, never `/usr/bin/swiftc` | |
| What the route reads is validated before it is used | `RecordingUpload` (size), strict base64, the WAV read chunk by chunk into a model (`Recording`), the language by `^[a-z]{2,3}(-[A-Z]{2})?$` before it is passed, the program's answer into a model | |
| No test builds Swift or opens a microphone | `DOT_SWIFTC` is a compiler that does not exist for every smoke test; the browser tests give the page a stream of their own | |

**As built.**
- *The program* (`src/dotagent/voice/transcribe.swift`). `<program> <wav> <language>` prints one line of JSON,
  `{"text": "…"}` or `{"error": "<code>", "detail": "…"}` (`unsupported-language`, `no-audio`, `failed`), exits 0 or 1
  and prints the words nowhere else. It checks the language with `DictationTranscriber.supportedLocale(equivalentTo:)`,
  installs its assets when they are missing (`AssetInventory`, once for each language) and joins the final results.
- *Can this Mac* (`voice/toolchain.py`). macOS 26 or later (`platform.mac_ver()`), the developer tools (`xcode-select
  -p`), a Swift compiler and an SDK in them (`xcrun --find swiftc`, `xcrun --sdk macosx --show-sdk-path`), each said in
  plain words when missing. `DOT_SWIFTC` names another compiler, as the other seams do. Every program started here has a
  small environment (`PATH` of the system's folders, `HOME`, `USER`, `LOGNAME`, `TMPDIR`, `LANG`).
- *Building and running* (`voice/helper.py`, `Transcriber`). The first use in a daemon's life builds the program (an
  asynchronous subprocess, so that the loop is never blocked) in a fresh folder of mode 0700 under `<home>/voice`,
  from a copy of the source made there; callers wait on one lock. A failed build is not tried again for ten minutes
  and says why, with the compiler's first complaint. Each run starts the program with an argument list, a time limit
  (60 s; five minutes the first time a language is used, which downloads its model) and at most two at once. What the
  Mac has to build with is asked once (see the review below): a compiler found is kept for the daemon's life, a Mac
  without the tools for 30 s (`MISSING_KEPT_S`), a built program makes the question unnecessary, a failed build forgets
  the answer.
- *The recording* (`voice/wav.py`). `read_wav` reads the bytes into a model (16-bit mono, 8 to 48 kHz, not empty, five
  minutes at the most); `canonical_wav` writes the samples again behind a header of Dot's own, with 0.8 s of silence.
- *The routes* (`server/routes_voice.py`, `views.py`, `app.py`, `core/errors.py`, `core/paths.py`, `runtime.py`).
  `GET /api/voice` answers `{"transcribe", "why"}` and starts the build in the background when the Mac can.
  `POST /api/voice/transcribe` takes `{"language", "data_b64"}`, with a limit of its own (about 10 MB, five minutes),
  and answers `{"text"}`; a failure is a 422 with `VoiceError`'s words and `context.code`. Both are behind the token, so
  the phone's paired token reaches them.
- *The page.*
  - `wav.js`: loudness, a resampler that makes each output sample the mean of its stretch (counted in whole units, so
    that the blocks it is pushed in never change it), the WAV file, base64.
  - `voicecapture.js` (the worklet) and `capture.js`: the microphone with echo cancelling and noise suppression, an
    audio context made and resumed in the press, asked once more when iOS puts it aside as it turns to the microphone;
    every end lets the tracks go (the iPhone's light) and closes the context.
  - `recording.js`: `createRecording`, with the dictation's interface and callbacks, so that `microphone.js` takes
    either; the silence (`SILENCE_MS`, `PAUSE_MS`) is counted from what the worklet hears. The owner's tick always
    sends what was recorded, a voice too quiet to count as a sound included, and the Mac says when it heard nothing: the
    level of a sound is a guess without a real microphone, and a quiet voice is not to be thrown away unheard (the
    review's choice between this and dropping a recording with no sound, which the docs had said). Only a tick before
    the first block of the microphone's sound came in has no samples, and is dropped. Six seconds with no sound at all
    end a recording by themselves, saying nothing was heard, and send nothing; the five minutes end it as the tick.
  - `voice.js`, `microphone.js`, `macvoice.js`, `main.js`: which way a page listens (`listeningWay`, from
    `GET /api/voice`, read in the background as the page starts: nothing of the start waits for it) and the words of
    what can fail. Only the failures the Mac words itself (`VoiceError`'s codes) reach the owner as the
    Mac said them; anything else, such as a request the Mac could not read, is told as a failure in plain words (no
    file format, no program) and logged in the console. `app.css`: the quiet line, a live region that a screen reader
    announces as it appears.
  - `views/system.js`: the Voice card says where the voice goes for the way the page listens.

**Which way a page listens.** An iPhone or an iPad records when the Mac can write down, and otherwise keeps the
browser's recognition with item 616's toast, which then adds what the Mac lacks. A browser with no speech recognition
(Firefox) records when the Mac can, and shows the microphone it never had. Everywhere else (a Mac's Safari or Chrome,
Dot.app) the browser's recognition stays: it shows the words while the owner speaks, and it does not go deaf there.

**Review.** The pre-push review found no Important, and two nits, both taken in a second commit.
- *A tick before any sound.* The docs said a tick before any sound came in has nothing to send and drops it; the code
  drops only a recording with no samples at all (`kept === 0`), and a tick after three quiet seconds sends it, and the
  Mac answers that it heard nothing. The choice is to keep the code and say so in the docs: the owner's tick is a
  request, the level of a sound is a guess without a real microphone, and a quiet voice that never counted as a sound
  is not to be thrown away unheard. The docs, the comments of `recording.js` and a test (a tick after quiet blocks
  sends, and the Mac's empty answer is told) say what it does; the test of the tick with no samples was renamed for
  what it is (before the first block of sound).
- *The Mac's tools were asked at every page load.* `GET /api/voice` asked up to three programs of the Mac's
  (`xcode-select -p`, `xcrun --find swiftc`, `xcrun --sdk macosx --show-sdk-path`, ten seconds each) each time, and
  `main.js` waited for the answer with the rest of what the page needs to start, so that a slow `xcrun` held up every
  page, a Mac browser's and Dot.app's too. Both ends are mended.
  - *The daemon* (`Transcriber._toolchain`) asks once and remembers: a compiler found for the daemon's life, a Mac
    without the tools for 30 s (`MISSING_KEPT_S`, so that installing them is seen without a restart), and nothing at all
    once the program is built. A build that fails forgets what the Mac said (an Xcode that was moved leaves a path that
    is out of date), and the callers at the same moment wait for one question.
  - *The page* reads the answer in the background (`macvoice.js`, started by `connect()`, awaited by nobody): the
    start waits for nothing. As asked, a composer made before the answer listens the browser's way. One thing more: a
    microphone that is not listening takes the way the page has as it is pressed (`microphone.js`), since the answer
    comes in a moment later than the composer in the slow case this is for, and an iPhone's first press would otherwise
    be Safari's deaf recognition until another composer is made; and Settings › General › Voice is written again when
    the answer comes in. A Mac that could not be asked at all leaves the page as it was, with the browser's
    recognition, and the card says it. A browser with no speech recognition (Firefox) has the microphone in the
    composers made after the answer.
  - *Tests.* The unit tests of the memory (once for the daemon's life, nothing once built, one question for the
    callers at the same moment, a Mac without the tools asked again after 30 s, a failed build forgets) fail against the
    helper as it was. So does the route test (six pages, one question, and a route that waits for no one) and the
    browser test of a page that starts with the answer held, which fails against a `main.js` that waits for it. The
    daemon of the browser tests remembers what it found, so the tests of a Mac that cannot have a daemon of their own.
