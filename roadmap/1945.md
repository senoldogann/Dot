### 1945. The shell guard reads a long line in time that grows with its length

**Status: done (2026-11-26).** Item 17 of the review of 2026-11-26 (`reports/review-2026-11-26/B-code.md`, M7; K1-07
of the code review of 2026-11-19, left open by item 1250): the guard runs on the event loop, twice for every command,
and a model's `sed -e` script of 16,000 digits froze the whole daemon for about 4 s.

What must hold:
- Every line is decided as before: the same risk, the same flags, the same verdict of the policy. Nothing new asks in
  any mode, and nothing that ran is refused, yolo and free mode included.
- A line of the shapes that were quadratic is judged in time that grows with its length.

What changed (three parts were quadratic):
- `_SED_FILES` (sed's `w`/`W`/`r`/`R` commands and flags): the old pattern tried a match from every digit of a run, as a
  digit is both an address and a flag, and each try read the rest of the run. The new one starts a match only at the
  left end of a run of flags (a line start, a command's end or an address before it, or the run's first digit), with
  possessive runs; its matches are the old pattern's.
- `_assess_all` and `_substituted_listing`: a pipeline was read again from its first stage for every stage (`_piped`,
  `_fed_a_download`, `_fed_a_decode`). It is read once, stage by stage: what the stages before write into the pipe, their
  operands and whether one of them decodes are carried to the next. A pipe's operands are not read when the line has
  fetched and decoded nothing, as none can name such a file.
- `policy/paths.py` `kernel_path`: each folder on the path was made again as a `Path` from its parts. The folders are
  cut from the path's text, and one longer than MAXPATHLEN is not handed to the kernel, whose open fails with
  ENAMETOOLONG for it anyway.

Not changed: the guard still runs on the event loop. A word that names a path of hundreds of thousands of characters
(thousands of `$a` in a row) still spends Python's own `os.path.realpath` time that grows with its square. A line of
thousands of assignments (`a=1 a=1 ... cmd`) still ends the guard in a `RecursionError`, as before.

Measured (process CPU time on the owner's Mac under load, the micro-benchmark in the pull request's body), before and
after: a `sed` script of 16,000 digits 4,170 ms and 27 ms, of 64,000 digits 66,953 ms and 182 ms; a pipeline of 2,000
`cat` stages 16,401 ms and 1,839 ms, of 3,200 shells 68,015 ms and 1,617 ms; `echo` and 8,000 `$a` 5,191 ms and 428 ms;
a path of 8,000 parts 1,012 ms and 78 ms.

Checked: `tests/unit/test_shell_guard_linear.py`: the new sed pattern finds what the old one found on 20,000 scripts
made at random; 1,500 lines made at random from the parts the three changes read (sed scripts, pipelines of printers,
listers, filters, shells, decoders and downloads, protected and long paths) get the decisions the guard gave before the
change, kept in `tests/fixtures/shell_guard_decisions.json` (written by the old guard); each quadratic shape is judged
under 8 s of CPU (before: 16-68 s). `kernel_path` was compared with the old one on 8,013 paths (links, closed folders,
the Data volume, relative paths, paths over MAXPATHLEN): the same on all. The other shell guard tests, ruff, mypy.
