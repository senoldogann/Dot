### 1029. A scroll of the browser reads the page once the wheel has moved it

**Status: done (2026-11-07).** **Found** by `tests/smoke/test_browser.py::test_a_scroll_shows_the_part_of_the_page_it_
moved_to` in the nightly `browser` runs (2 of the last 6): a scroll of 2000 px answered "no change since the last
observation (101 lines unchanged)". `scroll_page` sent the wheel and returned, and the session read the page after its
settle, on the assumption that "the wheel lands a few tens of milliseconds after the call returns". On a busy runner it
landed later, so the model was told that the scroll did nothing and would scroll again.

**Built.** `browser/driver.py` `scroll_page` reads where the page stands under the wheel before it (the window's scroll
and that of each element from the one under the wheel up, which one of them the wheel moves), and returns once that has
changed and stands still at two looks 30 ms apart; a wheel that moves nothing is waited for 1 s at most
(`_WHEEL_LANDS_S`). Each read is bounded by that second too (`_scroll_state`, the pre-push review): a page whose script
holds its main thread, an element drawn again under the wheel, or a document the wheel led to ends the wait, never the
scroll, as the wheel was sent and the session's settle reads what it set off. Docs: `docs/browser-and-computer-use.md`.

**Also in the tests** (the same race, of the test's own making): `test_a_tab_still_opening_when_the_wait_ends_stops_
the_batch_and_joins_later` slept 1.6 s on the server for the late tab, which a slow runner's batch outlasted; the tab
now answers once the test lets it, and the test looks again until it has joined. `test_a_wait_that_times_out_checks_
where_a_pending_navigation_went_before_showing_the_page` let the blocked host answer after 3 s, inside the click's own
settle on a slow runner, so the click was refused before the wait the test is about; the host answers once the click
has returned and the wait has timed out.
