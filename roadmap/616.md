### 616. A later dictation Safari does not hear says so, and Reload brings the microphone back

**Status: done (2026-10-24).** The owner, from their iPhone (Safari over Tailscale), the day item 605's recorder came:
"I allow it, the microphone works once; I press it again, I speak, and it does not work." Their screenshot showed the
recorder of the second dictation with its clock running (0:04) and its wave flat.

**What it was.**

- **Safari's, not Dot's.** The clock runs from the engine's `start` or `audiostart`, so the second engine began and
  opened the microphone; the wave moves on its `soundstart` and `speechstart` and nothing came, nor a word. That is
  WebKit bug 321436: on an iPhone, a later `start()` gives `start` and `audiostart` and then nothing, and a stop or an
  abort brings "aborted" ("No speech detected") and `end`. Reported on iOS 26.6 and 27.0 after media played, and on
  27.2 with none, as a regression since 26.6.2. Bug 317741 (fixed in WebKit on 2026-06-26, not yet in every iOS) says
  why: the speech recognizer's microphone did not keep its audio session active. The first dictation after the page
  loads hears; reloading the page brings it back. The workarounds reported (a pause before the next start, opening the
  microphone with `getUserMedia` first, a Web Audio sound, `navigator.audioSession.type`) did not help on 27.2 or made
  every dictation deaf, so none is taken.
- **Dot hid it.** Safari answers the stop of a dictation that heard nothing with "aborted", where Chrome says
  "no-speech". The dictation took any "aborted" for the owner's own cancel and said nothing, so the recorder closed
  on the box as it was. And when Safari answered no stop at all, the tick's wait (`SETTLE_MS`) ended it with the same
  silence.

**As built** (`static/js/voice.js`, `microphone.js`, `ui.js`).

- **"aborted" heard by a dictation is the engine's own.** The dictation's own abort (the cross, typing, a send, the
  page out of sight) lets the engine go first, so its "aborted" never reaches it; one that does is Safari's word for a
  silence and counts as "no-speech" does: one the owner did not stop says nothing was heard and what to check.
- **A later dictation that hears nothing on an iPhone or an iPad** (one whose page, by its document, had a dictation
  that began before; an iPad asking for a computer's pages counts) says so, whoever ended it: "Safari heard nothing
  this time. On an iPhone or an iPad it at times hears only the first dictation after the page loads: reload the page
  and press the microphone again, or use the keyboard's microphone." Its toast has **Reload** (`toast` takes an
  action, and stays 15 seconds with one); the box's words are its draft, which the reload keeps.
- **The tick that Safari never answers** ends the dictation with what it heard, as the abort it was ended with did,
  and an iPhone's later dictation that heard nothing says it is Safari's (`end()`), where it said nothing.
- **A dictation stops a reading aloud first**: Safari does not hear while it speaks, and a microphone that heard the
  reading would write the answer into the box.
- **The docs.** `docs/daemon.md` (the composer's microphone) and `docs/phone.md` (by voice).

**Not done, and why.** Dot cannot make Safari hear: the cause is in WebKit, and no page-side workaround held on the
iOS where it is reported. A page reload is what is known to work, and it is one press away now.

**Tests.**

- `tests/js/dictation.test.js` (+6):
  - a page's first dictation that Safari answers with "aborted" says nothing was heard;
  - on an iPhone, a later one that hears nothing says it is Safari's and offers Reload;
  - on a computer every one says only that nothing was heard;
  - an iPad asking for a computer's pages is an iPad;
  - the owner's tick that the engine never answers ends with the words heard, or says it heard none;
  - a dictation stops a reading aloud.

  Four of them fail against the code before (the computer one guards what did not change).
- `tests/smoke/test_voice.py`: on an iPhone page, the second dictation that hears nothing shows the toast with Reload,
  the box as it was, and Reload gives a page that starts anew with the box's words. It fails against the code before.
