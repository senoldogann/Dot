### 1252. A click at a pixel is judged by the element under it

**Status: done (2026-11-19).** The code review of 2026-11-19 (B2-01) found that `click_at` and `drag_at` named no
element, so the policy judged them by nothing: a run held back from `click e3 "Pay now"` could press the same button by
its pixel, and a page could ask for that.

`browser/snapshot.py` `refs_at_point` finds the elements the last snapshot drew under a pixel: of the boxes holding
it, in an iframe too (an iframe's boxes count from where the frame stands), each that holds no other, put in the place
of the nearest control above it (a button's icon presses the button), and all of them where boxes overlap, since the
snapshot does not say which is on top. `browser_act` judges the step by them as it judges a click on a ref (the name,
the typed judge for a name no pattern knows, the payment count), and `drag_at` as a `drag` onto the element under its
end. A pixel step after a step that may move the page in the same batch is sent back, since the snapshot's boxes no
longer hold for it; several pixel steps in a row still go together (a captcha's tiles).

The independent security review of this branch found the first version's two gaps (the icon inside a button, an
overlapping box) and a stale snapshot after a scroll in the batch; all three are closed above. Where the snapshot drew nothing under the
pixel (a canvas, a page drawn since), the step is judged as before: asking there would stop every click on a map or a
game.

Checked: `tests/unit/test_pixel_click.py` (the button's pixel is a payment, a frame's offset, nothing under the pixel),
the browser policy's unit tests.
