### 708. A long chat opens whole at its foot, at once

**Status: done (2026-10-26).** **Asked by the owner:** in a chat of many messages, going in and out threw the scroll up
and the page took a while to come right, as if it froze. **Measured** on a copy of the owner's longest chat (78 turns,
2,742 events), in a throwaway server with no scheduler and a scripted model: opening it read the work of all 40 turns
shown at once (40 reads of `GET /runs/{id}/events`), and the page moved 9 times over 1.5 s (Chrome) and 1.35 s (WebKit)
before it stood at its foot.

**Why.** A chat's page holds `html.chat-shell` at every width, and `history.js`'s `scrollBox` took it for "the phone,
where the conversation scrolls in its own box". On a wider window that box does not scroll and is as tall as every turn,
so each older turn's watch (`whenNear`) saw its turn near what is seen and read and drew its work in as the chat opened;
and where the browser keeps no anchor (WebKit: Safari, Dot.app), the place kept as a turn grew above the reader was
counted against a box that does not scroll. Then the last six turns, read after the page was drawn, grew in under the
reader.

**The change.** The box is the phone's only within the phone's width. The last six turns' work is read before the page
is drawn, side by side (`READ_FIRST`), so the chat opens whole at its foot; older turns read theirs as the reader
scrolls up to them.

**Checked.** The same measure: 6 reads of events instead of 40; the page stands at its foot in 0.32 s (Chrome) and 0.58 s
(WebKit), with one move; back to the chat from another, in 0.03 s and 0.07 s (0.29 s and 0.5 s before); on a phone
(WebKit, 390 px) one move to the foot. `tests/smoke/test_thread_turns.py`: a chat of 20 tall turns reads the work of
its last six only as it opens, stands at its foot, and reads the oldest's once scrolled up to it (it fails without the
change); the chat and scroll tests of the chat pages and the phone, and `npm run check`.
