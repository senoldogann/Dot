### 693. An Office file that unpacks to more than it may is refused before it is read

**Status: done (2026-10-26).** `_package_problem` of `office.py` looked only at how the parts of a package were packed
and whether they were locked. A 100 KB `.docx` whose `word/document.xml` unpacks to 34 MB took the daemon to 1.15 GB
of resident memory while `python-docx` built the tree of it; `read_file` (`tools/files.py`) and the attachments of a
message (`agent/attachments.py`) call `office_text` on any `.docx`, `.xlsx` or `.pptx`, and the panel beside a chat
(`previews/shown.py`) calls `office_content`.

**The change.** `_package_problem` now refuses, with the reason in the error that says the file cannot be read, a
package of more than `_MAX_PARTS` (10,000) parts; whose parts declare more than `_MAX_PACKAGE_BYTES` (200 MB) in all;
with a part that declares more than `_MAX_PART_BYTES` (64 MB), or, when it is XML that its reader builds a tree of,
`_MAX_XML_PART_BYTES` (16 MB; the sheets of a workbook, which are read as a stream, are exempt from this one); or a part of
1 MiB or more packed more than `_MAX_RATIO` (200) times smaller than it unpacks to. The checks go by the sizes the parts
declare, so each part is then unpacked in 64 KB chunks, to the size it declares and to what the package may hold, before
any reader takes it: `zipfile` cuts a part at its declared size and checks its CRC at the end, so a part that declares
less than it holds is a damaged package (`BadZipFile`), and the chunked read refuses it before a reader builds a tree of
it. `docs/runs.md` says so.

**Checked.** `tests/unit/test_office.py`: a 100 KB package whose document.xml unpacks to 20 MB is refused, naming the part;
8 MB of zeros as a picture is refused for its ratio; the part count and the total, with the bounds lowered for the test;
a part that declares 100 bytes of a document.xml is refused as damaged; a package with 12 MB of incompressible picture
is still read. The earlier tests of the file still pass.

**Not done.** The 16 MB bound of an XML part is a choice: a tree takes about thirty times the XML, so a part at the bound
still takes about half a gigabyte for the moment it is read. A workbook's `sharedStrings.xml` is read whole by openpyxl
and has the XML bound too: a workbook with more than 16 MB of distinct strings is refused. Neither was measured on files
of other people.
