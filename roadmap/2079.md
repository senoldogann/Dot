### 2079. A stuck tab with a navigation under way is said after one timeout, within browser_snapshot's limit

**Status: done (2026-11-28).** Dot's record held `browser_snapshot timed out after 45s` four times. The log of
2026-11-23 18:46-18:56 shows two runs (`run_mwths0fo_6e9809`, `run_mwtf9lsn_1ebc07`) whose Chrome stopped answering
(an X upload and a form in the same Chrome; even `goto` of other pages timed out until Chrome was started again at
18:55). Each `browser_snapshot` was cut by the tool's own 45 s limit, retried once and cut again: the run got two
minutes of waiting and a bare timeout instead of the error item 753 wrote for a stuck tab ("close this tab and open the
page in a new one").

The cause is Dot's: settling the page before a view (`BrowserSession._settle`) waited up to `navigation_timeout_s`
(30 s) for a navigation under way to commit, and only then asked the page to answer, with another 30 s. A stuck tab
with a navigation under way was said after 60 s, past the limit of the tool that waited.

What changed:
- `driver.tab_answers` asks the page to answer within a bound (a document replaced under the call is asked again) and
  raises `TabSilentError` when it does not; `_wait_quiet` uses the same check.
- `_settle` asks it first when a navigation of the tab is under way, so a stuck tab is said after one timeout. A page
  that answers waits for the commit as before; a tab with no navigation under way costs no extra call.
- docs/browser-and-computer-use.md says so.

How it was checked: `tests/unit/test_browser_stuck_tab.py::test_a_stuck_tab_with_a_navigation_under_way_is_said_after_one_bound`
fails without the change (2 s for a 1 s timeout) and passes with it; the file's other tests, `tests/smoke/test_browser.py`,
ruff and mypy pass.
