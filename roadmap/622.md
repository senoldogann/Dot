### 622. Rules for someone who knows no software: one line to add a rule, rules as sentences, the rest under Advanced

**Status: done (2026-10-24).** The owner showed another assistant's "Bot" card (an Auto review switch, "Custom rules",
and one line: "When it wants to …", "It should …", Add rule) and asked for the Rules page to be like it: "a simple UI
but a solid structure, easier to understand", and, for the whole app, "everything should be easy even for someone who
knows nothing about software". This item is the page and its words. What a rule means and how the policy decides are not
touched: nothing under `src/dotagent/policy/`, `agent/review.py` or `agent/approvals.py` changed (no path under "Always
needs a human" in REVIEW.md does), there is no new route, and the drafter, the order the rules are tried in, the
hand-off that decides wherever it stands, the checks that are always on and the sandbox are as they were.

**What stayed, and why.**
- A rule said in words is drafted by the review model into its parts (item 110), and Dot writes the pattern from them:
  the page does not write one, and does not change the answer a draft came back with. The adder gives the drafter the
  words and the choice as one sentence, "When Dot wants to run git status, it should ask me first." (the box holds 400
  characters, so that it stays under the 500 the route takes), and a draft whose answer is not the one chosen is not
  offered: the drafter writes a wide pattern for a rule that stops Dot and a narrow one for a rule that lets Dot act
  (item 561), so changing only the answer of a draft would make one into the other.
- A rule is tried against the last 30 days before it is added (item 469): the draft is tried at once, a line says how
  many of the things Dot did it would have applied to, and the whole of it, with Test on the last 30 days for after a
  part is changed, is under the draft's Details. How a rule was used (item 473) is under its own Details.
- The order and the hand-off that decides wherever it stands (items 449 and 481), the arrows, the switch of a rule,
  Delete and the live reads of the page (a rule added or deleted elsewhere draws the list again).
- The checks that are always on and the sandbox of a run's commands (items 262, 559), with the words they had, said more
  plainly.
- "Make a rule of this" on an approval card.

**As built.**
- `views/rules.js` (the page) and `ruleadder.js` (the line that adds a rule and the rule it reads back),
  `rulesentence.js` (a rule drawn as a sentence) and `rulewords.js` (the words, pure, read by
  `tests/js/rulewords.test.js`).
- The head says what rules are for in one line. **Add a rule** is one card: "When Dot wants to" and a box ("e.g. open a
  website"), "It should" and a choice, **Add rule** (off while the box is empty; Enter adds too), and under them what
  the chosen answer does. On a phone, or wherever the page is narrow, the captions stand over their controls: the box on
  one line, the choice and the button on the next.
  - The choice is *Allow on its own*, *Ask me first* and *Leave it to me* (the answers `allow`, `ask` and `handoff`);
    it starts at *Ask me first*, the one that cannot make Dot do more than it does now. The fourth answer,
    `preapproved`, is *Allow, whatever it has read*, and is set under Details.
  - Add rule reads the words (a spinner and "Dot is reading your words…", the box and the choice held as they are). The
    draft comes back read as a sentence: **Dot understood: When Dot wants to run “git status” or “git diff” (with plain
    arguments, after changing folder at most), it goes ahead without asking.**, with a line
    of what it would have done on the last days, **Add it**, **Change the words** (the box is the owner's again, as it
    was) and **Details**: the tool, the pattern, the answer and why, as the old form had them, to be changed, and the
    try-out. The answer of a draft is the choice made and is not changed alone (its pattern was written for it); a tool
    or a pattern that is changed makes the rule the owner's own: the answer is theirs to set, the try-out is put away,
    why is emptied when it still holds the drafter's sentence, and the line says "Your rule:".
  - Words that make no rule (422) keep the words and the choice in the box and say, in a card, "Dot could not make a
    rule of these words." and why (the model's reason, in English), then what to do: say what Dot wants to do, like
    “open a website”, and try again, or **Fill in the details yourself**, which opens the parts empty under the choice
    made. Any other failure (the model could not answer: 502, 500, no daemon) says "Dot could not read your words as a
    rule just now." with its message and the same way out. A draft that is not the answer chosen says what Dot
    understood and what was chosen, and adds nothing.
- **Your rules** is a numbered list, the order they are checked in. A row is the switch, its number, **one sentence that
  ends in its answer as a badge** ("When Dot wants to run a command that mentions “rm”, it asks you first."), the
  arrows, **Details** and Delete (which asks first, in the rule's own sentence). What a rule fits is read from its
  pattern, which is what the policy matches, and from nothing else (`rulewords.js`): a sentence must say neither less
  nor more than its rule fits. A pattern that is nothing but words is "mentions “a” or “b”" when it keeps to whole words
  (a boundary, `\b`, at each end of a word that is a letter or a digit, as the drafter writes them) and "contains “a”"
  when it fits the letters anywhere, with no boundary (`rm` fits "format"); every character that is no sign of a
  pattern is itself (`don’t`, `50 €`, a space at the edge of a word: the rule asks for it), a sign only when a backslash
  makes it so (`x\.com`), and the drafter's `[ \t]+` between a command's words reads as a space. What is a letter is
  what the engine's `\b` takes as one (`\w`, held to the engine by `tests/unit/test_rule_words.py`: a circled letter, a
  vowel sign and `‿` are, `²` and `½` are not). The drafter's own pattern for a command a rule lets Dot run, with plain
  words between its head and its tail, is "run “git status” (with plain arguments, after changing folder at most)": the
  pattern fits the whole line of the command, its arguments plain when they hold none of `;` `&` `|` `$` `<` `>` `(`
  `)` or a backtick (they chain a command to it, substitute one into it or send it on), and not "any arguments", which
  says more than the rule fits. Any other pattern is shown as it is, in code, with "looks like", the whole of it in its
  tooltip and in Details: cut at 40 characters for a rule that stops Dot, whole for one that lets it act. These are such
  patterns, for they fit more or less than words say: a boundary at one end of a word only (`\bgit push` fits "git
  pushes" and not "digit push"), whole words in one alternative and the letters anywhere in another, a boundary beside a
  sign (`\b€50` needs a letter before the sign), a character that is not printed and a space that is not the plain one
  (a no-break space fits only itself). The tool is said as a person says it, in words that are its own ("run a
  command", "write a file", "find a file", "work on a web page (click, type, press keys, drag)", "use a connected app";
  two tools never share a phrase, any other is said by its name). The rule's why is not read at all: the old form let
  the owner change the pattern and keep the sentence the drafter had written for the old one, and `POST /api/rules`
  takes any why.
  - The arrows show when the pointer is on the row or a key is in it, always on a touch screen, and not at all while
    there is one rule: the order matters when a rule fits what an earlier one fits.
  - A rule that another rule decides in place of says **Never used** and, under its sentence, in words, which rule and
    what helps ("Rule 1 already covers everything this rule covers and is checked first, so this rule is never used.

  - **Details** (a button in the row's actions; the open ones stay open as the list is drawn again) show the tool, the
    pattern, why and how the rule was used on the last 30 days, or that it is switched off.
  - Under the rules: how two that fit are told apart ("the one higher up decides. A rule that leaves it to you always
    wins, wherever it stands.", with two or more rules) and that the built-in checks come first, with **See them all**,
    which opens Advanced. With no rule the card is the shield, "No rules yet", what Dot does meanwhile and three
    sentences to start from; each puts its words **and its choice** in the line.

**The words of the brief that are not the page's, and why.**
- *Never.* A rule has four answers, and none refuses outright (docs/autonomy.md: "a rule of yours cannot refuse"). The
  nearest is the hand-off: Dot does not do the step, it says what needs doing and waits for the owner to do it. The
  page calls it *Leave it to me* and says "Dot does not do it" in its words; a choice called "Never" would promise
  more than the policy does. A refusing answer is a change of the policy, which is a human's.
- *"If two rules disagree, asking you wins."* In the policy the first enabled rule that fits decides, and only a rule
  that hands the step to you decides wherever it stands (`ruling_rule`): an `ask` rule below an `allow` rule that fits
  the same actions never decides, which is why such a rule says "Never used". The page says what is true.
- *"System settings"* among the built-in checks. The questions of the preset (system settings, privilege, public posts)
  come after the owner's rules in the decision order, so a rule that allows them decides first. The page lists what
  docs/safety.md puts before the rules, and says that YOLO mode, which asks nothing, is the one exception.

**The switch.** Left out. Auto review on Grok's card is the check that asks first when needed; in Dot that check is the
policy, which is not a setting. The settings Dot has near it do not map without making Dot ask less: `[policy]
approvals` is a choice of four (who answers; *Auto* has the review model answer in the owner's place, so the switch
turned on would make Dot ask the owner less than it does now, and off is the default), `yolo_taint_check` is a check
that is on by default and off asks less, and the preset is not set on the page. A switch whose off is "ask nothing" is
what the owner excluded; one whose off is only "ask as much" has no setting behind it.
