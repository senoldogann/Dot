### 1672. A pause of Dot's event loop is caught with where it was, logged and listed under Diagnostics

**Status: done (2026-11-24).** One asyncio loop serves every run, every stream of the pages and the phone, and the API.
A call that blocks it (a synchronous read, a PyObjC call waiting on macOS) froze all of them at once, and nothing said
so: "the phone froze" had no trace.

What changed (`loop_watch.py`, `server/daemon.py`, `server/context.py`, `server/routes_about.py`, the Diagnostics page):

- `LoopWatch`, started by the daemon from inside its loop: a thread of its own asks the loop to answer every half second
  (`call_soon_threadsafe`). When it has not answered for 2 s, the thread reads the loop thread's stack from
  `sys._current_frames()` while the loop is still stuck (which names the blocking call, as a sampling from inside the
  loop could not), logs `loop_stalled` with the seconds and the stack, waits for the answer and logs `loop_stall_ended`
  with how long it lasted. The last 20 stalls are kept in memory.
- `GET /api/diagnostics/stalls` (`stall_s`, `stalls`: `began_at`, `seconds`, `ongoing`, `stack`, newest first), and a
  **Pauses** card under Settings › System › Diagnostics: when, how long, and the stack under **Details**.

Checked: `tests/unit/test_loop_watch.py` (a `time.sleep` of 2.8 s in the loop is one stall, ended, of 2 to 4 s, whose
stack names the blocking function; a loop that keeps answering has none; without the stack read the first fails),
`tests/smoke/test_about_pages.py` and `test_plain_words.py` (the settings screens), `npm run check`.
Not done: the page was not looked at on a phone-sized window by hand; the browser tests draw it.
