### 1926. DotNext's live streaming is light on the iPhone's battery and data, measured before and after, and the app's own performance record goes to the owner's Mac

**Status: done (2026-11-26).** The owner's backlog item P1: a run streaming to the phone must cost little battery and
data, measured before and after, and the app's own performance data must be collected for him.

**How it was measured.** The streaming check (`ios/Next/scripts/e2e_streaming.sh`) runs a throwaway daemon whose scripted
model streams a fixed run (`stream:`: four steps that think aloud for about 4 s and read a file, then a 300-word answer
at about 40 words a second; `stream long:`, ten steps, for the run seen from away). Two counters were added for it and
stay: the engine's `Traffic` (the bytes of every body read and sent, requests, streams opened and broken) and the app's
`PerfCounters` (pictures the app's model published, steps the answer's reveal drew) with the process's CPU time
(`getrusage`) and wakeups (`TASK_POWER_INFO`); the debug phase meter writes them per stretch (the chat open, the home, the
background) to `dotnext-phases.txt`, and the script samples the daemon's side every second (its established
connections, and the bytes it sent, by `nettop`), because the simulator suspends an app in the background and the app
then counts nothing. The "before" build is `origin/main` with these counters only. The Mac was loaded all day (load
average 90-200, several sessions' simulators and builds), so what depends on time (CPU and wakeups a second, the frame
meter's hitches) is noisy; the numbers below are per run (the run is the same every time) or the daemon's own counts.
Instruments (`xctrace`) was not used: under that load a trace of the simulator measures the Mac's contention more than
the app; the kernel's counters per run measure the same thing without it.

Before and after (`testAStreamingRunIsMeasured`: three runs before, two after; `testAStreamingRunCostsLittleUnseen`: two
runs each side, one before with the short run, which had ended by the time the app left the screen):

| --- | --- | --- |
| bytes the app read | 421-423 KB | 210-212 KB |

| pictures published (redraws of the chat) | 624-1,170 | 274-287 |

| --- | --- | --- |

| back in front | the kept streams replayed what was missed | one stream opened per screen from its cursors, the chat's view read once (3 requests), the chat caught up |

What cost, found while measuring:
- The daemon sent every token as its own SSE frame (about 50 a second while a model writes, each ~200 bytes of frame
  for ~5 bytes of word), and the app published a picture for nearly each (up to 30 a second).
- In the background nothing ended a stream: the supervisor only paused the outbox, so the list's and the open chat's
  streams stayed open while iOS suspended the app, and the daemon kept writing every keepalive and word into them, which
  wakes a phone's radio.
- A running tool's shimmer was a free-running `TimelineView(.animation)` (60 or 120 Hz on ProMotion), also through the
  1.45 s of each cycle when the band is still.
- Activity read everything again every 30 s while on screen, though the list's stream tells each run, ask and draft;
  Connect apps woke every 5 s even with nothing waiting.
- A photo went as the camera made it (12 MP, about 3-4 MB of JPEG, base64 on top), and a screenshot was decoded whole.

What changed:
- The daemon (`thread_sync.follow_thread`) holds a model's words for up to 80 ms and sends the tokens of the same run,
  step and kind (thinking or text) as one `delta`: about twelve frames a second. Any other event the stream sends goes
  out after the words held, so the order is kept; another chat's events leave them held. Only the phone reads this
  stream; `/api/runs/{id}/stream` (the web) is unchanged.
- The app's model publishes at most 15 pictures a second (was 30); the answer's reveal paces the words on screen as
  before, so the look is the same.
- `Supervisor.enterBackground` ends every stream (and the outbox's sending, as before); `enterForeground` opens each
  again from its cursors, the list's first, which reads each open chat's view once. A chat left while away is not
  opened again. The probe by heartbeat age and the 10 s rule are gone (`StreamRules.probeAge`, `resubscribeAfter`).
- The shimmer draws 30 frames a second while its band moves and none while it rests (`ShimmerSchedule`): about 15
  frames a second on average instead of 60-120. While building it the first schedule rounded a frame onto the same
  moment again and again in the rest (a busy loop of 100,000 frames in 30 s, which the UI tests caught as an app that
  never idles); the schedule now always moves a millisecond at least, and a test with a 2026 date holds it.
- Activity reads again when the list's stream tells a lifecycle event (`EngineSnapshot.heard`), settled 400 ms, and
  when the stream is up again; no timer. Connect apps reads every 5 s only while a service waits on the Mac.
- A photo is cut to 1,600 px on its long side before it is kept for sending (ImageIO decodes picked photos straight to
  that size), its composer preview to 240 px, and a screenshot is decoded to 1,600 px (`Data/ImageSizing.swift`).
- The streaming check's live chat scene opens its first chat when the list was read before the home was drawn too
  (`onChange(initial: true)`): with the new pace it sometimes was, and the check never opened the chat.

**The performance record.** `Data/PerfReporter.swift` subscribes to MetricKit (`MXMetricManager`): a day's CPU and GPU
time, time on screen and in the background, hang time (the histogram summed), the median launch, peak memory, disk
writes, cellular and Wi-Fi data, scroll hitches; and a diagnostics delivery's count of crash, hang, CPU and disk-write
reports, never their contents. Each time the app is open becomes a report of its own counters (seconds, bytes in and
out, requests, streams opened and broken, redraws, CPU). Reports wait in the app's caches (the newest 200) and go when
the app is open and paired, 50 at a time; nothing is sent from the background, and nothing goes anywhere but the Mac.
The daemon's new route `POST /api/phone/metrics` (a phone's own key; the Mac's token is refused) keeps the newest 300 of
the last 30 days per phone in its kv store (`server/phone_metrics.py`, `routes_phone_metrics.py`); `GET` answers the
last seven days summed; removing a phone drops its record. Settings › System › Maintenance shows **This phone's
performance**: processor, hangs, crashes, launch, memory at most, data with the Mac, reconnects, redraws a minute.

What must hold: a report is numbers only (the model refuses any other key); a phone writes and reads only its own record;
nothing is sent to a third party. What only asks: nothing (no route under "Always needs a human" changed; the guard lets a
phone's key reach `/api/phone/metrics` as it does `/api/phone/push-settings`).

Checks: `tests/smoke/test_thread_sync.py` (the words come joined, in order, a step's not with the next's, held words go
before the next event, another chat's never come), `tests/smoke/test_phone_metrics.py` (the record kept and summed, the
Mac's token refused, a malformed report refused whole, a removed phone's record dropped), DotEngine `SyncTests` (in the
background every stream ends and comes back from its cursors with one view read; a chat closed away stays closed) and
`PerformanceRecordTests`, DotNext `PerformanceTests` (picture sizes, the shimmer's frames, histograms, a time open as a
report, the Maintenance rows), and the streaming check before and after above.
