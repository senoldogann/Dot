### 625. Every screen in words someone who knows no software reads at once

**Status: done (2026-10-25).** The owner asked (2026-10-24): "The whole interface, its content and its use should be easy
even for someone who knows nothing about software." This item changes words, not what anything does: no route, id,
setting, event or datum changed, and nothing under `src/dotagent/policy/`, `agent/review.py` or another path REVIEW.md
lists under "Always needs a human" changed. Left to their own items: Settings › Rules (item 622, the model of this one),
Settings › Vault (item 621) and the microphone's words (`voice.js`, `microphone.js`, item 626).

**The way of working.** A scratch Dot (a scripted model, a throwaway home, a Mac at rest) was opened on every screen at
1280 and at 390, with its folds, menus and dialogs open, and the words a person sees were collected, with the titles
and labels of the controls (the inventory). Each text that a person who has never programmed would have to ask about
was changed, and the expert's words moved under a closed fold. The glossary at the foot of this file has a row for each
word or sentence that changed: the screen, the text as it was, the text as it is, and why (about 750 rows).

**One name for a thing, on every screen and in every toast.**
- Dot's own program is **Dot**, never the daemon or the server: "Could not reach Dot. Is it running? Open Dot again, or
  restart your Mac."
- A run is a **task** (a **chat** or **Dot** when the sentence is about those): "Tasks may use the browser", "Stop this
  task", "Task details", "Lessons from this task". What Dot does stays a verb ("Run now", "Ran 2 commands").
- A sign-in token is a **key** ("access key", "service account key"), and the code for a phone is the **sign-in code**,
  as Settings › Phone calls it (the button that said "Pair" says **Sign in**); a model's tokens are **usage** ("usage
  2.1k"), and the size of what a chat holds is the **chat size** (it was "context window" and "budget").
- A model's tool is an **action** ("Choose actions", "Actions of connected apps"; the harness's "Tool guidance" is
  **Tips**); an MCP server is a **connected app** ("Add connected app", "Skills & connected apps"); a provider is an
  **AI service** (the tab Settings › Providers is **AI services**); the preset is the **safety level** and the policy
  "Dot's safety rules"; the review model, where a sentence says what it does, is **the reviewer** (the role keeps the name
  Review model in Settings › Models, with its job said in words there).
- The config file is the **settings file**, and its keys (`[agent] resume`, `[harness] step_time`, `[server] reach`,
  `[watchers] enabled`, `[agent] context_tokens`) stand under Details; cron is the rhythm in words ("Every day at 08:00",
  with **Custom timing** for the rest), a firing is a **start** and the list of them **History**; Execution is **How Dot
  works**, the Composer is **Messages**, the Details panel is the **Side panel**, Artifacts are **Files made**, compacting
  is **summing up** the older part of a chat, a delegated run is a **helper's task**.

**What moved under a closed fold** (the expert's words are not removed):

  **Details** (`errors.js`: toasts, forms, an inline error, a page that could not open, the start-up screens, the notes of

  Dot daemon", "HTTP 500" and "fetch failed" alone.

  unless the key is what failed, and `dotx open`, `dotx url` and the path of the program stand one fold further in,
  **Sign in from Terminal**, so that a key that was refused does not put them in sight. The screen that cannot reach Dot
  says it tries again by itself, with `dotx daemon` under **More help**; the one that gets an error has the code and the

  say how it stands. Settings › Browser: **Advanced** holds how Chrome stands and the port for programs. Settings ›
