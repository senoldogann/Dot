### 582. What Dot tells you on its own (the morning brief, the watchers) reaches iMessage too

**Status: done (2026-10-23).** Items 570 and 571 tell the owner what Dot has to say on its own, the first line of the
morning brief and each tell of a watcher, through one function, `tell_owner` (`notify/owner.py`), which publishes the
live event `owner_told`: the phone's push and the page's notifications listen for it. Item 572 gave Dot a router of
channels and iMessage, with a `ChannelRouter.tell(text)` that nothing called yet, and item 570 left "the channels reach
the owner through the same event" for later. This item joins them: the router follows `owner_told` too, and sends each
tell into the owner's conversation with themselves, marked `◆`, while iMessage is on and a number is paired. It takes
`tell(text)` away: nothing but its own test called it, and a line sent through it would skip the window below.

**The approach.** Nothing new is let in, so there is little to hold: the line is Dot's own, it goes out through the
same `IMessageChannel.send` as every answer, and it comes back in the channel's own rule.
- *Never read back as a command.* `IMessageChannel._send_to` puts `◆ ` before every message it sends, and `_handle`
  lets go of every row that starts with it; a tell is such a message, whoever wrote the words in it (a mail's subject
  may be in its text). A test writes the row Messages would keep of a tell into the owner's conversation and checks that
  the message after it, which is a command, is the only run. A tell ends with the link to its page, and Messages may
  split a link at the end of a message into a row of its own, a link preview, which has no `◆` (the review of the
  batch): the channel lets go of a row that is only a link into the address the router's links open at, as a link
  preview and as words (item 572), and a test writes both rows after a tell.
- *One address.* A tell carries a route of Dot's own pages (`told()` refuses any other), and the router opens it at its
  `base`, as it opens the links of its other lines: `https://<[server] reach>/` or this Mac's own address, never one
  that carries the access token. `base` and the route are joined; nothing the third party wrote is in the address.
- *Masked.* The line goes through `_say`, as the answers do: the secrets Dot holds are masked, then the plain text is
  made, then it is cut to the channel's size (a tell is far shorter: 80 characters of title, 400 of text).
- *The pace.* A watcher that finds five mails tells five times in a row, and the owner's conversation is not a feed.
  The router already limits the line that says a run waits to one in `ASKS_APART_S` (20 s) and lets the rest go; a
  tell is what the owner wants to know of, so it is not let go but folded: a channel gets one line of tells in a
  window, the tells that come inside it are held (their number, the title they share, the newest) and go as one line,
  *Bank mail: and 3 more*, when the window is over, with the link of the newest. The first tell after a quiet window
  goes at once; a tell that waited alone goes as itself, not as "and 1 more"; tells of different titles are counted
  under the name "Dot". The window is each channel's own (`_told_at`), apart from the pace of the asks, since a line
  that says a run waits and a tell have nothing to do with one another.

**What was built.**
- `channels/router.py`: `OWNER_TOLD_EVENT` joins `WATCHED_KINDS`, so the one subscription of `Channels.start()` carries
  it. `_tell_of` sends an `owner_told` event (it has no run: `SYSTEM_RUN_ID`) to `_told_on_its_own`, which holds the
  tell for each channel that is on and paired (`_reachable`) and sends what a channel's window allows (`_tell_held`).
  The held tells wait without a task of their own: `watch` waits for the next event for no longer than the window has
  left (`_held_for`), and sends the held line when that time passes. A channel that was switched off or lost its number
  while its tells were held is not told of them, and no window starts for a line that was not sent. A line that could
  not go (Messages refused it) is logged by `_say`, not tried again, and its window starts as a sent line's does, so
  that a refusing Messages is not asked a line after line. The log says how many tells a line held, never their words.
- `ChannelRouter.tell` is gone, with its test and the parts of the test's fake channel that only it used; `_say`, whose
  answer only `tell` read, returns nothing.
- `channels/service.py`: `Channels` takes `asks_apart_s` (as it takes `poll_s` and `ask_wait_s`) and hands it to the
  router, so that a test can see a line held and then sent in under a second.
- Docs: `docs/channels.md` (how a tell comes, the pace, what leaves), `docs/proactive.md` ("Telling you", and what the
  brief and a tell are), `docs/safety.md` ("What leaves this Mac": the iMessage bullet names the brief and the
  watchers, with their 400 characters of text, among Dot's answers that go to Apple; and the rule of `◆`), the comment
  of `[channels]` in `defaults.toml`, the docstrings of `ChannelsConfig` and `notify/owner.py`, and the hint of the
  Channels tab's switch (Settings › Channels › iMessage: "…and tells you there what it has to say on its own: the
  morning brief and your watchers").

**Tests.** `tests/smoke/test_channel_tells.py`, on a real runtime and run manager with the real `Channels` over the
stand-ins of item 572 (`Osascript`, `FakeMacos`, a `chat.db` the test writes, here with a window of 0.8 s), each fails
without the change:
- a tell of the brief reaches a paired, switched-on iMessage once, marked `◆`, as *title: text* and the link; the row
  Messages keeps of it is not a command, and the message that follows it is the only run;
- the link that Messages may split off a tell, as a link preview and as plain words, is not a command either;
- the link opens at the name a phone reaches the Mac by (`[server] reach`) and is a route of Dot's pages;
- a channel that is off, or has no number paired, is told nothing, and what came while it was is not kept for later;
- a burst of four is the first tell, then one line "and 3 more" linking the newest, not before the window is over and
  nothing after; one tell inside the window goes as itself after it; tells of different titles are "Dot: and 2 more"; a
  tell after a quiet window goes at once;
- the line is masked of a secret Dot holds (written to the `.env` of the test's home);
- the tells held when the channel is switched off are let go, and the next one after it is on again is told alone;
- a line Messages refuses is not sent again and the next tell still goes.

**Found, and not changed here.** `as_plain_text` (item 572) takes `**…**` for bold, so a line that holds two masks

is kept; a mask the plain text leaves alone is a change of `channels/text.py` and its tests, apart from this item.

**Left for later.**

  (the brief and not the watchers) is not built.
- The held tells are in the router's memory: a daemon that stops with some held does not tell them (the phone's push and
  the page's notification have told them already).
- The tell of a run that a watcher started links to the Watchers chat's line, as it does for the push; a line that
  could not be kept links to Schedules.
