### 590. A loop's rhythm in words for the schedules a run made, and its next firing by its day

**Status: done (2026-10-23).** With item 580 live, the owner's three looping chats (a social media chat on Mondays,
Wednesdays and Fridays at 18:30, one at 09:00 and 19:00, a daily paper at 10:00) wore the mark with "Loops on its
schedule, next 10:00": the words knew only the rhythms `/loop` makes, and a schedule a run made keeps days and times.
The loop line over the composer said such a rhythm as `cron 0 10 * * *`.

**As built.**
- `rhythm.js` (pure, a module of its own): `loopRhythm` says the rhythms `/loop` makes as before ("every 30 minutes",
  "every hour", "every 2 hours") and a cron of plain times on days of the week in words: "every day at 10:00", "every
  day at 09:00 and 19:00", "on weekdays at 09:00", "on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays at 18:30", "at weekends at
  08:15"; another form (a day of the month, a month, a range of hours, day names) stays the daemon's words. The hours
  of a day and the days of a week (from Monday) are named in order, whatever order the cron gives them.
- `nextFiring` says the next firing by its time later today ("15:30"), by its day's name and time within the coming
  week ("Sat 10:00"), else by its date ("1 Nov 09:00"), so that a firing weeks away, or one past due while the daemon
  was off, does not read as the coming one; the mark said a bare time that could be tomorrow's.
- The sidebar's mark and the loop line over the composer read both; the loop line no longer says a cron expression for
  a rhythm of days and times.
- Two nits of the last review of #554, taken here: the API table of `docs/daemon.md` names a chat's `loop_cron` and
  `loop_next_at` (item 580), and the docstring of the Connectors server view says a server of no service wears Dot's
  icon (item 581); each is a commit of its item.
- `docs/daemon.md` and `docs/runs.md` say it.

**Tests.** `tests/js/rhythm.test.js`: the rhythms of `/loop`, the owner's three crons and the other day forms read as
above, the forms with no words give none, and the next firing reads by its day when it is not today.
`tests/smoke/test_chat_loop.py` reads a next firing that may fall on another day.

Not changed: the words say a cron's hours as it keeps them, in the schedule's zone, as `cron 0 10 * * *` did; a
schedule with a zone of its own other than the Mac's (rare: a run makes one in the chat's zone) reads its hours in that
zone, while its next firing is said in the browser's.
