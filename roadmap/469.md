### 469. A rule is tried on past steps before it is added

**Status: done (2026-10-21).** The sixth of the nine ideas taken from OpenBot (item 449 has the plan), its second part:
"try a rule on past steps before adding it". Until now a rule, written by hand or drafted from a sentence, was added
blind. **As built.** The design's preview (its "c1"). Its usage line on every rule ("c2": `GET /api/rules/usage`)
follows as its own item. The shadow state, a rule that only watches, is not built: it needs a fifth behaviour through
the policy, the drafting and the page, all on a human path, for what the history of the steps already shows of any tool
Dot has used. The design leaned on a refactor of `policy/engine.py` that the "strictest" pull request owns: the try-out
matches with the engine's own `match_rule`, read, never changed, so no human path is touched, and
`tests/unit/test_rule_replay.py` checks that the rule it finds deciding is the one `decide_past_review` picks, so that
the strictest change (a hand-off rule never shadowed, which the design's "a hand-off candidate wins" assumed) fails it
until the try-out follows. The store finds where the days start by halving the event ids (`first_event_id_since`; the
events have no index on the time and are never pruned by themselves, so no read walks the table) and reads the steps
(`tool_steps`: the `tool_result` events whose action reached the policy, newest first, with whether the event has a
decision field and whether its run was in yolo) by pages of 1000, at most 20,000 steps and two seconds, counting in
slices of 50 ms between which the runs at work go on; `partial` says when steps were left. `server/rule_replay.py`
counts what the rule fits; which of those an earlier rule decides first; the steps of yolo runs apart, as yolo asks
nothing whatever the rules say; the steps settled before any rule (the always-on review, a research run, Dot's own
files, money and credentials, the guard of messages and posts, a spend within its caps, the pace), but for a hand-off
rule over the guard or a spend, which the policy lets take their place; and, against the decision recorded since item
468, whether it would ask more, less or the same (an allow on a step the taint made a question stays a question), a step
the gate did not let through and one from before item 468 apart. It keeps the newest 20 steps it fits with their
summaries masked and cut; a pattern that takes longer than the daemon allows on a past step stops it with the problem
said, as the daemon would refuse the step. `POST /api/rules/preview` writes nothing; a pattern that is not one is 422 as
for a rule added. The form says it in a few lines (`rulepreview.js`) with the steps leading to their runs; a drafted
rule is tried at once, and a change of the rule, or its adding, puts the try-out away, an answer that comes after a
change being left unshown. Measured: 20,000 seeded steps in 0.24 s, a page of 1000 in 5 ms; on a copy of the owner's
store, the 8,199 steps of the last 30 days in 0.10 s, a page of 1000 in 7 ms. 7,881 of those steps ran in yolo, so a
try-out that counted them as decided would have told of questions no yolo run asks. Yolo is untouched: nothing a run
does or asks changes. Tests: `tests/unit/test_rule_replay.py` (fits, an earlier rule first, yolo and the checks before
the rules apart, the comparison with a hand-off, an allow and a pre-approval, the rule the policy picks, the newest
twenty, a slice ending at its deadline, a pattern that takes too long), `tests/smoke/test_rule_preview.py` (the store's
start of the days; the route on a seeded store of two runs, nothing written, a bad pattern and no days refused; the
Rules page at 1280 px light and 390 px dark as a touch screen: the lines and the steps, no "null", a change puts it
away, a step leads to its run), `tests/smoke/test_rule_preview_load.py` (20,000 steps read within the budget while the
daemon answers other requests), `tests/smoke/test_rules.py` (a drafted rule tried at once, put away once added; both
checked to fail without the change), `tests/js/rulepreview.test.js`. Docs: `docs/safety.md`, `docs/daemon.md`.
