### 743. A page too heavy for the snapshot still gives one

**Status: done (2026-10-27).** **Measured:** in the owner's runs of the last 30 days, `browser_open`,
`browser_snapshot` and `browser_wait_text` failed with "taking the page snapshot failed ... Page.aria_snapshot: Timeout
8000ms exceeded" on Slack, LinkedIn and a webmail: a run that had loaded the page could not see it.

**What Playwright gives.** `aria_snapshot` takes `depth`, but it cuts the output, not the walk of a wide page (measured:
a page of 15 000 lists read in about the same time at depth 1 as whole), and every snapshot replaces the ref table, so
two snapshots of two parts leave only the last one's refs. A fallback has to be one snapshot of one subtree.

**The change.** `driver.capture_page` (`capture_lines` before, which returned the lines alone) takes the snapshot as it
did; when it fails with a timeout, `_partial_snapshot` names a part with `_PARTIAL_ROOT_SCRIPT` (the window sampled on a
grid of 6 by 4 points, each point's element climbed to the biggest ancestor that holds at most 3 000 elements, the chunk
that most points fell in) as a selector path, and takes `locator.aria_snapshot` of it with the same timeout. Its lines
go through the same hiding of secret values and folding of options as a whole one's, and its refs are the ones that
work. `PageLines.partial` carries a sentence that the session puts in the view's notes: it says that the view is
partial, what part (its tag and size), and to use `browser_find` or `browser_read_text` for the rest. A partial view
is never shown as a change against the view before it. A page for which no part smaller than the page can be named,
or whose part cannot be read either, fails as before, with the first timeout. A failure that is not a timeout is not
made a partial view. The checks of the tools (the URL rules held either side of the read, untrusted marking) are the
read's own and unchanged. `browser_find`, `browser_links` and `browser_read_text` read the same lines, so on such a
page they search the part; their results do not say so.

**Checked.** `tests/unit/test_snapshot_partial.py` (a stand-in page: the whole times out, the part is read through a
locator; no part named, and a failure that is not a timeout, fail as before) and
`tests/smoke/test_browser_partial_snapshot.py` in a real headless Chrome (a page of 4 000 items whose whole snapshot is
made to time out: the part is read with the real script and locator, says it is partial, and a ref of it answers to
`aria-ref=`; a page that can be read is read whole with no note). The Slack and LinkedIn pages themselves were not
measured.
