### 603. The first dictation on an iPhone hears, and leaves no microphone on

**Status: done (2026-10-24).** The owner pressed the composer's microphone on the iPhone for the first time, over
Tailscale, and allowed what Safari asked. The page said "Nothing was heard…" and froze. Closed and opened again, it asked
for the microphone once more, and with that allowed the dictation worked.

**Why.** `createDictation` (`static/js/voice.js`, item 574) armed its six-second silence timer as soon as it called the
engine's `start()`. The first time, an iPhone asks twice before the engine listens: Safari's speech recognition, then
the page's microphone. The owner read both questions for longer than six seconds, so the timer stopped an engine that
had not begun. It ended at once, the dictation said it had heard nothing (item 593's words), and Safari, once allowed,
started the engine all the same, for nobody: no handler of the dictation's own took its words any more, and nothing
stopped it. That is the likeliest cause of the freeze, with the microphone held open. The second time, Safari asked only
for the microphone, and the owner was quicker than the timer.

**As built.**

- The silence is counted from the engine's `start`, or its `audiostart`, or the first word it hears, whichever comes
  first, never from the press. While the browser asks, the button shows the microphone on and nothing is timed.
- A second press before the engine began drops the dictation (`abort()`): there is nothing to settle, and an engine
  that has not begun is not asked to stop. An engine the dictation no longer holds that begins or hears anyway is
  aborted, so the microphone is not left on, and its words never reach the box.
- An engine that ends before it began says nothing of a silence: its error, a refusal for one, says why.
- `docs/daemon.md` (the composer's microphone) says it.

**Tests.** `tests/js/dictation.test.js` (+4), with an engine and a clock of the test's own on a phone's page:
- twenty seconds of questions stop nothing, and six seconds after `start` the engine is stopped and the silence named;
- a second press while the browser asks aborts, and an engine started afterwards is aborted again and writes nothing;
- an end before the start says nothing, and a refusal says why;
- `audiostart` or a word heard counts as begun, and a second press then settles what was heard.

Three of the four fail against the code before (checked). `npm run check` and the browser tests of the voice
(`tests/smoke/test_voice.py`, 14) pass. Not tried on an iPhone here: the owner's next first dictation is the test.
