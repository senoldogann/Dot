### 1150. Dot answers in the call, as Claude Meetings does

**Status: done (2026-11-13).** Claude Meetings (Anthropic, beta 2026-11-10) joins a Google Meet call as a visible
participant, posts a notice in the call's chat, follows the talk, and answers in writing whoever asks it, without
asking anyone first. Dot took notes (item 1138) but answered nothing: each post waited on the owner's yes, the notes
came every 5 minutes, and a line was read at most every 2 s after it stood still 2.5 s. The owner asked for the same,
and better. This item is the first part: the answers, the notice, and the pace.

What holds:

- **An answer is written by a model with no tool and nothing of the owner's**: the call's own lines (fenced as data),
  the owner's name and Dot's. Not memory, files, mail, the calendar, the vault, any task, nor the notes. All it can put
  in the call is what was said there and what the model knows of the world. It is posted without asking, in every
  mode. A task's `meeting_post` still asks the owner with its words whole, every time: a task holds the owner's tools.
- **In code, not in the prompt**: one line of at most 400 characters; no link, web or mail address; none that speaks to
  Dot. One answer at a time, one question waiting per person and three in all, the same words of the same person
  within a minute are one question, a question that waited a minute is dropped, at most 6 questions a minute and 60 a
  meeting go to the model, counted when the call starts (an empty or refused answer counts too). Dot's own messages
  ("You" in Meet) and its answers are never questions. Each answer is a line of the meeting (kind `dot`).
- **Which line is a question** is code: the name among a line's first two words after greetings, the last word of a
  line of three or more (not after "to", "ask" and the like, unless a comma comes before it: "let's ask Dot" is not,
  "what is it like, Dot?" is), `@Dot` in the chat, and the next line within 15 s of someone who only said the name.
  "Dot com", "the dot", "Dot'un notları" and a name further in are not. The answerer may give no answer, and nothing is posted.
- **The notice** goes up once Dot is let in, without the owner's yes, and says that Dot is an AI whose answers are not
  the owner's word. A join that is not the owner's own word (the task or its chat read outside content, or Dot opened
  the chat) asks the owner first, in every mode, free mode too.
- **Pace**: the page is read every 0.5 s in one call into it (Leave call, captions, chat box and the buffer; the whole
  page's text only when Leave call is gone); the chat is opened again when found closed; `settle_s` 1.5 (was 2.5);
  `notes_minutes` 3 (was 5); the meeting's Chrome is not slowed down behind other windows.
- **Off by one setting**: `[meetings] answers = false`.

Reviewed before the code by two independent agents (a red team and a practicality review). Taken from them:

- The red team found that masking the answerer's words and answer by the values Dot holds (as the note maker does)
  would tell the call which of its guesses is one of the owner's passwords: the answerer masks by the forms of secrets
  only, and does not read the notes, which are masked by values.
- It found that without the owner's yes on the notice, a task that read a mail with a Meet link would join an attacker's
  call unasked in yolo and free mode: the question moved to such a join, in every mode.
- It asked for caps per minute and per meeting, no answer that speaks to Dot (two bots answering each other), no links
  in answers, a notice that says the answers are not the owner's word, a lock so that an answer and a task's post do
  not type at once, and that a failed answer never ends the meeting. All taken.
- The practicality review asked for the name at the end of a line, the line after a bare "Hey Dot", the exclusions of
  "dot com" and "the dot", dropping the same question said twice, one read of the page per tick, the chat kept open,
  and notes every 3 minutes rather than 1 (about five times the model's cost for a page that does not exist yet). All
  taken.
- The pre-push review found that the caps counted posted answers, so empty, refused and failed ones were free model
  calls; they count calls now. It also asked that free mode's one new question (a join not the owner's word) be in
  CLAUDE.md and docs/daemon.md, that an answer that broke in an unexpected way be logged, and that "Node.js" not be
  taken for a domain. All taken.
- Its second pass found that a name at the end of a line that speaks of Dot ("let's ask Dot") was taken for a
  question: excluded. It also noted that a task that read outside content, in a mode whose gate asks for its join, is
  asked twice (the gate's `external_send` and the desk's question). Kept: taking the gate's question away would let a
  join through unasked in the strict modes, and the desk's is the one that holds in every mode.
- Its review of the whole branch asked that the README line say what Dot does now, that the join question name its
  real reason (outside content, or a chat Dot opened), and that a spoken question cut by a pause not be answered by
  half. The first two were taken. The third was tried with a 2 s wait after a caption line, and its next review found
  that a line's time is when it settles, not when it was said, so the wait almost never joined the rest: after the
  branch's third block it was taken out (CLAUDE.md: split the change), and is the next item, built on whether the
  speaker's caption is still open, with a test through the caption folding. Until then such a question is answered by
  its first half.
- Its next pass found that the link check knew some 55 domain endings only, so "meet-google.cc/join", a host written
  with an ideographic dot and a bare IP address got through: any word.word with a last part of two letters or more is
  refused now, but a few file endings that are no domain's, the other dots are read as dots, and IPv4 is caught. It
  also found that a host who turns writing off leaves no message box, so Dot would press the chat button and close the
  panel: the chat counts as open when its messages show too. Both taken.
- Left for later, with their reasons: a per-answer prefix ("Dot (AI): …"; the notice says it once, and Dot posts as its
  own account); Meet's caption language as a setting (the selectors of Meet's caption settings cannot be checked
  without the real page); logging near misses of the name.
