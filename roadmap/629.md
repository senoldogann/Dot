### 629. A rule drafted from words is bounded where the engine that matches it sees a word

**Status: done (2026-10-24); needs the owner's look (`src/dotagent/policy/`).**

**The defect.** Item 622's agent found it while making the Rules page read a pattern exactly. Its own page's word
characters are held to the engine code point by code point (`tests/unit/test_rule_words.py`).
- `policy/drafting.py` writes the pattern of a rule from the drafted mentions. A mention that starts or ends with a
  word character gets a word boundary (`\b`) there, so that `x.com` does not fit `netflix.com`.
- It told a word character with Python's `re` (`_WORD = re.compile(r"\w")`). The engine that matches a rule
  (`policy.engine`, `rule_fits`) is the `regex` module, and the two disagree:
  - `re` takes `²` and `½` for word characters, `regex` does not. A rule that stops Dot at `x²` was written
    `\bx²\b`, and the boundary after `²`, between two characters that are no word characters to the engine, never
    fits: the rule stopped Dot at nothing.
  - `regex` takes a circled letter (`ⓐ`), a vowel sign (`ा`), a connector (`‿`) and a joiner for word characters,
    `re` does not. The pattern had no boundary there and fitted inside a longer word: wider than the rule says, which
    for a rule that lets Dot act is more than the owner allowed.

**The fix.** `_WORD` is `regex.compile(r"\w")`, the engine's own idea of a word character, for the edges. Whether a
part holds a word at all ("a command or a mention needs a word to match on") is told by a letter, a number or `_`
(`_HAS_WORD`): the engine's `\w` also takes a joiner, a variation selector and a combining mark, and a mention of
those alone stays refused as it was. A mention of `½` alone, refused before as nothing, is a rule now (it holds a
number) and fits "Write ½ now". Nothing else in what the policy decides changes. `docs/safety.md` says the edges are
told as the engine tells them.

**What it narrows.** A mention that ends in a mark (a Thai or Hindi vowel sign) now has a boundary there, as any word
has. A rule that stops Dot at it fitted the word inside running text before, by accident of `re`; it fits it standing
apart now. In a script written without spaces that is less cover for such a rule, and `docs/safety.md` and the
changes entry say so.

**Tests.** `tests/unit/test_rule_drafting.py`, against the real engine (`match_rule`):
- a rule that stops Dot at `x²` fits "Write the sum of x² and y", and `½ cup` fits its line;
- `ⓐbc` fits "ⓐbc" and not "xⓐbc";
- `का` fits "का" and not "काम".
- Four of the six fail against the drafter before (the two that pass are the twins). The drafter's tests and those of
  the page's reading (`test_rule_words.py`) pass.
- A mention of a joiner, a variation selector or a combining mark alone is refused, and one of `½` alone makes a rule
  that fits "Write ½ now".

**The independent review** (security, as `src/dotagent/policy/` asks) found nothing Important. It swept 30,757
characters and 60,000 random mentions against an oracle of its own: the new patterns deviate in none. Its two nits are
taken:
- the guard took invisible characters for a word, now `_HAS_WORD`;
- the narrowing above went unsaid, and two places still said "a letter or a digit".
