### 1722. A call Dot's own rule turns another way is recorded as steered, not failed

**Status: done (2026-11-24).** The audit of 2026-11-24 (A3, improvement 1) measured `terminal_open` at 24.6 % errors
and `delegate` at 24.7 % in 14 days, most of them Dot's own guidance rather than failures: 22 of 32 `terminal_open`
errors were "a command in the background is told to its chat when it ends, and a delegated run ... has none", and 14
`delegate` and `create_agent` errors were "the team may have an agent for this" (item 950). Every health number that
counts `"ok": false` (the tool health table of `dotx stats --by tool`, the run ledger, the self-improvement loop's tool
outcomes, an audit of the store) counted them as failures.

What changed (`core/errors.py`, `tools/base.py`, `agent/executor.py`, `tools/terminal.py`, `tools/delegate.py`):

- `ToolSteer`, a `ToolInputError`: a call Dot's own rule turns another way. The two refusals above raise it; every other
  refusal stays an error (the second `terminal_open` refusal, of a process whose chats hear of no command's end, too).
- `ToolResult.steered`, set by the executor for a `ToolSteer`, and one classification, `executor.failed_call(result)`:
  an error that is not steered. The model reads a steered result as an error, since the call did nothing; its
  `tool_result` event is `"ok": true, "steered": true`, and no lessons of failures are handed for it. What reads
  `"ok": true` as "the call did its job" reads it for `finish` and `send_to_chat`, which are never steered.

Checked: `tests/smoke/test_terminal.py` (the delegated run's background command raises `ToolSteer`; through
`execute_calls` its result is an error the model reads, recorded `ok: true, steered: true`, while the other refusal is
recorded `ok: false, steered: false`), `tests/unit/test_delegate_team.py` (the team refusal is a `ToolSteer`),
`dotx eval check`, `tests/unit/test_freedom_contract.py`.
Not done: earlier events keep `"ok": false`; the 14-day rates fall only as new runs come. The timeline draws a steered
call as one that did not fail, with the words that say what to do instead.
