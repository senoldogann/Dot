### 984. The partial snapshot's test holds the page for a known time

**Status: done (2026-11-05).** **Found** by the Linear notification of 08:18 for `senoldogann/Dot#785`: the check
`browser (pages)` failed on `test_a_whole_snapshot_that_really_times_out_still_gives_a_part` with

```
browser_partial_snapshot_failed error=timeout url=about:blank
BrowserError: taking the page snapshot failed | context={"attempts": "1", "url": "about:blank",
  "error": "Page.aria_snapshot: Timeout 2887ms exceeded."}
```

A pull request of five items on the composer's panel, the office tools and the web UI touches nothing under
`src/dotagent/browser/`: the failure is not the change's.

**Root cause.** The test (written for item 923) measured how long a whole snapshot of its 60 000-link page took, then gave
`capture_page` half that time, so that the snapshot times out for real and the page stays busy behind it; the naming of
the part waits twice the time it is given, which is 1.5 times the measured whole. Measured on this Mac, four whole
snapshots of that page took 2 510, 2 050, 1 981 and 1 876 ms: the page's own time swings by a quarter from run to run,
and the gate's five jobs share one runner, so the run that timed out took more than 1.5 times the one measured and the
wait ran out. This is the second time the same bound lost a pull request (#756's CI, 2026-11-02, when it was a third).
Raising the ratio does not fix it: the test needs the snapshot to time out (`timeout <` the whole) and the wait to hold
(the whole `<` three times the timeout) out of the *same* measurement, a window one loaded run wide. Two thirds of a
twice-measured whole was tried first and failed here too, the other way: the whole then finished inside its own timeout
and there was no part at all.

**The change.** The test no longer times the page. It holds the page's main thread for a known 7 s (`_HELD_SCRIPT`, a
busy loop that says `held` on the console first, so that the hold has the page before the read asks anything of it), and
gives the snapshot 3 s: the snapshot times out for real, and the page is still busy when it does. The page is the light
one of the test above, because what waits behind the hold is the snapshot that timed out, which runs on to its end: a
page whose own snapshot takes seconds would put the wait at its bound again. That was the first shape of this change and
the pre-push review was right to stop it (7 s of hold plus 2 s of 60 000 links, against a bound of 3 s + 2 x 3 s). Now
the page is free at about 7.2 s and the naming waits until 9 s. The old wait of the whole's own time fails the test (3 s
+ 3 s against a 7 s hold), so item 923 stays guarded. The hold is awaited in a `finally`, so a read that gives up leaves
the page free for the next test rather than a task that raises when the browser closes.

**Checked.** `tests/smoke/test_browser_partial_snapshot.py` in a real headless Chrome on this Mac: both tests green four
times running (the timed-out read 7.2 s, against 16 s when the test timed the page itself), green again under six busy
loops on the cores, and with `_PARTIAL_WAIT_TIMES = 1` the test fails at once with the clean `BrowserError` of item 923's
report. `ruff format --check`, `ruff check` and `mypy` on the file.
