### 1522. The page's streams drop a connection that answers nothing in 8 seconds and a stream that says nothing in 35, and go on from the last event

**Status: done in part by what was there, and in part by this item (2026-11-24).** The brief took the live stream for an
`EventSource`, which cannot see the daemon's `: keepalive` comment, so that a dead socket is waited for. **That was not so:**
`api.js` already read every stream with `fetch` and a stream reader (`sse`), cut one that said nothing for 45 s (`STALL_MS`),
cut one at once when the page came back into view after being put away, followed again from the last event with `after=`
(`follow`) and made it again at once on `online` and when the page was in view (`untilRetry`), after 0.5, 1, 2, 4 and 8 s. The
iPhone app's rules (item 1391) differ from that in four places, and those are what changed.

What changed:

- `js/sseframes.js` (pure, `tests/js/sseframes.test.js`, 18 tests): the parser of the stream's text, which was a loop in
  `api.js` that cut at `\n\n` and threw on a frame whose data was not JSON (the stream was then made again from the same
  event, for ever). It takes chunks cut anywhere (every split point of a stream, one character at a time, CRLF cut between
  two chunks, a CR held to the end of the stream), comments, `data` over several lines, ids (0 for a frame with none, as the
  page's live pieces have), the event name, a byte order mark, and a frame cut by a drop (`pending()`: it is no event); data
  that is not JSON is a frame told of and skipped. The times: 8 s to connect, 35 s of silence, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8 and then 15 s
  between tries.
- `js/api.js`: `sse` reads with the parser, drops a connection that has answered nothing in 8 s (`CONNECT_TIMEOUT_MS`) and a
  stream that has said nothing for 35 s (`IDLE_TIMEOUT_MS`; 45 s before: the daemon's keepalive is every 15 s, so two missed
  and a little more), and sends `Last-Event-ID` beside `after`; `follow` and `watchInView` wait as `reconnectWait` says
  (15 s at the longest, 8 s before). A chat's stored steps, read once when it opens, keep the 45 s (`ONE_SHOT`): they have
  no stream to go on after, and a slow connection must not make the chat unreadable.
- The consumers are as they were: the events come in the shape they had (`{ id, event, data }`), a drop shows the note and the
  pill after the quiet time they already had (`QUIET_BREAK_MS`, `RETURN_GRACE_MS`), not at once.

Checked: `tests/js/sseframes.test.js`; `tests/smoke/test_phone_stream.py` (a connection left open that passes nothing, as a
phone's network leaves one: 34 seconds of the page's clock later it is still the one stream, 36 later it is dropped and made
again, with `after=<last id>` and the same `Last-Event-ID`, and no history is read again); `tests/smoke/test_reconnect.py` and
`tests/smoke/test_takeover.py` (the older tests of a stream gone silent, of a page back in view and of a daemon that
restarts, whose comments named `STALL_MS`; `test_reconnect.py`'s page now has no IndexedDB, because a page that has copies
of its chats opens on them instead of on the screen of a daemon out of reach, which is what its three tests of that screen
were about); `npm run check`.
Not verified: a real phone's network: the 8 s and 35 s are the iPhone app's numbers and were not measured on a bad link; the
Watch screencast's stream is cut at 35 s of silence like the others, and the daemon writes a keepalive into it when it has no
frame, but how often a frame-less screencast writes one was not read here.
