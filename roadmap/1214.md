### 1214. `watch_video` takes a frame in every scene, so a short slide is not missed

**Status: done (2026-11-17).** The frames of item 1212 were spread evenly over the part, so a slide or a shot that
lasts a few seconds between two frames was never seen: on a test deck, four frames over 80 s fell at 10, 30, 50 and
70 s and missed the slide of 20-23 s.

- `video/media.py`: `scene_cuts` asks ffmpeg for the moments the picture changes (`select='gt(scene,0.3)'` on four
  small frames a second, printed with `metadata=print`), decoding on the Mac's own decoder (`-hwaccel videotoolbox`).
  Measured on a 3:33 video: 0.8 s of the processor against 15 s for the software decoder (4.2 s of wall time against
  2.6, for six cores less). Looking only at keyframes was 30 times quicker but missed two of a deck's five changes;
  every frame found all five, at their moments.
- `video/watch.py`: `scenes_of` (the parts between changes, a flash under a second being none) and `frame_plan`: one
  frame in the middle of each scene, the frames left to the longest scenes in equal slices, at most one a second of
  the time the scenes cover; more scenes than frames, none, or scenes over less than half the part (quick cuts)
  spread them evenly as before. The plan says which, and `frame_plan_line` tells the model.
- `tools/video.py`: the result says how the frames were chosen.

The pre-push review found that frames were counted over the whole part where flashes were dropped, that a minute of
quick cuts with one scene of 1.5 s got one frame, and that the line the model reads did not know the frames had been
spread for that reason: the plan now carries how it chose, and the line is written from it.

Checked: `tests/unit/test_video_watch.py` (scenes, frames per scene, the even spread), `tests/smoke/test_watch_video.py`
(a real deck whose three-second slide is now seen), and a real music video (35 scenes over 3:33: spread evenly; 6 in
20 s: each seen), which took 3.7 s longer to look at than before.
