### 565. Settings › Rules and Phone follow the design

**Status: done (2026-10-23).** The owner's redraw of the settings (a PDF of eight screens in Dot's Paper theme, the
frame of which is item 562) drew Rules and Phone; this item builds the two on that frame. What the design leaves out
stays, drawn in the same look; nothing the daemon does not say is shown.

**As built.**
- `views/rules.js`: the head says what rules are for and has **New rule** at its right (it opens and shuts the two ways
  to write a rule, now a section under the head: the rule in your words, and the rule part by part with its try-out, one
  card of two rows; Close sits in the form's buttons). Above the list: the protections that are always on in a card of
  their own (a lock, four checked lines in two columns and what a yolo run does; the facts of the note it replaces,
  nothing added), then **Commands**, the sandbox of a run's commands with its whole explanation, its key `[shell]
  sandbox` and its switch. **Your rules** is one card of hairline-parted rows (the switch, `#n`, the sentence, the
  answer's badge, *Never decides*, the why, how the rule was used on the last days, the arrows and the delete, all as
  before), and the head says how many rules there are. With no rule the card is the shield, *No rules yet*, what the
  preset does meanwhile and the three sentences to start from: one opens the ways to write a rule with the sentence in
  the box (the drafter turns each into a rule: a command or two of the shell, a command that stops Dot, every step on
  one site); the form's own examples show once there is a rule. Under the rules, with any or none, **What a rule can
  answer** tells the four answers by their badges and what Dot does then, from the one list the form's hint and a rule's
  tooltip read too; the design's words, checked against `docs/safety.md`, with the hand-off's tail kept (when you say
  you did it, the run goes on). The answer that hands the step to you is blue, as the design draws it.
- `views/phone.js`: the head says what the tab is for. The first card says whether the phone reaches Dot (**Ready: your
  phone reaches Dot** and the name, on a green tile; while a step is left, how many of the three are done, in words and
  in a track of three, on a tile in the accent) and holds the three steps on this Mac as cells side by side (a tick, the
  accent number of the step to do now or a grey one; each cell says where it stands and offers what does it: Get or Open
  Tailscale, Set up, Turn on HTTPS, Restart Dot, what Tailscale said), then the switch that keeps the Mac awake. **Set
  up a phone** is one card in two columns: the phone's four steps, and one QR code with the choice **Tailscale app** or
  **Open Dot** (Dot's address once Dot answers to a name, else Tailscale's page, and the choice kept through the page's
  rereads), the address in mono with **Copy** (every address of `[server] reach`) or, with none, the note that says
  where it comes from. The step marked is the one whose code is shown, the ticks the ones before it, and the last once a
  pairing code is shown: the guide's own place, nothing the daemon would have to say of a phone. **Sign-in code** keeps
  eight dots until **Show code** makes the code (as **Pair a phone** did), with how long it is good; the button then
  reads **New code**. That row is drawn once and stays through the guide's rereads of Tailscale.
- `settings.css`: the Rules and Phone blocks drawn anew on the `.set-*` pieces and Dot's tokens; the CSS the redraw made
  dead (the steps' counters and connecting lines, the progress bar, the QR row, the rules' panel head and note) is gone.
- `docs/phone.md` and `docs/safety.md` say it.

**Tests.** `test_rules.py`: the empty card offers the three sentences (one opens the form with it in the box), says what
a rule can answer, counts the rules in the head and follows a rule added or deleted elsewhere, wide and on a phone; the
test of a rule in plain words follows the head's New rule and the empty card's sentence. `test_phone_reach.py`: the
choice of the QR code (Tailscale's page, the note when Dot has no address) and the step the guide is at;
`test_phone_pairing.py`: the code is hidden until **Show code**, then on the last step with **New code**. The tests of
the sandbox, the previews, the usage lines, the hand-off, the rules page's live reads and the phone's text size and
touch targets pass on the new DOM.

Not built: a "done" state of the phone's steps from the daemon (it cannot know what a phone has installed); the design's
sample lines ("Checked just now.", "Served over HTTPS; the config names this Mac.") are the lines the steps already
said.
