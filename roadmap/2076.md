### 2076. A page opened from the tab a login was filled into opens in a new tab in its place

**Status: done (2026-11-28).** Dot's record held `browser_eval: a login from the password manager was filled into tab
<n> of this chat` four times, in four runs of 2026-11-23 to 2026-11-25 (a job-application schedule, webmail, LinkedIn,
Google). In three of them the run had filled a login, signed in, opened another page in the same tab with
`browser_open`, and then run a script; in the fourth the filled tab was one it had left behind. Each time the refusal
was the one item 493 made: no script runs in the chat's tabs while a tab a login was filled into is open, since going
back can bring the filled page back whole. And each time the run did the next call what the refusal says: closed the
tab by its number and opened the page again, where the sign-in held and the script ran. The refusal is right; the
step was one Dot could take itself.

What changed (`browser/session.py`): `open_url` on a tab a login was filled into closes that tab and opens the page in
a new tab in its place, at its number, with a note that says so (`_replace_held`). The filled page is then in no tab's
history, and scripts run again in the chat's tabs once no other tab a login was filled into is open. `open_url` reads
the rules on the address before it touches a tab, as `new_tab` does, so that an address they refuse leaves the filled
tab as it was. What the new tab does not have is what a tab the run closed and opened again did not have either: what
Dot holds by the tab (the values kept out of the addresses its pages send, the fields marked again) is the filled
tab's and closes with it; the values stay the chat's to mask. A tab a login was
filled into that the run left behind still keeps scripts off until it is closed, and a page that tab reaches by a click
or a form stays in it. docs/safety.md says it.

How it was checked: `tests/smoke/test_vault_fill.py::test_a_page_opened_from_the_filled_tab_opens_in_a_new_tab_where_scripts_run`
(the filled tab the chat's only one, the new tab's history that of a tab opened at the page) and
`::test_another_filled_tab_keeps_scripts_off_and_a_refused_address_leaves_the_filled_tab` fail without the change and
pass with it, the second also without the early read of the rules; `tests/smoke/test_vault_items_fill.py` (its test
of a page of another origin reads the page that took the filled tab's place), `tests/smoke/test_vault_fill.py`,
`tests/smoke/test_browser_page_steps.py`, `tests/unit/test_freedom_contract.py`, ruff and mypy pass.
