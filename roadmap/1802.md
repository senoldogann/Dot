### 1802. DotNext's voice typing: the composer becomes a recorder; Settings › Voice

**Status: done (2026-11-25).** The owner's shots (PLAN §24 `ref-voice-recording.png`, §25 `ref-voice-settings.png`):
the microphone turns the composer into a recorder (✕, a live waveform, a running clock, ✓), the words go into the
field; it must work on a real iPhone (permissions, a call, the background, a long dictation, no deafness after one go).
Settings › Voice: transcribe on this phone or the paired Mac; the preferred order of the microphones.

Before: the microphone dictated into the field with Apple's recognizer on the phone, ending at the recognizer's first
pause; no waveform, no clock, no choice of microphone or of where it is written down.

Which way to write down: the old app wrote down on the phone (Speech, `requiresOnDeviceRecognition`); the web records
on the phone and the Mac writes down (items 626, 969), because Safari's recognizer went deaf after one dictation. Native
Speech does not have Safari's fault when each go starts clean, and works with no link, so it is the default; the Mac's
is the owner's choice (it also takes languages the phone has no model for).

What was built:
- `Data/VoiceInput.swift`: each go a fresh audio engine, recognizer and request; the audio session given back at its
  end. On the phone a segment the recognizer closes on a pause is kept and a new one begins, so a long dictation goes
  on; on the Mac the sound streams in half-second pieces of 16 kHz samples in order (`/api/voice/streams`), ended by ✓,
  dropped by ✕, ended a little before the Mac's five minutes. A call or Siri (an interruption), the app leaving the
  screen, a reset of the media services end the go with what was heard, said in a line; a microphone plugged in or out
  is taken up again. The first connected microphone of the owner's order is preferred; a wired or Bluetooth one is
  learned by name on its first go.
- `Components/VoiceRecorder.swift`: the recorder in the composer's pill, the words heard so far above the waveform,
  haptics as it starts and ends.
- `Settings/VoicePage.swift` (Settings › Voice): Transcribe on (this device, or the Mac by name and address, with the
  Mac's reason when it cannot), the dictation language, the preferred order (drag), a named microphone forgotten.
- `Data/VoiceRules.swift`: the pure rules (the microphone taken, learned, forgotten; the clock; levels), tested.
- DotEngine `DaemonAPI+Voice.swift`: the voice routes and `voicePieces`, tested on the fake daemon.

Not verified here: a real iPhone (a call, AirPods, CarPlay) — the simulator has no call and no Bluetooth; the owner's
check on the phone. The usage text of the microphone now says the Mac may write it down.
