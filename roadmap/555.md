### 555. Computer use judges what a step touches: terminals reached through buttons and menus, a step approved after a wait, Return on a focused button, a failed hit test, Messages' newest codes

**Status: done (2026-10-23).** From the deep audit of 2026-10-23 and the second, independent audit that followed it: the
findings F9, F10, F19, F29, F30, F31, F32, F91, F92 and F93, and X1 and X2, all in what the desktop tools judge, read
or do, fixed together because they stand in the same files.

**What must hold, and what only asks.** Nothing here can go in the kernel: the sandbox seals the processes of a run, and
a step of the desktop tools is another application acting with its own rights, which no profile of Dot's holds back. So
everything below only asks, by what the guard can read, and the docs say what it does not read
(`docs/browser-and-computer-use.md`). What does hold is that a step the owner approved goes where they approved it
(F29) and that a click on a control nobody read does not go as a click on nothing (X1).

**As built.**
- F9 (a terminal run through buttons, menus and fields, and a shell inside an editor): in a terminal application every
  step that may run a command other than by the text and keys of its line (a press or a click, a menu item that is no
  quit, a drag, a `set_value`) is judged as the Return of a line Dot cannot tell, the verdict the shell guard gives
  unread code (high, destructive outside the workspace), which every preset asks about, and is a `shell` step, so a
  batch that was approved as another kind asks again as it reaches one. `hover`, `scroll`, `focus`, `activate` and
  Quit stay as they were. Editors with a terminal and applications that run scripts (VS Code, Cursor, Windsurf,
  VSCodium, Zed, the JetBrains IDEs, Script Editor, Automator, Shortcuts) are a role of their own, `shell_host`, not a
  terminal: typing code in them is no shell line. What asks there, as a `privilege` step, is what runs: a line break
  typed or pasted, Return, F5, Cmd-R, a button or a menu item named run, play, execute or debug. `safety.py` has
  `reaches_a_shell`, `_runs_unread` and `_runs_in_host`; the terminal's own judgement of typed text and keys moved from
  `_typing_parts` and `_chord_parts` to `_shell_parts`, unchanged.
- F91 (the card says nothing of the command): `safety.shell_input` gives the lines a step submits, typed from the
  line Dot knows (a line it cannot tell is `(a line Dot cannot read)`), key-like text masked (`redact_secrets`); the
  card of a `shell` step, at the batch's question and at a step asked again, has them as its target, which the web UI
  labels Command, and what the judgement found after its summary.
- F92: the comment on the Chrome grant says what the docs say, refused in every mode but yolo (item 433).
- F29 (a step approved after a wait went to whatever stood there): `execute_step` takes the inspection the review
  judged and does not perform the step when the fresh one differs in the application (its pid and name), the kind of
  window, the control's role or name, a secure field or the default button; the step fails and says what it was to
  touch and what it would touch.
- F32 (the desktop held while the owner decides): kept held, since a hand-off (a password they type, a payment they
  press) is done by them on this screen and the gate, not the session, decides whether a step is one (an owner's rule
  can hand off any). A run that asks for the desktop meanwhile is told at once, `DesktopSession._turn` polling the
  lock so that one queued before the wait began is told as well, instead of queueing until its tool times out (a
  batch's preview has no timeout at all).
- F30 (Dot's window found by the window server's title): a click at coordinates takes the title from the window of the
  control accessibility finds there (`ax.window_of`), then the window server's, then the owner's focused window.
- X1 (a failed hit test judged as a click on nothing): `_hit_test` says that it failed (`_Hit.failed`), and
  `_peek_point` refuses the step with words that say to take a snapshot (which wakes the accessibility of an
  application that builds it on demand) and press or click by ref. Nothing at the point, and an application that has
  nothing to say there (no such attribute, accessibility not implemented, no value: codes -25205, -25208, -25212), is
  still a click on nothing: coordinates are for what accessibility does not show. An application that blocks
  accessibility outright (it answers -25204 whatever it is asked) gets no coordinate click either; the step says why.
- X2 (Return or Space on a focused button): a `key` step of Return, Enter or Space judges the control that has the
  keyboard by its name as a click does, and Return the default button of the window or sheet too
  (`ax.default_button_of`, read live for a step that presses Return, carried as `StepContext.default_button`). A `type`
  text with a line break, which presses Return, is judged the same way.
- F10 (`code_fetch` missed the newest message): `chat.db` is opened with `mode=ro` and no `immutable=1`, so SQLite
  reads the write-ahead log where a message that has just arrived waits.
- F31 (screenshots blacked out a password manager by its localized name): `hidden_regions` knows a window by the bundle
  id of its application too (`apps.bundle_ids`, one `lsappinfo` call), at `desktop_screenshot` and at the capture of
  the window in front.
- F19 (the AppleScript tool's `osascript` inherited the daemon's environment): `run_osascript` takes an `env`, and the
  tool gives it `ctx.runtime.shell_env`, which the shell tool's commands have. Dot's own scripts (the mail of a code, a
  draft) keep the daemon's environment: they run a script of Dot's, never one a run wrote.
- F93 (the clipboard lost when the paste failed): `paste_text` puts the clipboard back in a `finally`, when it still
  holds what the paste put there.

**Left, and why.**
- F9: a Cmd shortcut in a terminal stays a key that leaves the line unknown, with the next Return asking, as before:
  what then runs a command is a Return or a button, which ask. An editor or an application that runs what it is given
  and is not in the list is not read, nor a button that runs under another name than run, play, execute or debug, nor
  a line of the clipboard pasted into a program that does not wait for Return; the docs say so. A list of names is
  what the guard can read, and each name added closes one and leaves the next (items 531 and 534).
- F32: the desktop is not lent to other runs while a step waits. Lending it only for the questions that are not
  hand-offs needs the gate's decision before it waits, which the session cannot know.
- F10: no copy of the database as a fallback when the shared memory file cannot be used: the folder is the owner's to
  write, Messages keeps the file open, and a copy of a database of gigabytes for every poll is a worse cost than the
  error `could not be read`, which says it. `dotx doctor` still opens the database with `immutable=1`: it only asks
  whether the file opens, and `doctor.py` is outside this item's files.
- X1: a ref click's check that nothing covers the point (`_check_uncovered`) still lets a click go when its own hit test
  fails: it looks for a window of another application over a control Dot just read, and a failure there is not a
  control nobody read.

**Tests.** `tests/unit/test_desktop_terminal_steps.py`: a press, a click, a drag, a menu item and a `set_value` in a
terminal ask in every preset and a hover, a scroll, a focus, a wait, an activate and a Quit do not; the same steps in
another application are as before; a line typed in parts is still judged whole; the editors and script applications are
known by bundle id, JetBrains' prefix and name, and ask on a line break, Return, F5, Cmd-R and a run-named button or
menu item, in two of them, while code typed, Cmd-S, Tab, an Explorer button and File › Save do not; Return, Enter and
Space on a Pay, a Delete or a default Send ask as the click does, and Return in a field, with Shift, without a default
button and a text without a line break do not; the lines a card shows, with a key masked and a line Dot cannot read
named; and the cards of the batch and of a step asked again through the tool with a fake session.
`tests/unit/test_desktop_review.py`: Dot's window behind the focused one is Dot's by the title accessibility gives, in
a window with no title from the window server too; a hit test that fails refuses the click, and nothing under the point
or an application with nothing to say there does not; a Return, an Enter or a typed line break reads the default button
of the sheet or window the keyboard is in, as a Pay it presses is judged, and no other key does; a step whose pid, role,
application, control, secure flag or default button changed does not go, and one whose terminal line differs does; the
batch goes for the inspection that was reviewed; a run that asks for the desktop while a step waits for the owner is
told at once, one queued before the wait too, and is served when the wait is over or when a batch at work ends.
`tests/unit/test_code_rules.py`: a message in the write-ahead log is read.
`tests/unit/test_osascript_env.py`: a script's shell has the environment it is given, the tool gives it the shell
tool's, and Dot's own scripts keep the daemon's. `tests/unit/test_desktop_screenshots.py`: a password manager is known
by bundle id under a German, French or Spanish name, only what is on screen is blacked out, and a screenshot reads the
bundle ids. `tests/unit/test_desktop_paste.py`: on a fake clipboard, what the owner copied is back after a paste, a
refused text and keys that cannot be posted, and what someone copied meanwhile is left. The unit tests (3231),
`ruff`, `mypy`, `dotx eval check` and the smoke tests that do not drive the desktop pass, but for one that asks about
a left question, which fails under load and passes alone (`test_left_questions.py`).
