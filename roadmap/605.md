### 605. The composer's microphone is a recorder: a wave while you speak, the words when you are done

**Status: done (2026-10-24).** The owner sent a screenshot of their coding app's composer while it records: a cross at
the left, a dotted line that turns into a moving wave as they speak, the time, and a round tick at the right. They
asked: "when we press the voice button, a wave should start at the bottom, and when we press to finish while speaking,
the words should come". Dot's microphone (item 574) filled the box live as the engine heard, its words quieter until
settled, and stopped after six seconds of silence.

**As built.**

- **The recorder.** Pressed, the microphone turns the composer's controls (the "+", the choices, the microphone and the
  send) into a recorder on their line, under the box. On a phone it takes the whole line under the box. It holds:
  - a cross, which drops the dictation;
  - a wave of 96 bars that moves while the engine hears a sound;
  - the time since the engine began to listen;
  - a tick, which ends the dictation and puts its words in the box after what it held.
- **Until the tick the box keeps what it held.** What the owner types drops the dictation, as before. A message sent and
  the page put out of sight end it.
- **The wave** is drawn from the engine's own events, `soundstart` and `speechstart` against `soundend` and
  `speechend`, and a word heard. A real level would need a second capture of the microphone (`getUserMedia`). On an
  iPhone, two at once is the kind of trouble item 603 just mended. And the docs promise that Dot's page takes only the
  words (item 594). With reduced motion the wave stays still, and the time still counts.
- **Silence and pauses.** Six seconds with no word still end a dictation that hears nothing, saying so. Once a word was
  heard, a pause may last fifteen seconds before the dictation ends by itself, with its words going into the box, so
  the owner can stop to think.
- **The tick waits for the engine** to settle the words it may still change: the recorder stays, its buttons off,
  until the engine ends, and the settled words go into the box then. An engine that has not ended within three
  seconds is ended with the words it has.
- **The focus.** The microphone goes out of sight while it listens, so a keyboard that pressed it gives its focus to
  the tick; when the recorder ends, the keyboard's focus its buttons had goes to the box with the words. A tap's
  focus (Chrome on a phone gives a tapped button one) stays put, so no keyboard opens on the screen.
- **The code.** `voice.js`: `createDictation` takes `began()` and `sound(loud)`, waits `PAUSE_MS` after a word, and
  gives the recorder `clockText` and `nextWave`. `microphone.js`: the recorder. `composer.js`: the bar holds it.
  `app.css`: its look, and its line on a phone. `run.css`, which holds the phone's bar, is not touched: the recorder's
  phone rules are in `app.css`, the more specific.
- **The docs.** `docs/daemon.md` (the composer's microphone) says it.

**Tests.**

- `tests/js/voice.test.js` (+2): the clock, and the wave moving a bar in.
- `tests/js/dictation.test.js` (+2): `began` once and `sound`, nothing from an engine left behind; and the pause after a
  word, fifteen seconds where six were.
- `tests/smoke/test_voice.py`, the dictation's tests made the recorder's, at 1280 and 390:
  - the recorder takes the controls' place, and nothing comes into the box until the tick;
  - the clock counts from the engine's start (this caught a reset of the start time on the first try);
  - the cross and typing drop the words, and the tick puts them after what the box held;
  - a pause of fifteen seconds ends the dictation with its words, on a clock the test moves;
  - the tick waits for the engine to end, and puts the settled words in the box; an engine that does not end is
    ended after three seconds with what it heard;
  - the focus goes to the tick, and back to the box;
  - the owner's own stop says nothing;
  - the page fits a phone while it records.
- The shots of the recorder at 390, light and dark, and at 1280 were read by eye.
