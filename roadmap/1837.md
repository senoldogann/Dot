### 1837. The Mac's screen in a light quality, for a phone on a weak link

**Status: done (2026-11-25).** Asked by the owner: watching the Mac from the phone, smooth on a weak network too.

What must hold: a phone on a weak link sees the screen as it is now, with a short delay, rather than a picture that
stalls for seconds; the web page's full HD stream is unchanged; nothing is captured while nobody reads either stream.

Measured before (the owner's MacBook, a throwaway daemon, `GET /api/screen/live` read for 10 s): an ordinary desktop is
0.6 to 0.9 Mbit/s at 27 to 30 frames a second, but each key frame is 90 to 230 KB: three seconds of a 600 kbit/s link.
A viewer that falls 30 frames behind loses every frame until the next key frame (every 4 s), so on such a link the
picture moved in jumps seconds apart.

What was built: `GET /api/screen/live?quality=light` (`server/routes_watch.py`), a capture of its own beside `hd` (each
starts with its first viewer and stops after its last): 1280 pixels on the longest side, 30 frames a second, an average
of 1.5 Mbit/s (a fifth of `hd`'s ceiling) and a key frame every 2 s, so a frame dropped heals twice as soon.
`LiveSettings` carries the key frame interval (`desktop/live.py`). An unknown quality is a 422.

Not measured: `light` itself. Its key frame size, bit rate and delay on a weak link were not read, because
ScreenCaptureKit failed to start for every try of that hour on the loaded Mac (see item 1838); the settings are a
design from the `hd` numbers, not a measured result. `AverageBitRate` is the encoder's target, not a ceiling: a screen
where much moves may still be more than a 600 kbit/s link carries, which the daemon's drop-to-the-next-key-frame then
heals within 2 s.

Checked: `tests/smoke/test_watch_quality.py` (a paired phone reads `light` with its settings, `hd` is as it was, `4k` is
refused; fails without the change), `tests/unit/test_live_screen.py`, ruff, mypy.
