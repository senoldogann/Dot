### 627. An answer on iMessage reads well on a phone's narrow screen

**Status: done (2026-10-24).** The owner asked Dot on iMessage what was on the Mac's screen. The answer came clean of
Markdown (item 617) but as one dense block: an intro line, then five list items of two to six lines each on a phone,
with no space between them, full of quoted labels, parentheses and arrows. Their words: "the texts in iMessage are
very crammed; could they come more orderly, readable? Punctuation, spaces, marks done properly."

**As built.**
- **What Dot does to the text** (`channels/text.py`, `as_plain_text`, which every answer on a channel passes through):
  - **A list that holds a long item stands apart.** An item of more than 60 characters wraps on a phone. In a list
    with one, there is a blank line between the items of its first level, and before and after the list (`_aired`).
    - A nested item, and an indented line that goes on with an item's words, stay right under their item.
    - The first item stays under a heading right above it, as the heading's first words.
    - A list of short items (things to buy, steps of a word or two) stays one block, a line to each, as before.
  - **A heading is titled with a colon** when words follow it and it ends in a letter, a digit or a closing bracket or
    quote, as a title typed in a message is (*Summary:*). One that ends in a mark of its own (*What changed?*) keeps
    it, and one with nothing after it takes none.
  - **A run of spaces between two words is one space.** A code span and a block of code keep theirs, and the indent of
    a nested item is no run between words.
- **What the run is told** (`agent/prompt.py`, the Environment section): a run whose source is a channel was started
  by the owner's message there, on their phone, and its final answer goes back as plain text. It is to write for a
  phone's screen:
  - short paragraphs of one to three sentences with a blank line between them;
  - a list only for separate points, one short point to an item;
  - no tables, headings or nested lists;
  - quotes, parentheses, arrows and file paths only where they are needed.
  - The text's own rules above still hold for a model that writes Markdown anyway.
- `docs/channels.md` says both.

**What was not done, and why.**
- **No bold or larger headings.** Messages, through AppleScript, sends plain text alone. The bold letters of Unicode's
  mathematical alphabets have no ş, ğ or ı, and VoiceOver reads them letter by letter.
- **No change to the model's punctuation.** A space put after every full stop would break `defaults.toml`, `2.5` and
  the addresses an answer holds. The prompt asks for plain sentences instead.
- **The underlined numbers and times** in the owner's screenshot (`967K`, `20:50'de`) are iMessage's own data
  detectors, which a sender cannot turn off.

**Tests.**

  - nested items and an item's further words stay under it;
  - the first item stays under its heading;
  - a heading with words after it takes a colon, one that ends in a mark keeps it, and the last one takes none
    (Turkish words too);
  - runs of spaces, code spans and blocks, indents.
  - Each new test but the short list's fails against the code before.
- `tests/unit/test_channel_answer_style.py`: a run from `imessage` is told its answer goes to the phone as plain text,
