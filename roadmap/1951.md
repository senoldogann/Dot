### 1951. A schedule whose chat is busy waits for it, due, instead of skipping the firing

**Status: done (2026-11-26).**

The run review of 2026-11-26 (problem 7) counted 66 log lines "a run is already active on this thread" in a week:
four Gistsmith schedules share one chat, two money-line schedules another, and a long run (761 s) holds the chat when
the next comes due. The firing was claimed (the schedule moved to its next time), its start refused, and it counted as a
skip: "Gistsmith haftalık ship" lost its week. The owner chose to queue (RAPOR, decision 4).

The queue is the store's own due time, not a new list, so it survives a restart and cannot repeat a firing:

- `Scheduler._fire` asks `Store.chat_has_working_run` before it weighs a firing that goes on in its chat (`same_chat`).
  With a run at work (not a helper's) the firing is left due, its status says "waiting: a run is working in its chat"
  (written once), and no check runs and nothing is claimed. The next tick asks again, 15 s apart.
- When the chat's run ended, the firing starts at the next tick. Firings that came due meanwhile are the same due
  schedule: they collapse into the newest, as the firings missed while Dot was off do (item 1204), so one waits, never a
  pile; a firing that waited past `catch_up_s` (12 h) is skipped as a late one, and past `misfire_grace_s` (1 h) it is
  told to its run as late.
- A run that starts in the chat between that look and the claim (the owner's message, another process) is refused at
  the start as before; `Store.release_schedule_fire` takes the claim back (the due time, last firing, chat and status the
  schedule had), so the firing waits, due, instead of being counted a skip or failing a one-time schedule. A schedule
  changed or switched in the meantime is left as it is.
- A firing that makes a chat of its own never waits for the last chat.

Schedules that fire at the same tick are served one after the other, so the second sees the first's run and waits.

This adds no ask and no deny in yolo or free mode: the scheduler's firing path is not the gate.
