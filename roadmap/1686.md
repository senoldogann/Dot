### 1686. A chat's revision, moved by triggers; the chat's view, the list's view and their streams for the phone

**Status: done (2026-11-24).** The daemon side of the iPhone rewrite's sync contract (PLAN v2 §9, after its architecture
review: per-run cursors for the conversation, a view and a revision for the small state). The contract is in
[Dot on the phone](../docs/phone.md#a-chat-kept-in-sync-views-revisions-and-streams-item-1686), its schema in
`docs/schemas/thread-sync.schema.json`. The old calls are as they were.

**The revision.** `threads.revision` (a column added at every open, `INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0`: no new schema
version, no data rewritten) is moved by SQLite triggers (`store/revisions.py`, made at every open beside the indexes,
`CREATE TRIGGER IF NOT EXISTS`) on every insert, update and delete of a chat's state: `threads` (its name, pin, seen
mark, choices, agent, team, schedule, history version; not `updated_at`), `runs` (status, end, rewind, thread; not the
steps or tokens; a helper's run moves its parent's chat too), `queued_messages`, `queue_holds`, `queue_claims`,
`reactions`, `schedules`, `campaigns`, `meetings` (not each line said), `approvals` and `drafts` (the chat of their run
and of its parent), and the one key of `kv` that is a chat's state, `detached_helpers` (the helpers let go to the
background, each moving the chat it reports to; found by the first pre-push review, which saw the view's `blocking`
and `background` read it while no trigger watched it). Triggers, not a helper each writer calls, because a chat's state has about a hundred writes in the
store and a `dotx` command writes it from another process: a writer the triggers miss cannot exist. The lead chose this
over a call in each writer (2026-11-24) on these conditions, each with its test: helpers move their parent's chat, no
trigger on events or a per-step column, a write of another process reaches an open stream, a deleted chat reaches the
list's stream and its view is 404, a test lists every table. The revisions come from one counter (`kv` key
`thread_revision`), so the daemon finds what changed since a revision with one indexed read (`threads_revision`); a
deleted chat leaves a mark in `kv` (`thread_deleted:<id>`) that the daemon tells and removes. A chat's terminal sessions
live in the daemon: `RunManager.sessions_changed` moves the revision with the same statement (`bump_thread_revision`).

*Write cost, measured* (a throw-away store, 1,500 steps of a busy run, each 3 events, its tokens and its step count, and
300 renames; median of 3 runs each): a run's step 0.380 ms with the triggers, 0.373 ms without (the noise of the runs);
a write of a chat's state (a rename) 0.083 ms with, 0.057 ms without.

**What the daemon tells.** `watch_revisions` (a task of the daemon, every 0.5 s, as it relays `dotx` runs) reads the chats
whose revision moved and the marks of deleted ones, and puts a live `thread_changed` (`{thread_id, revision, deleted}`)
on its bus: whichever process wrote, an open stream hears it within half a second.

**The calls** (`server/thread_sync.py`, routes in `routes_threads.py`, any token as every `/api` call):

- `GET /api/threads/{id}/view?tail=&before=`: `revision` (read first), the chat, its list row, its newest runs (20, at
  most 50, `older`, `before`), the newest run's page and the last 200 of its events, its queue, its asks (its helpers'
  among them), its helpers as the owner's reference shots of a helpers sheet show them (PLAN §18: title from the goal's
  first sentence with no ids, status, start and end, provider, model, effort, the current step as tool, short target
  and plain line, whether the parent still waits for it, and its stop route), the background work (helpers let go,
  background commands, the loop), its schedules, reactions, drafts and terminal sessions. A cold open is this one call,
  beside the stream's.
- `GET /api/threads/view`: the chats with each one's revision and how many asks wait in it.
- `GET /api/threads/{id}/stream?cursors=run:id,...`: each named run (the chat's, or a helper of one of its runs, however
  many helpers it has had: the first review found a check against the newest 50 refusing an older one) from its own cursor, the chat's runs and helpers at
  work, then `synchronized` (`{revision, runs}`); live, a run that starts later in the chat and every helper a followed
  run starts join it, no run's end closes it, `thread_changed` of the chat comes on it, and a deleted chat ends it. After
  a drop it finds the chat's runs at work and the helpers of the runs it follows again (a start may be among what was
  lost: the second pre-push review), and the chat's runs made since it opened, one that started and ended unread
  among them (the third review's nit) and reads each run again 64 ids behind the newest it sent (item 1313's rule), so an
  event may come twice; the client keeps one per id. A page of older runs ends before the run `before` by its time and
  its row, so a run made at the same instant is on one page (the second review's nit).
- `GET /api/threads/stream`: the lifecycle kinds and every chat's `thread_changed`; never a run's steps.
- Both new streams write a keepalive every 5 s (the old ones keep 15 s), so a client drops one silent for 12 s.

**A helper's report is typed** (R-f H3): the `steer` event of a note of Dot's from another run says `of_dot: true`, and a
helper's report `source: "helper_report"` and `helper_run_id` (`RunManager.note` takes the helper's id). The web
timeline and the old app draw it as before.

The run's page, the chat's queue and its Automations are made by functions the old routes and the view share
(`run_detail`, `queue_view`, `thread_automations`); `server/stream.py`'s helpers the thread stream uses lost their leading
underscore.
