### 593. The microphone says what stopped it: macOS and Dot in the app, nothing heard, and macOS Dictation meanwhile

**Status: done (2026-10-23).** The owner pressed the composer's microphone (item 574) in Dot.app and read "The browser
has not allowed the microphone… Allow it in the browser's settings for this address": there is no browser to set in the
app. In Chrome, with the microphone allowed, they spoke and nothing came into the box, with no word of why. The other
session, which built item 574, handed the fix over.

Why: Dot.app's web view has WebKit's speech recognition, so the microphone shows there (item 574 took it for none), but
macOS gives the app neither the microphone nor speech recognition, since the app declares and is entitled to neither
(item 594 adds them). In a browser, a dictation that hears nothing (macOS keeping the microphone from Chrome, another
input chosen) ended without a word: `no-speech` said nothing, and a dictation the silence timer ended said nothing either.

**As built.**
- `problemWords(code, place)`, the place by `voicePlace`: in Dot.app a refused microphone says that macOS has not given
  Dot the microphone or its speech recognition and where to allow it (System Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone
  and Speech Recognition; a Dot not listed there is an older Dot.app, which cannot ask for them); in a Mac's browser,
  the browser for the address and in System Settings; both name macOS Dictation (the Dictation key, or Fn twice), which
  types into the box meanwhile. On a phone (or an iPad that asks for the desktop's pages, or any other) the words name
  the browser's and the device's settings and the keyboard's own microphone, and nothing of a Mac's.
- A dictation that ends by itself having heard no word, by the engine's `no-speech` or the six seconds of silence, says
  that nothing was heard and what to check: the microphone's permission (of Dot or the browser), the input in System
  Settings › Sound, the language in Settings › General › Voice. One the owner stops or types over says nothing.
- The page's language is listened in with its region ("en" as "en-US", "tr" as "tr-TR"): a bare tag can hear nothing
  in Chrome. The voices chosen in Settings had their regions already.
- The comment that took the desktop shell for one with no recognition says what it has. `docs/daemon.md` says it,
  and `docs/safety.md` that in the app the recognition is macOS's own, which may send the voice to Apple.
