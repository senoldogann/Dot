### 562. Settings: the sidebar is their nav, and each tab is a page under its own head

**Status: done (2026-10-23).** The owner had Claude Design redraw the settings (a PDF of eight screens: Models, Providers,
Browser, Rules, Memory, Self-improvement, Phone and System, drawn in Dot's Paper theme, with a nav of four groups) and
asked for them in Dot. This item is the frame the screens stand in; items 563 to 566 are the screens, one commit each.

**As built.**
- `js/settingstabs.js`: the settings' eleven tabs and the four groups they stand in (Interface, Models, How Dot works,
  This Mac), once, for the nav, the phone's list and the page; `tabAt(hash)` says which tab an address is on (the pages
  under System, Diagnostics and the licences, count as System).
- `js/settingsnav.js` and `sidebar.js`: while the settings are open, in a Mac's window the sidebar holds their nav in the
  place of the chats and the pages (`data-mode="settings"` on the sidebar, `shell.css`): Back to chats (to the chat last
  open, `dot.lastChat`), a button that hides the sidebar, the title, the groups with a link for each tab, and at the foot
  a card that says whether the daemon runs and for how long (or that the emergency stop is on, or that Dot does not
  answer). Each row says what its tab holds: the main model's name (Models), how many providers have a key (Providers), how
  many notes Dot keeps (Memory) and a green dot when a phone reaches Dot (Phone, read from Tailscale at most once a
  minute); a value that cannot be read leaves its place empty. A phone keeps its sidebar and its list of tabs (item 330).
  In a window too narrow for the docked sidebar the nav is in the drawer.
- `views/settings.js`: the page is the open tab alone, in a column of 920 px (`settings.css`), under its head: the tab's
  name as the page's one h1 (the bar says nothing; on a phone the bar names the tab, as before), the line that says what it
  is for, and at the right what the tab holds and the buttons of the whole page. A tab gets the head as `ctx.head`
  (`setLead`, `setMeta`, `setActions`) in place of the old `paneHead`. The row of tabs above the pane, in a narrow window,
  is gone with the rail.
- `panels.js` and `settings.css`: the pieces a tab is drawn with, `setSection` (a name over a card, a line under the name,
  buttons at its right), `setCard` and `setRow` (a title and a muted line on the left, the control on the right, the
  hairline between rows), `switchRowSetting` and `choiceRowSetting` (a switch and a choice as such a row), on Dot's
  tokens. SnapShots, which the design does not draw, is drawn with them: two sections, Capture and When it captures,
  each a card of rows. A tab whose cards still carry a head has the head over the card as a section's
  name, as a phone's always did (before, the head was inside the card), so the tabs that are not drawn as rows yet
  (General, Appearance) follow the design's look as well.
- `docs/daemon.md` and `docs/phone.md` say it.

**Tests.** `tests/smoke/test_settings_nav.py`: in a Mac's window the sidebar holds the nav (groups, labels in order, the open
tab marked, the values of Models, Memory and Providers, the daemon's card, one h1, nothing in the bar), follows the tab,
keeps System marked on Diagnostics and leads Back to chats; a narrow window has it in the drawer; a phone keeps its
sidebar. `test_phone_settings.py` and `test_phone_back.py` follow the new frame; the two tests of the row of tabs in
`test_phone_pages.py` are gone with the row.

Not built: the three tabs the design does not draw (General, Appearance, SnapShots) keep their contents; a search of the
settings; the nav's value for Self-improvement (the design shows none).
