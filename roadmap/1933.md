### 1933. A phone's video is sent by target bitrate and streamed to disk, so a quarter of an hour fits

**Status: done (2026-11-26).** Builds on item 1930 (#1167). A 720p AVFoundation preset made 26.0 MB per 30 s and 53.8 MB
per 60 s (7 Mbps), and the upload was base64 in a JSON body held whole in memory by the daemon (20 MiB), so any phone
video over about 20 s had to be trimmed.

**The export.** DotNext re-encodes with `AVAssetReader` and `AVAssetWriter` (`VideoTranscoder.swift`): H.264 High at
1.8 Mbps, AAC at 80 kbps, longest side at most 1280, at most 30 frames a second (a 60 fps recording drops every second
frame), a keyframe every two seconds, BT.709 so an HDR recording is tone-mapped, the recorded rotation kept as the
file's own flag. H.264 and not HEVC: ffmpeg and the Mac's decoder read both, but H.264 plays in every browser and viewer
the owner may open an attachment in. Measured on this Mac with a 1080p, 30 fps clip of scrolling code and moving shapes
with a tone (a screen recording's worst case for text), source about 5 Mbps:

| clip   | preset 720p (item 1930) | target bitrate | video / audio kbps |
|--------|-------------------------|----------------|--------------------|
| 30 s   | 26.0 MB                 | 6.7 MB         | 1809 / 63          |
| 60 s   | 53.8 MB                 | 13.5 MB        | 1812 / 65          |
| 120 s  | (about 108 MB)          | 26.8 MB        | 1804 / 66          |

A frame `watch_video` takes of it (768 px on its longer side, the tool's own `frame`) still reads 22-pixel code line by
line. At 14 MB a minute, 200 MiB is about 15 minutes.

**The upload.** `PUT /api/attachments/stream?name=&media_type=` takes the file as the raw body and writes it to the
attachment's folder chunk by chunk (`store_attachment_stream`): nothing is held whole. A video or a sound is held to
200 MiB (`MAX_MEDIA_BYTES`, which the JSON route could not reach under its 32 MiB request limit), any other file to
20 MiB; a body that passes it is refused (422) at the chunk that passes it, a declared length over the cap is refused
at once (413, `BodyLimit` lets this route alone through unbuffered), and a body that is cut, cancelled or refused leaves
no file and no folder. A phone's key reaches it like the JSON route (every `/api` path but the ways in), so
`guard.py` is untouched. DotNext sends a video or a sound this way, from a file read by `URLSession` as it sends; the
outbox keeps the video as a file of its own beside its record, so the message waiting for a link holds none of it in
memory. The web composer sends a video, a sound or a file over 2 MB this way too, and a smaller one as JSON as before.
The trim offer stays for a video over 200 MiB only.

**Checks.** `tests/unit/test_attachment_stream.py` (a chunk is on disk before the next is asked for, the cap, a cut and a
cancelled upload leave nothing, a document keeps its rules), `tests/smoke/test_server.py` (the route over a real server,
chunked, 413 and 422, no token), `tests/js/attachmentrules.test.js`, the composer's browser test (both ways of
attaching a video go as `PUT /api/attachments/stream`) and the engine's `OutboxTests` (a video is kept as a file, sent as
the raw body, cancelled and retried).
