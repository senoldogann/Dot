### 1380. Dot for iPhone: a design system (tokens, glass, type, motion, haptics) and the audit that asked for it

**Status: done (2026-11-23).** The owner's word of 2026-11-23: the iPhone app's design must be as good as it can be,
iOS-native "glass" (Liquid Glass), gradients and so on; plain and professional like the best coding-agent phone apps (near-black surfaces,
hairline borders, a calm blue, an 8-point radius scale, links plain and brighter with no chip, the queue reordered with
arrows). Dot is an autonomy, not a coding harness, so what it does for the owner (approvals, helpers, schedules and
loops, run status) has to be what the app shows first. This item is the system and the audit; items 1381 (the screens)
and 1382 (the icon, the launch screen, the Live Activity) use it. No behaviour, no `Data`, `Core`, `ChatModel`,
`ChatSession` or `NativeStore` changed.

**The audit** (the app as slice 2 left it, run in the iPhone 17 Pro simulator on iOS 26.5 against a scratch daemon with a
scripted model; screenshots before and after in
`/Users/dogan/Library/Application Support/Dot/workspace/ios-native-shots/glass/before` and `.../after`). What looked
toy-like, inconsistent or cramped, worst first:

1. **Type ignored Dynamic Type.** Every size was `.system(size: 15)`, `13`, `11`, `10`: at the largest accessibility size
   the list and the chat were the same tiny text (`before/list-dark-accessibility-extra-extra-extra-large.png`). Ten- and
   eleven-point metadata (the queue's header was 10) is also under what a phone can read.
2. **Dark only, forced.** `preferredColorScheme(.dark)` on the root and on every sheet, colours as `white.opacity(...)`:
   the light appearance did not exist (the "light" shots before are the dark ones), and an `opacity` ink cannot be
   contrast-checked.
3. **No glass, no material, nothing floating.** The composer, the status line and the queue were a flat strip on an opaque
   bar under the scroller with a hairline, so the transcript was cut off above it; the toolbar was painted opaque with
   `toolbarBackground`, which turns off iOS 26's own bar; inline title, no large title, no scroll edge effect. It read as a
   web page in a frame, not as an iPhone app.
4. **The one thing a run waits on was the quietest.** An approval was an 11-point amber caption and two 32-point flat
   buttons in a card like any other; "Waiting on you" in the list was a line of 13-point text; the agents strip was plain
   circles with an 11-point dot. A run that is blocked on the owner looked like a run that was not.
5. **A wall of red.** Every failed step was a red cross and red text, one per line; steps had no symbol of their own, so a
   run of ten steps was ten identical lines.
6. **Cramped targets.** Buttons 36 points, queue arrows 36 by 34, the status line 28 points tall: under 44.
7. **Three card languages.** `List` sections with `listRowBackground`, `.card()` boxes and hairline-only buttons, and the
   address screen on the system's colours; empty states were one faint line ("No messages yet.", "None bound to this
   chat.", "No schedules."), with no state for offline or an error that looked designed.
8. **A placeholder identity.** The icon was a yellow circle on white, the global accent colour yellow, Dot's mark yellow in
   the app and in the Live Activity, which used the system's orange, green and red instead of the app's own.

**What was decided.**

- **Colour is data** (`Shared/DotPalette.swift`): `DotRGB` (with WCAG luminance and contrast) and `DotPalette.dark` and
  `.light`, read by `Theme` (which turns them into colours that follow the appearance) and by the Live Activity, and
  measured by `DotPaletteTests`: every text token on every ground in both looks holds 4.5:1 (the smallest margin is the
  light look's `faint` on the raised surface, 4.69), white on the accent fill holds it at both ends of its gradient
  (4.6 and 5.5). Dark is the signature: `#0A0A0B` ground, `#121214` and `#1B1B1F` surfaces, hairlines at 9 % white,
  accent ink `#6AA4FF`; light is a cool white with the same roles.
- **Type is SF by text style**, so it all follows Dynamic Type (`body` is `.callout`, `small` `.footnote`, `meta`
  `.caption`, code is SF Mono). Two scales, `Space` (2 to 32) and `Radius` (4, 8, 12, 16, 24).
- **Glass goes on floating chrome only**, through three helpers in `Theme.swift`: `dotGlass(in:tint:interactive:)`
  (`glassEffect` on iOS 26; a thin material, a hairline and the tint before it; a solid raised surface under Reduce
  Transparency; a stronger hairline under Increase Contrast), `dotButton(.glass|.prominent)` (`.glass` and
  `.glassProminent` on iOS 26, capsule buttons before) and `DotGlassGroup` (a `GlassEffectContainer`); plus
  `dotProminentGlass` for the accent as glass. Text, tool lines, rows and long lists are never glass. Sheets keep the
  system's own glass on iOS 26 and paint the app's ground before it (`Chrome.swift`: `dotSheet`, `dotScreenBackground`,
  `dotSubtitle`, `dotScrollEdges`), so nothing is painted over what the system draws.
- **Motion and haptics are presets**: `Motion.snappy|smooth|bouncy` through `dotAnimation`, which stays still under
  Reduce Motion; `sensoryFeedback` for an approval (success, or warning when denied), a send, a stop, a switch, a
  selection, a copy.
- **The OS decides how it looks on an older iOS; a flag shows it.** `DotDesign.liquidGlass` is true wherever the system has
  Liquid Glass; a debug build started with `-DotMaterialLook 1` turns it off, so the look an iOS 18 phone gets can be seen
  on an iOS 26 simulator (no iOS 18 runtime is installed here). A debug-only `DesignGallery` (`-DotGallery 1|2|3`)
  shows the pieces with made-up data, because the scratch daemon never asks for an approval (its policy lets every step
  through, even `sudo`) and its `shell` step is refused as an unknown tool.
- **Not done on purpose:** swipe actions other than "Read" (the store has no pin, archive or delete to call, and `Data`
  is another change's); a custom navigation bar (the system's is what makes the glass right).

**Checked.** `xcodebuild test -scheme Dot`: 139 tests, 0 failures (the 127 of before, the link test now reads the dark
look's colour, and 12 new: `DotPaletteTests` and `ToolGlyphTests`). `xcodebuild test -scheme DotUI`: the ten tests of
slice 2 and the four of `GlassShotsTests`, 0 failures (the scroll-older test, which depends on the scroll metrics of the
transcript, passes with the composer floating over it). Screenshots: dark and light at the default size, dark and light at
the largest accessibility size, and the material look, of every screen. **Not verified:** a real iPhone (glass renders
from the live backdrop and may differ), an iOS 18 runtime (not installed), Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast as
seen (the code paths are a solid fill and a stronger line; the simulator was not put in them), VoiceOver.
