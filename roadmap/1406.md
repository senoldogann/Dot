### 1406. A call's result says how long its tool ran, and a tool that runs well past its limit is logged

**Status: done (2026-11-24).** Found by the autonomy audit (finding 11): a `browser_requests` call with `timeout_s: 45`
had `seconds: 102.79` in its `tool_result` (`run_mwsy4h0u`, 09:47:23 to 09:49:06, the moment the daemon was shut down).
One instance; the audit did not verify the cause. The item asked for a fix when the cause is plain, and otherwise the
evidence and the narrowest guard. The cause is not plain, so this is the second.

What was found. A call's `seconds` is the whole call: the gate before it, a hold of the run and the lessons after it are in
it, so the number alone does not say that the limit was overrun (the event's `waited_s` is the gate's part; the audit did
not quote it, and the owner's own database was not read: **not verified** for that run). `browser_requests` is not one of
the read-only tools that are tried twice (`_READ_ONLY_TOOLS`), so a retry is not the cause. One way a call outlives its limit
is real and is pinned by a test with a stand-in of the Playwright driver: a call that waits for Dot's own Chrome to start
(`BrowserSession._ensure_context`) and is cut at its limit goes on to wait for the launch to end and for the Chrome it
started to be closed (`_start_own` awaits `_close_given_up` in the handler of the cancellation), so that no Chrome is left
holding the profile (item 144, `test_browser_profile.py`); the cut call takes the rest of the launch, which Playwright ends
after 30 s at most. That wait is by design and inside the call's clock, and it fits a call that ends at the moment the
driver is stopped, as the audit's did, but nothing shows that it is what happened to that call.

A first version of this item let the cut call go at once and closed the Chrome in the background. CI refused it:
`test_a_launch_given_up_while_chrome_starts_leaves_the_profile_free` asserts that the profile is free when the cut call
returns, which a background closing cannot give, and the lock that holds the profile for a start
(`held_for_start`, item 554) would have been let go before its Chrome was closed. It was taken back: a choice between a
prompt end and a profile that is free at once is the owner's, and no evidence says which the audit's call needed.

What changed: the executor measures what a call spends in its tool (`ran_s`: each attempt's run and its unwinding, less the
time the running tool waited at the gate), puts it in the `tool_result` event beside `seconds` and `waited_s`, and logs
`tool_overran` (tool, limit, seconds) for an attempt that took more than five seconds past its limit, so that an overrun of
any cause shows next time with its numbers instead of as one doubtful figure. docs/runs.md and the browser page say it.

Checked: `tests/unit/test_tool_own_clock.py` (a tool that holds its cut call is logged and its `ran_s` says so; a tool cut at
its limit is not; the time a running tool waits for the owner is not its own; a call cut while the stand-in driver starts a
Chrome waits for the launch and leaves no Chrome behind, and is told as an overrun with its numbers),
`tests/unit/test_aggressive_autonomy.py` (the executor's retries and holds, unchanged), `tests/smoke/test_browser_profile.py`,
`dotx eval check`.
Not done: a prompt end for a call cut while Chrome starts (the owner's choice, above: the closing would have to run in a
task of the session's own, keep the profile held for the start until it ends, and the next start and `aclose` would wait
for it); the audit's own call is not explained: its record has no `ran_s`, and its `waited_s` was not read.
