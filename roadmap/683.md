### 683. The emergency stop stops what was still going on

**Status: done (2026-10-26).** The whole-project review of 2026-10-26 found five ways the emergency stop, which
`docs/safety.md` and CLAUDE.md promise stops Dot at once, left something going:

- a desktop step checked the stop and the corner once, before it began, and a text typed key by key (8 ms a key, no
  limit on its length) went on in its worker thread after the stop, into whatever then had the keyboard;
- `check_drift`, which the docs say looks before each step that the pointer is where the previous step left it, was
  called nowhere;
- the iMessage channel's limit of thirty messages a minute came before its reading of a stop, so a run that flooded the
  owner's chat kept `/stop` out;
- a watcher's look (up to minutes each) checked the stop once before all of them, so a stop engaged during one let the
  next look and its run start, and the reflection and the harness's step (which plays real runs in a process of its
  own) neither looked at the stop nor ended with it;
- what a run had sent a terminal's program that it had not read yet was typed after the stop, and what a run left
  running in a terminal went on.

**The change.** `desktop/input.post` takes a check it calls every five events, and typing, scrolling and dragging pass
the stop's (the `STOP` file and the corner); `execute_step` calls `check_drift` after the stop's check. The channel's
intake reads a stop that is not stale before its limit. A watcher's look and the start of its run look at the stop
first. The scheduler neither starts the reflection or the harness's step while the stop is engaged nor lets one under
way go on: it cancels it, as its own stop does (each records nothing until its end; the probe's process ends with its
commands). The emergency stop closes every terminal session (`Terminals.close_all`), which drops what waited to be
typed and ends the programs.

The stop is looked at only while nothing is held down (`post` counts the presses and the releases), so it never
leaves a key or a button down: a drag stopped after its button went down turned the owner's next move into the rest of
the drag (the code review of this item).

A step whose review took longer than a second may have been approved with the mouse on this Mac, so the check that no
one else is at the mouse is not made for it; the step still goes only to what was reviewed (the fourth code review:
every approved step after the first of a batch failed when the owner answered with the mouse). The emergency stop
closes the terminals of a chat and tells its page once.

**Checked.** `tests/unit/test_desktop_stop.py` (the posting ends between events once the check raises; a step whose
pointer was moved stops), `tests/unit/test_channel_intake.py` (a stop and `dur` read through a flood),
`tests/smoke/test_terminal.py` (every session of every chat closed, a long input dropped),
`tests/smoke/test_scheduler.py` (a step and a reflection under way cancelled by the stop), and the tests of the paths
the stop reaches (388).
