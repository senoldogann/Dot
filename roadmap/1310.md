### 1310. A restart that waits for Dot to be idle is kept in the store, told to the owner, goes on after two hours, and a refused load no longer leaves Dot down

**Status: done (2026-11-22).** `dotx service restart --when-idle` gave up after two hours (7201 s in the log) while a
long run held it; the owner heard of it from one banner of the Mac, nothing re-armed it, and the daemon stayed on the old
code. Separately, a `launchctl bootstrap` that failed after the `bootout` of a restart left no job, and the watchdog
returned early when launchd had no job.

- **The flag** (`restart_flag.py`, kv `service.restart_pending`): since when, the run that asked, the run it waits for
  now, whether the wait ran out. The job (`restart_when_idle.py`) writes it as it waits (through its own SQLite
  connection) and removes it when the daemon restarted or the job ends. `GET /api/status` gives `restart_pending` (a
  flag older than the daemon that reads it is none), Settings › System shows one line, "Restart", and `dotx service
  status` names the run it waits for and says when the two hours are over.
- **The run that asked** is named (`shell` sets `DOT_RUN_ID` beside `DOT_RUN_SHELL`; `dotx service restart --when-idle`
  hands it to its job as `DOT_RESTART_ASKER`) and shown as the one that asked in the flag and the notice.
- **The notice**: the scheduler's tick (`restart_notice.py`) tells the owner once, at 30 minutes, through `tell_owner`
  (the phone, the desktop, the messaging apps), which run holds the restart, by its goal and id, or that it waits for the
  night's learning; it is kept by the time the restart was asked for, so a later restart is told again.
- **After the two hours** the job gives up as before, with its banner (now "Dot was not restarted yet ... Dot restarts by
  itself the next time it is idle") and keeps the flag (`gave_up`), then goes on waiting, with no limit but a week, for a
  moment in which no run works (a run blocked on the owner works), no night's learning is under way, and restarts once.
  Nothing is stopped meanwhile; schedules and channels start their runs as ever.
- **launchd**: `load_job` tries `bootstrap` twice (2 s apart) and then `launchctl kickstart -k`, and raises what each step
  said when none starts the job; `restart_job` uses it. The watchdog counts a miss when the daemon's property list is in
  place and launchd has no job (and nothing answers at the daemon's address, and no restart waits), and after three
  looks loads the job with `load_job`; an uninstalled service is left alone. A load that fails is tried again three
  looks on and the owner is told once ("Dot is not running"; a mark in `watchdog.json`, as for the settings that do not
  read), not at every try.

Skipped, and why: the literal reading of "do not count the run that asked as at work". A restart that does not count the
asker restarts the daemon at the asker's last step, a few seconds after it asked, and cuts it off (it is interrupted, and
resumed only if `[agent] resume` allows): that makes the normal case, a run that asks for the restart as its last act,
worse, and kills a run, which the item forbids elsewhere. The asker is counted like any run, named in the flag and the
notice, and what held the restart two hours (a long asker included) is now told at 30 minutes and restarted at the next
idle moment, with nothing stopped.
