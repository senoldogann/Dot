### 891. A settled approval is one quiet line, and the line that asked never stays above its card

**Status: done (2026-10-31).** **Asked** by the owner (2026-10-31), with a screenshot of a chat of Dot's own initiative
whose `vercel build` approval had expired: an orange "Needs approval: Run: cd … && rm -rf … && vercel build …" line,
then an "Approval · Expired" card with the same command and the same reason again, and "Note: no decision before the
deadline": "this looks very ugly, let's not show it like this".

What it was: item 871 took the policy's line away only when the card came right after it, so a step's row or a thought
between them left both; item 872 made the waiting card say each thing once, and a settled one still printed the
policy's whole summary (command, brackets and the fixed sentence of why Dot asks) and the daemon's note.

- `timeline.js`: the line that asked goes when its own card comes (the card's summary is the line's step and why Dot
  asks), wherever it stands; a refusal's line, which no card follows, stays.
- A summary whose command the line cannot cut out (a folder, several lines, a long one) is kept whole under Details,
  but for the fixed sentence; a summary with no label ("Fetch https://…") is the line itself and is not said again.
- `approvals.js` `settle`: one line (`approval.decided.compact`): the action's label, the command on one line (cut,
  whole in its title), the status alone ("Expired"); a note of the owner's under it; **Details** with "Nobody answered
  in time, so the step did not run." for an expired one, the whole command, what the step does and a reason of its own
  (never `FIXED_REASONS`). A question keeps its question and its answer.
- `app.css`: the line's styles, borderless; `docs/daemon.md` says so.
