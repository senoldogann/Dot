### 1264. Watch: the Mac's screen, live, at any moment

**Status: done (2026-11-20).** The owner: they can watch Dot's browser while a task works, but want to say "watch" and
see the Mac's screen at any moment, while nothing works or something else does, in HD and at a good frame rate. Dot's
computer is the screencast of a run's browser, JPEG frames that come only while a run has a page.

- **Watch** (`#/watch`, a screen icon in the sidebar's head beside Campaigns and Schedules) shows the main display live,
  on the Mac or a paired phone. `GET /api/screen/live` (`routes_watch.py`) streams it: ScreenCaptureKit captures at up to
  1920 pixels on the longest side and 60 frames a second, VideoToolbox's hardware encoder makes H.264 (real time, no
  frame reordering, a key frame at least every 4 s, 8 Mbit/s average at most), and the page decodes it with WebCodecs
  and draws it on a canvas (`views/watch.js`, the parsing in `livestream.js`).
- One capture for every viewer (`desktop/live.py`): it starts with the first, stops once the last has gone, so nothing
  is captured while nobody watches. A viewer whose queue is full (half a second of frames) loses what waits and goes on
  from a key frame it asks of the encoder, so a slow phone sees the screen as it is now. The page stops the stream
  while it is out of sight and starts it again when it is back.
- A plain HTTP answer rather than a WebSocket, which the guard turns away; read with the installation's token or a
  phone's key as every answer of the API, with `Content-Encoding: identity` so that gzip does not hold the frames.
  A Mac without Screen Recording answers with that error before the stream begins.
- New dependency, approved by the owner: `pyobjc-framework-videotoolbox` (Apple's encoder; PyObjC knows its session only
  as an opaque pointer, so it is registered as its Core Foundation type, or VideoToolbox refuses every setting).
- It only watches; control from the phone is not part of it.

Measured on the owner's MacBook (1710 x 1112 points): 57 frames a second, 15 ms to encode a frame (p50; 18 ms p95),
8% of one core, 1.1 to 1.4 Mbit/s for an ordinary desktop, the first frame in 0.1 to 0.35 s; two viewers share one
capture, which stops 1 to 2 s after the last leaves.

Checked: `test_live_screen.py` (the frame's size and its bytes), `tests/js/watch.test.js` (frames split however the
bytes come, the configuration and codec of a key frame), the stream read end to end from a throwaway daemon (two viewers
at once, then one, the capture stopping after each), and the page in WebKit at 390 px and 1440 px drawing the live screen.
