### 1334. The Automations of a chat are a section of its side panel, each with a pencil, a play and a switch

**Status: done (2026-11-22).** The owner's word of 2026-11-22: in the right menu the schedules and loops that were set
up in that chat, with a switch to make each active or passive (a developer tool's Automations list is the model).

`automations.js` is a section of the Task tab, under the Lineage: it reads `GET /api/threads/{id}/automations` (item 1333)
and shows a row for each: a calendar-clock mark (new icon `calendarClock`) with a green dot while the schedule is on (red
when its last run failed, a ring when it is paused), the title (a loop without its "Loop: ") over a quiet line
(`automationwords.js`: "Every 6 hours · paused", "Once, Thursday at 19:31", "Loops every day at 09:00 · next Mon 09:00",
"done" for a time that ran once), a pencil that opens the schedule's editor on the Schedules page
(`#/schedules?schedule=<id>&edit=1`: the page marks the row, as a chat's schedule line leads there (item 665), and with
`edit=1` opens its form once, the address forgetting `edit=1` as the editor opens so that a reload or a Back does not open
it again; item 1334 teaches `views/schedules.js` that), a play that posts `POST /api/schedules/{id}/run`
and a switch that posts `…/enable` or `…/disable`: the routes the Schedules page uses, so the scheduler reads what they
change at the schedule's next firing, as before, and nothing else changes. The head has a button to all the schedules
(`#/schedules`). The rows are read again when the chat's tasks come and go and as the page comes back, as the
loops over the composer are. A chat with none shows no section. A schedule belongs to a chat through its `thread_id`:
the chat a run made it in (it keeps it, item 291), a loop's, and the chat a schedule fired into last, so a schedule that
fires into a new chat each time shows in the newest.
