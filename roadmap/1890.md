### 1890. Dot takes the Mac's pointer and keyboard only while the owner is away

**Status: done (2026-11-25).** The owner's rule of 2026-11-25 (backlog A4): with no keyboard or mouse input for 10
minutes the owner is away, and Dot may use the real pointer, its most reliable path. At the owner's first input Dot
stops taking it and goes on only through a path that needs no pointer, or waits until they are away again; it never
switches back to the real pointer silently.

What was measured first, read only, from the owner's own records (`~/Library/Application Support/Dot/dot.sqlite3`, which
holds 30 October to 25 November since the fresh start of 28 October, and `~/Library/Logs/Dot/dotx.log*`, 20 to 25
November; the old home of before 28 October is no longer on the Mac):

- 10 calls of `desktop_*` tools in 26 days, in 2 runs, both chats the owner started on the Mac in yolo: 7
  `desktop_screenshot`, 2 `desktop_open_app`, 1 `desktop_apps`, and no `desktop_act`. The logs add 2 runs of 20
  November (7 calls) whose records were deleted, among them the one `desktop_act` of the whole span: a press of "Load
  again" in Dot's own window, through accessibility, with the pointer left alone.
- Steps that took the real pointer or keys: 0. Steps while the owner was active: the 2 kept runs made their first desktop
  call 19 seconds and 2 minutes after the owner's own message, so the owner was most likely at the Mac for them; the pointer stood at the same
  point in the screenshots of each run (388, 766 and 592, 649), so they did not move it meanwhile.
- Failed or clashed: 1 of 17 calls failed, `desktop_open_app` of QuickTime Player ("the application did not answer",
  AX -25204, while it was opening a file an AppleScript had just handed it); no focus stolen and no wrong window found
  in any result.
- So the clash this item prevents has not happened yet in the owner's records, because Dot has hardly used the real
  pointer; the rule matters for the work the desktop is being built for (the night bank, item 1214's follow-ups), and
  costs a wait only when a pointer step meets the owner at the Mac.
- macOS's clock of the last input (HID idle time) counts Dot's own events: a pointer move posted to the HID tap read
  0.3 s idle after 10 155 s on the owner's Mac, whether its source was the session's or a private one; `caffeinate -u`,
  which Dot holds to keep the display awake, did not reset it. One posted to the session tap does not reset the HID
  state, but Dot's steps post to the HID tap.

What must hold: a step that moves the pointer or sends keys (a real click, double and right click, hover, scroll, drag,
type, paste, key, `activate`, and the front taken for them) is never sent while the owner is at the Mac; nothing of it
goes before it waits; Dot's own events never read as the owner's return; a yolo or free run asks nothing new.

What was built:

- `desktop/presence.py`: `OwnerPresence` reads the HID idle time (`idle_seconds`, moved here from the initiative) and
  takes an input that falls within a step Dot posted, give or take 1 s, as Dot's own, so the owner's last input stays
  what it was before. `[desktop] input_only_when_away` (on), `away_after_s` (600), `away_wait_s` (14 400).
- `actions.execute_step` refuses such a step (`OwnerAtTheMac`, not sent) while the step's environment says the owner is
  here; a click on a ref is done as a press and goes, and its real click when the element takes no press is refused.
  The session reads the presence before each step, takes no front for a batch while the owner is here, and notes the
  span of each step that posts as Dot's own input.
- The batch stops at that step (`waits_from`, `owner_came_back` when an earlier step had the pointer). `desktop_act`
  then waits off its own clock (`ToolContext.off_clock`, the executor's paused clock, which an approval used before),
  polling every 5 s, and goes on with the rest of the batch, numbered as its own, in the application it got to. A
  message of the owner's ends the wait, as it ends `wait_until`'s; the run being stopped ends it too.
- The run shows `desktop_waiting_away` ("Waiting for you to step away", or "You came back, so Dot let go of the pointer")
  and `desktop_away` (went on after, stayed, wrote), in Turkish and English; the model reads a line of each wait.

Not covered: `desktop_open_app` brings an application to the front and does not wait, nor do keys an `apple_script`
or `osascript` sends through System Events; the wait counts toward the run's `run_max_minutes`; the owner's input while Dot posts
a step is hidden by Dot's own (their next input after it is seen); a Mac whose idle time cannot be read is not away.

Step 3, a pointer-free background driver (cua-driver: events sent to a window that is not in front, through private
SkyLight SPIs, as Hermes and OpenClaw's CUA provider do; `reports/refs-2026-11-25/clawdbot.md` item 8,
`hermes-agent.md` item 10), is not built: the measure above shows nothing for it to win yet (no pointer step in 26 days),
and it would put a third-party binary with Accessibility and Screen Recording behind private APIs that a macOS update can
break. If the owner wants it later, the plan is an opt-in `[desktop] driver = "cua"` with no fallback per action (a
failure says the driver is unavailable and the step waits as here), the driver pinned and its signature checked, a
trial on the night bank of item 1214 against this item's waits, and the owner's yes before it is installed.

Checked: `tests/unit/test_desktop_away.py` (a fake idle clock: the threshold, Dot's own input not read as the owner's, a
pointer step waiting while accessibility steps go, the owner's return at the next step, the wait's end by stepping away,
by its limit and by the run's stop, the tool going on with the rest and showing the wait),
`test_desktop_press_click.py` (a click is a press or nothing while the owner is here), `test_tool_own_clock.py` (a wait
off the clock is not cut at the tool's limit), `test_freedom_contract.py`, `dotx eval check`, `npm run check`.
