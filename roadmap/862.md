### 862. Three ideas from agent-desktop in Dot's own desktop: a click that is a press, a window's skeleton, a failure that says whether to retry

**Status: done (2026-10-31).** **Asked** by the owner, who sent vercel-labs/agent-desktop ("bunu kesinlikle
kullanmalıyız"), then accepted the proposal ("agent desktop'daki önerilerini de kabul ediyorum").

**Measured first.** agent-desktop 1.0.2 was downloaded from its GitHub release and checked against its `checksums.txt`
(ad-hoc signed, no Developer ID). Then both read the same windows, without acting on them, three tries each, the
median taken:
- Finder: agent-desktop 0.39 s, Dot's own walk 0.016 s.
- Mail: 3.06 s against 0.20 s.
- Dot: 0.18 s against 0.057 s.
- Chrome: agent-desktop refused (several windows); Dot 0.012 s.

agent-desktop starts a process for every command. As a replacement for Dot's walk it would have made every look at a
window several times slower, so it is not wired in. Three of its ideas Dot lacked are built into Dot's own code instead:
1. **A click on a control's ref is a press** (`desktop/actions.py`, `session.py`). A `click` on the ref of a button, a
   box to tick, a radio button, a menu item, a menu bar item, a menu button, a pop-up, a link or a disclosure
   triangle, enabled and with no key held, is `AXPress`. The pointer stays where the owner has it, and their front
   window stays in front: a batch whose clicks are all such does not bring the application forward. An element that
   takes no press (-25206, -25208) has its window brought forward and is clicked as before. Any other click (a field,
   a row, a point, a double click, a key held) is a real one, as it was. A real click on such a control, for one that
   takes the press and does nothing (a custom control, web content), is a click at its `x` and `y`, as the tool's
   description and the prompt say. They had said "click does a real click (use it when press does nothing)", which
   would now have pressed it again (the pre-push review). A control the last view showed enabled and that is disabled
   by its step is clicked with its window brought forward, as the batch had left it behind.
2. **A dense window's skeleton** (`desktop_snapshot` `skeleton`, `desktop/views.py`). It lists the regions down to three
   levels, each with how many elements it holds and how many can be acted on. It leaves out a list's items and the
   controls, and gives each region a ref to read it by. On the owner's Mail window it took 692 characters, where the
   whole view takes 10,115.
3. **A failure that says whether to retry** (`desktop/ax.py`). A failed accessibility call says what to do after it:
   - A read that got no answer is safe to retry.
   - An action that got none may have been done, so it is to be looked at first.
   - An element that is gone needs a new snapshot.
   - An action the element does not support fails the same way again.
   The error's context keeps it as `retry`.

**Left for later, with the owner there.** agent-desktop's notifications (list, dismiss, a notification's action) read
Notification Center, which on macOS has to be open. Its tree on macOS 26 has to be probed by opening it on the owner's
screen. That is done when they are at the Mac, as its own item.

**Checked.**
- `tests/unit/test_desktop_press_click.py`:
  - every role of the set is pressed, with no event posted and no app brought forward;
  - a control that takes no press is clicked with its window in front;
  - a press failing otherwise fails the step;
  - a key held, a point, a field, a row, a text and a double click are real clicks;
  - `_needs_front` leaves the front alone for a batch of presses, and not for a field, a disabled button, an unseen
    ref or a key;
  - the retry hints by code, read and action;
  - the skeleton's regions and counts.
- `tests/smoke/test_desktop.py` (`desktop`, `DOT_DESKTOP_TESTS=1`, on this Mac against the fixture window): a click on
  the Increment button's ref reaches the button with the pointer where it was, and the checkbox toggles both ways.
- The skeleton read on the owner's Mail, Finder and Dot windows: roles and counts only.
