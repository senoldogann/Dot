### 481. A rule that hands the step off decides wherever it stands

**Status: done (2026-10-21).** The second half of the strictest-rule design (the second idea of the nine from OpenBot;
item 449 was its order and its note, item 480 the match in parts). A hand-off is the owner's strictest answer, and the
policy treated it so in some layers (it takes the place of the question about a message, item 429, and of a spend
within its caps, item 385) while the rules dropped it when an earlier rule of another answer fit the same action: the
test of the approval modes had to delete an allow rule for a hand-off to hold. Full strictest-wins (ask over allow) was
left out on the design's word: the order is the only way to write an exception, and the shipped examples are an allow
above a broader ask. The critique asked that the change come only after the owner could see a shadowed hand-off; the
owner's rules, read from a copy of their database, are none, so nothing of theirs changes. **As built.**
`ruling_rule` (`policy/engine.py`): a rule that hands off decides wherever it stands, the first by position when two
fit; any other answer is the first rule that fits; once a rule fits, only the later ones that hand off are read, so a
slow or unreadable expression of another answer after the first fit refuses nothing, and one that hands off refuses the
step as a rule the policy reads. `decide_past_review` is unchanged: its questions "is the rule a hand-off" now meet a
hand-off an allow used to hide. `rule_shadow.py`: a hand-off never decides only behind an earlier hand-off that fits all
it fits; a rule of another answer never decides behind any hand-off that does, earlier or later, or an earlier rule that
does; the page's note says so, and for a hand-off that decides in its place says that moving does not help.
`rule_replay.py`: the try-out follows the policy (it asks `match_rule`), and the usage of today's rules picks the rule
that decides as the policy does, and says a slow hand-off after the first fit is reached. The review model's drafter is
told the rule. Yolo is untouched: a yolo run waives the owner's hand-off by design (item 443). Tests:
`tests/unit/test_rule_answers.py` (a hand-off after allow, preapproved and ask decides; switched off, not fitting or
absent leaves the first; of two hand-offs the first; the message guard and a spend allowance give way to a hand-off an
allow used to hide), `test_rule_timeout.py` (a later hand-off is read and a slow one refuses; others are not read),
`test_rule_shadow.py` (both directions), `test_rule_replay.py` (try-out and usage), `tests/smoke/test_rule_handoff.py`
(an "ask" run with an allow rule first hands the write over and does not write; fails without the change),
`test_approval_modes.py` (the hand-off holds with the allow rule still there). Docs: safety.md.
