### 999. A Mac's settings are the phone's groups of rows, and the sidebar's foot is your account

**Status: done (2026-11-06).** **Why.** The owner's word of 2026-11-06: the rework of 2026-11-05 (items 991, 993 and
995) changed the phone alone, and on the Mac "nothing changed"; bring the desktop to the same plain design. A Mac's
settings were seven sections, each page with a row of its tabs, so a setting was found by knowing its section; the way
to them was a cog beside Connect apps. An independent review of the proposal (AGREE WITH CHANGES) asked for rows of a
name alone, the page named after its row, a nav that scrolls on its own, the counts on their own rows, and an account
row at the foot whose name for a reader holds the name it shows (WCAG 2.5.3).

**Built.** `settingstabs.js`: `SETTINGS_GROUPS` (the phone's `PHONE_GROUPS`) for both widths; the sections and
`sectionOf` go. `settingsnav.js`: the groups' rows, a group's name over them, the counts of Memory, Vault and Phone at
their rows. `views/settings.js`: a tab's page is named after the tab, with no row of tabs. `sidebar.js` and `shell.css`:
the foot is Connect apps over your account's row (your initials in a round mark, your name; `Settings, <name>` for a
reader). The dead styles of the row of tabs go. Docs: `docs/daemon.md`.

**Checked.** In a browser on a throwaway daemon at 1400 px (the nav's groups and rows, Memory's page under its own name,
the foot) and 390 px; `tests/smoke/test_settings_nav.py`, `test_phone_settings.py`, `test_channels_page.py`,
`test_vault_page.py`, `test_rules.py`, `test_desktop_lights.py`; `npm run check`.
