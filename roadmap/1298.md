### 1298. A download has a size limit and a folder quota, and a crawl follows its start address through a redirect

**Status: done (2026-11-22).** Two unbounded things in the browser's reach. (a) `_on_download` saved every download of
Dot's own Chrome with no limit, so a page that streams an endless file could fill the disk. (b) `web_crawl` fixed its
scope from the start address before any redirect: `example.com` answering from `https://www.example.com/` put every
link of the page out of scope and the crawl returned one page.

(a) `[browser] download_max_mb` (default 500) and `download_quota_mb` (default 5120), in `config.py`,
`defaults.toml` and `docs/configuration.md`; 0 takes a limit off. Chrome reports a download's bytes
(`Page.downloadProgress`) only to a DevTools session enabled before the download began, so each tab of Dot's own
Chrome gets one when it is registered (`watch_downloads`, only while a limit is on). A download's room is the smaller of
the cap and what the quota leaves after the folder's weight; a download whose received or announced size passes it is
cancelled as it comes in (`Download.cancel`), what was written is deleted and the tab's notes say which limit it passed;
a folder already at its quota turns the download away before it is saved; and the size is judged again once a download
finished (it may have finished before a progress event, or the session could not be made, which is logged). Downloads
the owner's Chrome saves in attach mode are not Dot's and are not limited.

(b) `Scope` carries `origins`, the other forms of the site's address. After the crawl's first page, when the start
address ended on another origin, `rebased` moves the scope to where the site answers from if it is the same site (the
same host but for `www.`, over `http` or `https`; a subdomain is another site), keeping a prefix scope's folder; a start
that redirected to another site keeps its page, follows none of its links and says so. Each later page's final
address is checked with `in_scope`: one a redirect carried out of the scope is left out with its reason. The sites the
pages linked to outside the scope are counted and named in the index (`CrawlResult.dropped`, with `notes`, inside the
fence). A crawl whose start did not move keeps exactly the scope it had.

Checked: `tests/unit/test_download_limits.py` (the room each limit leaves, the folder's weight, the defaults, 0 and a
negative value, and a download turned away at the quota that leaves no id behind for the next download of its address), `tests/smoke/test_browser_download_limits.py` on real Chrome (an endless stream is cancelled after a
few megabytes, the server sent fewer chunks than it had, and nothing is left in the folder; a small file is saved as
before; a folder at its quota turns a download away), `tests/unit/test_crawl_redirect.py` (twelve tests: the www, the
https and the prefix cases, links to the other form, a start that moves to another site, a page carried out of scope, an
ordinary crawl unchanged, the same-site rules, the index) with `test_crawl.py`, `test_crawl_reads.py` and the crawl
smoke test unchanged.
