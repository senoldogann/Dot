### 1892. The nightly browser run's stale tests follow today's screens, and four small bugs they found are fixed

**Status: done (2026-11-25).** The nightly `browser` workflow had been red every night: in the runs of 23, 24 and 25
November about 18 tests failed a night, nearly all of them stale, written for screens and signatures that have changed
since (the sidebar's rows of item 1327, the composer of item 1330, the grouped tools, `add_objective` and
`update_objective`, the runtime's transcriber of item 1212, the owner's notes that a run's change only proposes).

What must hold: each test checks what the screen does today, as strictly as before; a test is not loosened to pass, and
where it showed a real bug the product is fixed. No sleep and no retry is added.

The product bugs the tests showed:

- The schedule's icon (`calendarClock`, item 1334) drew 19 by 20 of its 24 grid, past the area every icon draws (item
  849): it is given a frame as the others are, and the phone's copy of it is exported again
  (`scripts/export_ios_icons.py`, item 1461).
- A note a run changed or forgot with a tool of a group that was off left no "Updated memory" line in the conversation:
  the result starts with the line that says the group is on now, and the timeline read only a result that starts with
  `Updated:`. It reads past that one line now.
- A chat deleted while its page was open (on another page or the phone) left a "Run not found." toast over the page the
  reader went to: the Coding worktree panel's read of the deleted run toasted its 404, where every other panel says
  nothing and the page says Task not found. It says nothing now.
- On a phone, an earlier turn that grew above what is read (its "Waiting on you" and what Dot needs, item 1061) moved
  the conversation by its height when that turn ended a few pixels inside the top of the box: `keepPlace` took the gap
  between two turns for a part that is seen. It reads where the part itself ends now. Chrome's scroll anchoring hid it;
  WebKit's does not keep the place there (item 1165), so an iPhone's conversation jumped.

Mac in the file's runs, alone and with other tests; locally only `/api/browser/mode` (held by the test) and
`/api/stream` are open at the shot, so it is not Chrome's six connections to one host; the fonts are the daemon's own
files, nothing from the network. The runner's log of that failure is gone. The two shots of that test now say, when
they time out, each font face's status and the requests still open, so the next failure names its cause.
