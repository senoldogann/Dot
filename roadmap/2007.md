### 2007. A background driver for the desktop's steps, a trial: cua-driver sends them to the window, the Mac's pointer left alone

**Status: built, off by default (2026-11-27).** The owner approved the trial on 2026-11-27: item 1890's step 3, an
opt-in path that sends the desktop's pointer and keyboard steps to an application's window in the background, through
[cua-driver](https://github.com/trycua/cua) (`reports/refs-2026-11-25/clawdbot.md` item 8, `hermes-agent.md` item 10),
so that they go while the owner is at the Mac instead of waiting for them to step away.

The approach, written before the code:

- **How Dot talks to it.** cua-driver's documented embedding contract (its `EMBEDDING.md`): Dot starts
  `cua-driver serve --embedded --no-overlay --socket <private socket>` as its own child, waits for the socket, then
  `cua-driver mcp --embedded --socket <same>` and speaks MCP `2025-06-18` to it as line-delimited JSON-RPC on its
  standard input and output (`initialize`, `tools/call`). As Dot's child it is in Dot's responsibility chain, so macOS
  answers its Accessibility and Screen Recording checks with Dot's own grants (measured: `check_permissions` said
  `attribution: host`, both granted) and it never asks for one. The socket lives in a short folder of `/tmp`: a Unix
  socket's path must fit 104 bytes (measured: the scratch folder's path was refused, "shorter than SUN_LEN"). Its
  telemetry is off in its environment (`CUA_DRIVER_RS_TELEMETRY_ENABLED=false`, measured: "disabled (source:
  environment)"). Every call names the session label `dot`.
- **The version.** Pinned to 0.28.2 (cua-driver-rs v0.28.2, 15 September 2026), the version installed on this Mac and
  the only one measured; the latest is 0.63.2 and Homebrew's cask (`cuadriver`) is 0.61.3, neither measured. Dot uses a
  driver only when `codesign --verify --strict` passes with team `YCK386LBJ7` (Cua AI), checked before the binary is ever run, and then its manifest says 0.28.2;
  otherwise the batch fails with why. Moving the pin is a one-line change after measuring the new version.
- **Installing.** Dot never runs an installer. `dotx doctor`'s row `desktop background` says whether the driver is
  there, its version and signer, and the one command that installs the pinned release:
  `curl -fsSL https://cua.ai/driver/install.sh | CUA_DRIVER_RS_VERSION=0.28.2 bash -s -- --no-connect --no-test`
  (`--no-connect`: cua's installer otherwise wires the driver into Claude Code, Codex and Cursor). The cask installs only
  the latest release, so it is not the pinned command.
- **Which steps go through it** (`actions.in_background`): a click, a double and a right click with no key held, type,
  paste, a scroll and a key the driver names (return, tab, escape, arrows, space, delete, home, end, page up and down,
  F1 to F12, letters and digits). A click with a key held stays on the Mac (the driver itself needs the foreground for
  it), as do hover, drag, activate and keys it does not name (forward delete, punctuation): they keep item 1890's wait.
  Press, focus, set_value, menu and a click done as a press go through accessibility as before. Never `bring_to_front`
  and never the driver's `foreground` delivery: every call asks for `delivery_mode: "background"`, and the batch takes
  no front for the steps the driver sends.
- **The same policy, nothing new asked, nothing denied allowed.** The gate, the policy and the approvals are not
  touched: a step is classified, reviewed and recorded as before (`test_freedom_contract.py` classifies every desktop
  row alike with the setting on and runs the table). What changes is where a step goes, so what the review judges
  follows it: a keyboard step without a ref is judged against the batch's own application's focused field, where the
  driver's keys go (sent to the window accessibility says is focused, found by its place), not the field of the application in front; a step at a point the review found in another
  application's window is not sent (the driver would reach the batch's application under it); a ref is clicked in its
  window even when another covers it (the event goes to the window). The password-manager and Keychain Access refusal,
  the secure-field check, the failsafe, the drift check and the re-read before a step (item 555) run first, unchanged.
  The owner-away rule (item 1890) holds for every step that still takes the Mac's own pointer or keyboard. A step the driver sends is not noted as Dot's own input (it posts none to the HID), so the owner's touch while it runs, which may take seconds, reads as their return. The driver gets Dot's environment without the variables that hold a secret (the filter a command of a run gets, `tools/env_filter.py`).
- **Coordinates.** The driver reads a step's x and y in the pixels of the window's *last capture* (measured on 0.28.2:
  after a capture at 320 pixels wide, a click computed for that frame pressed the button and one computed for the 2x
  frame missed). So each pointer step first takes a capture of that window alone, without its accessibility walk,
  about a pixel per point, and places the point in the frame the answer gives.
- **What it cannot do.** It needs a window on the current Space; one minimised or on another Space fails the step.
  Typing goes in through accessibility where the field takes it and as the driver's own key events where not (web and
  Electron fields), and the driver itself reports web content as unverifiable; Dot's read-back of the field (item 1146)
  checks it as before. Menu shortcuts of native applications may not dispatch in the background (the driver says so:
  `escalation: delivery_failed` for cmd+a); the step's line says what the driver confirmed.
- **The emergency stop.** A text goes to the driver in pieces of 100 characters, with the failsafe (the STOP file, the corner, a stopped run's call stop) checked before each, so a stop ends typing within one piece, as the Mac's own typing ends between keys (item 683). A click on a menu item's ref that is not pressed through accessibility is refused with why: a menu is a window above the ones the driver clicks in.
- **No fallback.** A driver that cannot start fails the batch that needs it, and a step it fails fails, each with why; it never goes to the owner's
  pointer instead. A call with no answer within 60 s stops the driver and is not sent again (the driver's own rule:
  never replay an action whose completion is unknown); the next batch starts a new one.

Measured on this Mac, read only, before building (cua-driver 0.28.2, two of Dot's own fixture windows, the one driven
behind the other, which was in front; no application of the owner's): a click by pixel at a button 0.65 to 0.74 s
(the driver pressed it through accessibility), a click into a field 2.1 to 2.8 s, typing six characters 1.1 s
(accessibility, confirmed by its read-back), a key 1.2 s, a scroll of three notches 2.0 to 2.4 s (60 points), a capture
of the window alone 0.25 to 1.5 s, the accessibility walk the driver does for a full window state 5 to 19 s (Dot does
not use it). In every case the application in front and the pointer were the same before and after, and each event
reached the window behind and none the one in front.

Measured through Dot (`tests/smoke/test_desktop_background.py`, the same five steps off and on, the owner taken as at
the Mac, three runs, while other sessions' test runs loaded the Mac to a load average of 550 to 980, so the seconds are
slow and noisy and say little of the driver itself):

| step | off: outcome, s (runs 1, 2, 3) | on: outcome, s (runs 1, 2, 3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| click on the Increment button (a press both ways) | done, 2.9, 0.9, 6.4 | done, 6.7, 10.0, 12.8 (with the driver's start: its signature check and launch) |
| click into the Name field | waits, 2.4, 0.9, 1.7 | done, 6.4, 5.7, 23.6 |
| type "cua" into it | waits, 2.4, 0.5, 1.6 | done, 3.1, 1.8, 3.1 |
| key tab | waits, 2.0, 0.5, 1.7 | done, 3.1, 4.3, 2.6 |
| scroll the area down 300 px | waits, 0.8, 0.6, 1.8 | done, 27.1, 53.8, 4.6 |

Success 1 of 5 off (the four others wait for the owner, as item 1890 says) and 5 of 5 on, in every run; focus changes
0 of 15 both ways (the front window and the pointer were the same before and after every step). The spread followed
the Mac's load (in run 2 a capture took 22.6 s and the scroll's call 24.5 s). Run 4, on a quiet Mac (load average 8,
after the pieces of text were built), whole steps through Dot, off and on: the press 0.5 and 1.7 s (the driver's start
with it), the click into the field waits and 2.4 s, typing waits and 1.4 s, the key waits and 1.5 s, the scroll waits
and 2.4 s; the driver's own calls: a capture 0.3 to 0.7 s, the click 1.4 s, typing 1.1 s (accessibility, confirmed),
the key 1.2 s, the scroll of 15 notches 1.8 s. About a second of each call is the driver's watch for a window the step
opened (its `CUA_DRIVER_WINDOW_CHANGE_TIMEOUT_MS`, left at its default, which also guards the front).
