### 1333. A chat's schedules and loops are one list the side panel can read

**Status: done (2026-11-22).** The owner's word of 2026-11-22: in the right panel of a chat the schedules and loops set up
in that chat, with a switch for each.

A schedule belongs to a chat through `schedules.thread_id`: a loop made with `/loop` (item 281), a schedule a run made in
the chat (item 291) and the schedule a chat is in when it fires in the same chat. `GET /api/threads/{id}/automations`
(`routes_data.py`, next to the schedules' routes) returns them as `[{schedule, loop}]`: `schedule` is the
`ScheduleView` the Schedules page shows (its time in words, its local next and last time, its agent), `loop` says that it
is the chat's own loop (`is_loop` in `scheduler/loops.py`: a repeating schedule that `loop_name` named); switched-on ones
come first and soonest first, as the Schedules page orders them; a chat that does not exist is a 404. Nothing was added
for the switch and for Run now: `POST /schedules/{id}/enable|disable` and `POST /schedules/{id}/run` are the routes the
Schedules page uses, and what they change is read by the scheduler at the next firing, so autonomy is as it was: a
schedule switched off is not due, one switched on has its next firing again, and no other schedule is touched.

Checked: `tests/smoke/test_thread_automations.py` (the list of a chat, its loop, the paused one last, nothing of another
chat or of no chat, the 404; switching one schedule off and on against `due_schedules`, the sibling untouched, Run now
refused in a server without a scheduler and nothing changed), `tests/unit/test_loops.py::test_a_loop_is_a_repeating_schedule_the_loops_name_made`.
