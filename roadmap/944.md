### 944. A pull request's checks run on five runners at once, and a merge no longer sends the others back to wait

**Status: done (2026-11-03).** **Asked** by the owner, after an article on making CI fast for coding agents
(Owen Gretzinger, "How I made our CI 20x faster"). Measured on the run of #762: a runner waited seconds for its machine
and setup took about forty seconds a job; the time was the tests, run in a row: the phone's browser tests 13 minutes on
one runner, the gate 12.5 (the smoke tests 8.2, the unit tests 2.1, `dotx eval check` 1.5). And the rule that a pull
request be up to date with `main` before its merge ran every other open one again, 15 minutes each, after every merge.

**Built.**
- `.github/workflows/gate.yml`: the gate is two jobs, `gate` (the checks, the unit tests, the smoke tests' first part)
  and `gate (part 2)` (`dotx eval check`, the second part); the phone's browser tests are two parts, split by the time
  their files took. Five runners in all, the most macOS runners a free account runs together.
- `tests/smoke/conftest.py`: `--part k/n` keeps the files of part k of n, dealt in turn by their sorted paths, so that
  a new file joins a part by itself.
- `main`'s protection (the owner's yes, 2026-11-03): the up-to-date rule is off, and the required checks are the five
  jobs. Two pull requests green alone may still break `main` together; `main`'s own run of the gate, on every push,
  shows it after the merge.

Not taken from the article: faster runners (they are Linux, and Dot's tests need macOS; GitHub's larger macOS runners
are paid), a faster lint or type check (ruff and mypy take 20 seconds), a cache fix (setup took 40 seconds), running
only what a pull request touches (the browser tests reach the whole daemon through its pages), a pre-push check (the
pre-push review runs before every push).

**Checked.** Both smoke parts collect every test the whole run does, none twice (609 and 674 of 1283); the phone's
parts 20 and 25 of 45. The gate's times on this pull request: in its log.
