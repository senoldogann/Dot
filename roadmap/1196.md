### 1196. The daemon keeps WebDriverAgent on the owner's iPhone up, tells the owner when it is locked, and `phone_status` tells a run its state

**Status: done (2026-11-15).** The owner's iPhone runs WebDriverAgent (an XCUITest runner app, `dev.dotagent.wda`),
reachable over Tailscale and the home network. Until now Dot knew it only through notes and a script it wrote in its
workspace (`tools/telefon`, 453 lines) with a nohup watchdog (`tools/wda-nobetci`): on 2026-11-15 one run used 191
shell calls to set up a Shortcuts automation, and the owner found its use of the phone clumsy. The owner asked that
Dot know the phone better and reach it all the time, and chose (2026-11-15): the phone stays unlocked while it charges
and Dot tells them when it finds it locked; the daemon itself brings WebDriverAgent back up; Dot taps, types and opens
apps without asking, as on the Mac (what the policy asks for there it asks for here).

This item is the link only:

- `phone/`: `wda.py` (WebDriverAgent over HTTP, reads only: the first answering address, `/status` and `/wda/locked`,
  which need no session, retried with backoff; what the phone answers goes to the log, never into an error a run
  reads, since another device on the home network could answer the address), `devicectl.py` (the Mac's listing of the phone and the launch of the
  runner), `keeper.py` (up / locked / unreachable; the owner told once per state after `notify_after_s`), `desk.py`
  (a relaunch with `devicectl device process launch` when the phone is listed as available, none again for five
  minutes after a listing, a launch or a wait that failed).
- `tools/phone.py`: `phone_status` in the group `phone`, registered only with `[phone] enabled`; it says only Dot's own
  words of the link, so it taints no run.
- `server/daemon.py` runs the keeper; `GET /api/iphone` gives its last look, and Settings › Phone shows it in a card
  of its own while `[phone]` is on; a run with the tool is told the phone's state in a word that stays the same while
  the state does.
- `[phone]` in `defaults.toml` (off by default; the fresh-home budget is unchanged).

Measured against the owner's iPhone, read only: `/status` 0.13 s; a locked phone answers `/status` and `/wda/locked`
but refuses even `/source` ("Not authorized for performing UI testing actions"), so the words say a locked phone can be
neither read nor driven (the owner's own notes said it could be read). `devicectl list devices` lists it as
`tunnelState: disconnected`, `transportType: localNetwork` while it is in reach: only `unavailable` counts as out of
reach.

The pre-push review of the link found that the wait after a relaunch counted only its pauses, so an address that
drops packets (each look then takes its whole four seconds) stretched one round of the keeper past five minutes: the
wait is measured by the desk's clock now. It also found that an address that answered `/status` but refused
`/wda/locked` (another device at the home network's address) kept the last state for good and told no one: the link
is `unclear` then, told to the owner as the others; and that the notice opened a page that said nothing of the phone:
Settings › Phone shows the link's state while `[phone]` is on, and the notice opens it.

The screen and step tools (`phone_snapshot`, `phone_screenshot`, `phone_act`) were built in the same branch and left
out after four rounds of the pre-push review, each of which found a way to act on the phone that the Mac's desktop
does not allow: a batch stopped midway that left the run without word of what went out, a tap on the keyboard's send
key that asked nothing, a tap or keys sent twice when the answer was lost, a carriage return that sends as a line
break does, a password manager's screen read, and Dot able to tap its own approval card in the phone's web UI. They
come as their own item, with every rule of the Mac's desktop (a secrets app never read, Dot's own app never touched,
each run its own read of the screen) and an independent security review.

Checked: `tests/unit/test_phone.py`, `tests/smoke/test_iphone.py` (a stand-in WebDriverAgent server and a stand-in
devicectl: `phone_status` through the registry and the loop, with only reads sent to the phone and the run left
untainted; an address that will not say whether the phone is locked, its words kept out; the keeper's relaunch, its
wait measured by the clock, its five-minute wait after a failed launch or listing, the owner told once per state; the
card of Settings › Phone, wide and on a phone).
