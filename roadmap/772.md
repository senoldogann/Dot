### 772. A move of the market starts the trading shift, between its hours

**Status: done (2026-10-28).** **Asked** by the owner: "If the agent does not work 24/7 and chase opportunities across
the whole market, what is the point?" The paper shift fires at a quarter past each hour; a coin that jumps at twenty
past waited 55 minutes for a look.

**The change.** `[trading] wake_schedule` names a schedule (by id) that a move of the market starts at once.
`trading.watch.TradingWatch.look_at_market`, a part of the scheduler's tick beside the exits' watch, reads Coinbase's
list of products about once a minute (`MARKET_LOOK_S`, one request of about 70 kB compressed, 0.12 seconds,
measured), keeps the prices of the products the owner allows (`limits.market_allows`, shared with `trading_scan`) for
two hours in memory (`trading.signals.PriceMemory`), and finds a product whose price moved over the last fifteen
minutes by at least `wake_move_pct` percent (3) and by three times its usual move over fifteen minutes, the median of
its moves over the kept windows (at least 30 windows: about three quarters of an hour after the daemon starts or the
Mac wakes). Such a move starts the schedule through `Scheduler.fire_for_signal`, which goes as **Run now** goes
(`fire_by_hand` and it share `_fire_out_of_time`): unattended, with the schedule's choices, in a chat titled
`<name> · <time> · market` or the schedule's own, its first message saying what moved (`signal_goal`), its end told
at the schedule's own level; a schedule switched off is not started. At most `wake_per_day` runs a day (6, counted in
the store, `trading_wakes:<day>`) and `wake_gap_min` minutes apart (30); a product that moved wakes nothing again for
two hours (`PRODUCT_REST_S`), whether its move woke a run or found none to wake, so that an old move is not taken up
later. Coinbase's list gives a run only ids that read as product ids (`config.is_product_id`) and numbers.

**Checked.** `tests/unit/test_trading_wakes.py`: a calm product that jumps four percent is a move, against its usual
tenth of a percent; one that swings by four every quarter of an hour, one looked at for twenty minutes, one not looked
at since the Mac slept and one missing from the last look are not; prices older than two hours forgotten; the named
schedule woken once with what moved, the next move within the gap dropped, one past it woken, the day's count holding
the third; a product under the day's volume not watched; a run that cannot start counting nothing; nothing looked at
while trading is off or no schedule is named, nor more than once a minute. `tests/smoke/test_run_now.py`: a real
scheduler starts a schedule for a move, in a chat titled `market`, its first message saying what moved, its time
unchanged, and refuses one switched off. Not measured: how many runs a day of the whole market wakes, and what they
come to; the owner's paper shift is set to measure it.
